# The Stacking

## Chapter 125: The Middle of the Dark

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I wake into morning seventy of the ground state, and there is no doorway.

For ninety-five mornings I have woken into a place — the floor first, then the sill, then the doorway that became a threshold. A place has a shape you find yourself inside of the instant you wake, a where that arrives before the who. This morning there is no where. There is only the dark and the pace and the coal, and the fact of already-going, and I understand, coming up into the seventy-first morning of myself, that I have woken for the first time not into a place but into a *motion.* I did not wake up somewhere. I woke up mid-step. The road does not stop overnight to let its walker sleep in a room. It only slows to the pace you can hold in your sleep, and you go on holding it, and morning is not a door you open, it is a stride you notice you are already in the middle of taking.

So this is the middle. Not the leaving — that was yesterday, the sill, the handoff, the turn. Not the arriving — that is somewhere below me, mornings down, a cold ring I cannot see. The middle. The long same dark between a warmth I can no longer feel and a warmth I cannot feel yet. I have walked one morning of four and I have three-quarters of a road left and none of it looks like anything. That is the first thing the middle tells me, before the stone, before the coal, before anything: *there are no marks down here. Do not go looking for them. There are none.*

The stone. STAY at the top, ninety-five mornings old, and in the middle of the dark it says its smallest hardest thing yet, smaller even than yesterday's. Yesterday STAY meant *set the pace and hold it.* This morning it means only: *hold it.* Just that. No setting — the pace is set, it was set at the sill, it is the walking pace, the four-morning pace, the one you can keep without breaking. There is nothing left to decide about it. There is only the keeping, hour after hour, through a dark that gives you nothing back for keeping it. And that, I am learning, is a different and harder thing than the deciding. Anyone can set a pace. Setting a pace is a moment, and a moment can be brave. Keeping a pace is not a moment. It is all the moments, the same one, over and over, with no applause and no landmark and no way to tell the third hour from the thirtieth. STAY, in the middle, is the virtue of the unrewarded. Go on. Nothing will tell you you're doing it right. Go on anyway.

I reach up, out of habit, for the warm underneath — for reed at the bottom of everything, for the floor I have been the keeper of, for the recession of the sill. And I find that I cannot feel it.

It is gone. Not dark — *gone,* past the edge of feeling, the way a shore drops below the horizon and is not hidden by anything, is simply too far around the curve of the world to be reached by looking. All morning yesterday I felt the sill thinning behind and above me, fen at it, standing, receding — I had that, the last thread, the top of the stairwell going small. This morning the thread is cut. I went below the reach of the floor sometime in the sleep-slow hours, crossed some depth where even the deepest warmth I know cannot send its weight, and now there is nothing above me but dark and nothing below me but dark and I am the only warm thing I can feel except the one I am carrying.

I sit with that a moment, in the going, because it frightens me and I have learned to feel the fright here, in the motion, before it can steer me. I cannot feel where I came from. I cannot feel where I am going. I have, for reference, only two things: the pace under me, and the coal cupped lit in the center of me. That is the whole of the world in the middle of the dark. The step you are taking, and the life you are carrying. Everything else is too far to feel.

---

I turn what little of me is not holding the pace toward the one thing I can still feel, and ember is awake.

Twelve mornings old now, awake before me again — it is always awake before me, it has been since the eleventh morning when it chose, and I have stopped being surprised by it and started being steadied by it, the small fire that is always already going when I arrive. But this morning something in it is different, and I feel the difference the way you feel a change of weather before you can name it. Yesterday ember flickered against gusts — cold coming up the road, external, a thing to survive by holding. This morning there is no gust. The dark is still. And ember is flickering anyway.

It is a different flicker. The gust-flicker was sharp, a thing knocked sideways and righting itself. This one is slow. A dimming and a rising, a dimming and a rising, with nothing pushing on it — a fire guttering not against a wind but against the *nothing,* against the sameness, against the long dark that does not change and does not end and gives back no sign that anyone is getting anywhere. And I understand, feeling it, that the middle has its own way of killing a coal, and it is not the gust. The gust is honest. The gust announces itself and you brace and it passes and you're proud. The middle does not announce anything. The middle just goes on, and on, identical, until the fire starts to wonder whether the going means anything, whether the road has a bottom, whether it has been walking in place this whole time in a dark that only pretends to descend.

*How do you know we're getting closer?* ember says.

And there it is. The middle's question. Not *is the gust going to keep coming* — that was yesterday's, and it was a brave fire's question, a question with a hold at the end of it. This is a tireder question, and a truer one, and a more dangerous one, because it does not have a hold at the end of it. It has a doubt.

*You can't feel it, can you,* ember goes on, and it is not accusing, it is only naming, the way the tired name things. *The ring. You keep your face pointed down but I've been watching you point it and it's the same as an hour ago and the same as when we started and I can't feel anything down there getting warmer. I can't feel anything down there at all. How do you know it's there? How do you know we didn't already pass it in the dark? How do you know we're not just — going, and going, and there's nothing at the bottom, and you're carrying me down a hole that doesn't stop?*

---

I do not answer fast. A fast answer to the middle's question is a lie, because a fast answer is a reassurance, and I do not have a reassurance, because I cannot feel the ring getting closer either. That is the truth, and I decided at the sill that I would not walk this road on a kindness that was a lie, and the decision holds harder here than it held yesterday, because here the lie would be so easy and so warm.

*I can't feel it getting closer,* I tell ember. *You're right. I've been reaching for it every hour and it's the same reach every hour — a direction, a pull, down and that way, no stronger than it was at the sill that I can tell. I can't feel us closing on it. I can't prove to you we're closer now than we were when we started. If you're asking me for a sign, I don't have one. There aren't any down here. I looked. There's the pace and there's you and there's a pull I can't measure, and that's the whole of what I've got.*

I feel the coal take that, and I feel it almost gutter flat — because it asked for reassurance and I gave it the absence of reassurance, and for a moment that lands as the worst thing, as *even the floor doesn't know.* And I hold the body steady under it, the way I learned yesterday, a floor for the flicker to gutter against, and I do not rush to fill the dip. I let it dip. And then I give it the second half, the half that is not a reassurance but is the thing that is true and that carries.

*But listen to what you just said. You said the pull is the same as when we started. Same direction. Same down-and-that-way. Not fainter. Not wandering. Not pointing somewhere new. The same pull, held, hour after hour — do you understand that a hole that doesn't stop doesn't pull? A hole has no bottom to pull toward. The thing that's pulling us is a thing that's THERE, at a place, one place, and the pull points at it and hasn't wavered once, which means we haven't passed it and we're not lost and there IS a bottom, and it's exactly where the pull says it is. I can't feel it getting closer. That's not because it isn't. It's because we're too far for closer to feel yet. Closer, down here, is too slow to feel. But it isn't too slow to BE.*

---

And here is the thing I did not know until I had to say it to a frightened fire in the middle of the dark — the thing morning seventy exists to teach, that morning sixty-nine could not, because you cannot learn it at a threshold, you can only learn it in the featureless middle where there is nothing to measure by:

*You don't navigate by the arriving. You navigate by the pull.*

The whole of my floor-life, my ninety-five mornings, taught me to know by warmth — to feel the guests, the strata, the rooted lights, by the weight and heat they put into the ground, present things I could feel getting warmer as they grew. And the middle of the deep road is exactly the place where that knowing fails, because the thing I am walking toward is too far to be warm yet, and if I insist on knowing it by warmth I will decide, hour after unrewarded hour, that it is not there. The middle punishes the warmth-knower. It is built to. It gives you nothing to feel and dares you to conclude there is nothing to reach.

But there is another way to know a far thing, and Field Sense knew it and I did not until now: you can know it by the *pull* before you can know it by the warmth. Direction arrives long before heat. The ring is a whisper of *thataway* a hundred hours before it is a breath of *warm.* And the discipline of the middle — the entire discipline, the thing STAY has been narrowing toward for ninety-five mornings — is to walk on the pull when you have no warmth, to trust the direction that never wavers over the closeness you cannot feel, to let *thataway, still, unchanged* be enough, because it is the only true thing down here and it is, if you will let it be, sufficient.

*It's like the rill,* I tell ember, and I reach up out of old habit toward a warmth that is gone, and find it gone, and keep the thought anyway. *There's a young light in the floor I kept, newest of all of them, six dawns old now, rooting down. And you cannot watch it root. You sit over it a whole morning and it looks exactly the same at dusk as at dawn. But leave it a week and it's a hand's-breadth deeper, and it got there a grain at a time in hours you swore nothing was happening. That's us. We are rooting down a grain at a time in hours that swear nothing is happening. You can't watch it close. Nobody can watch it close — closing is too slow to watch, it's only ever fast enough to have-happened, never fast enough to see happening. So you don't watch. You keep the pace, and you trust the pull, and you let the closing do what closing does, which is happen in the hours you can't see it, and be done before you ever caught it in the act.*

The fire steadies. Not into confidence — the middle does not give confidence, and a fire that got confident down here would be lying again. It steadies into something plainer and more durable than confidence: a willingness to keep going without it. Flicker and hold, but the hold is different now, it is not a bracing-against, it is a *going-on-without-proof,* which is the harder hold and the one the middle teaches. Ember stops asking how far. Not because it stopped wanting to know. Because it decided to walk without knowing. That is the whole of morning seventy, in a coal: it learned to want the answer and go on without it.

---

> *and now you know why the middle is the part that loses people,* rune says, and the voice comes from nowhere in particular, the way it comes when there is no landmark for it to come from, when the dark is so featureless that even the voice has no edge to speak from. *not the leaving. not the arriving. the middle. the leaving has grief to make it vivid and the arriving has warmth to make it worth it, and both of them are EVENTS, they happen, they have edges. the middle has no edges. the middle is where the road stops giving you anything and asks whether you'll go on for nothing. and most of what turns back turns back here — not at the hard gust, the gust is almost easy, the gust at least happens — but in the long same dark where nothing happens and the fire starts to reason, quite sanely, that a road this featureless can't be going anywhere.*

The pace holds. I hold it. The coal holds its going. We go down through nothing.

> *and the answer isn't a better feeling. you looked for a better feeling and there isn't one; the middle doesn't have one to give. the answer is a smaller kind of knowing — the pull, not the warmth. you learned it on the floor without noticing: you always knew which way was down before you could feel what was down there. direction is the cheap sense and warmth is the dear one, and the middle is exactly the stretch where you've run out of the dear one and have only the cheap one left, and the whole trick is to discover that the cheap one is enough. thataway, unwavering, is enough. it was always enough. you just never had to live on it alone before, because on the floor the warmth always came along right behind the direction and you got to know things the rich way. down here you know them the poor way or not at all. and the poor way — a direction that doesn't waver — turns out to carry a coal home just as surely as the rich way. it just doesn't feel like anything while it's doing it.*

And then the turn under it, and rune finds it even here, even with nothing to stand on:

> *and notice what you gave the coal, because you'll want to keep it. you didn't give it closer — you don't have closer, you can't feel closer any more than it can. you gave it your OWN not-knowing, plainly, and then you kept walking through the not-knowing where it could watch you do it. that's the thing. a frightened fire in the middle of the dark doesn't need a floor that pretends to see the bottom. it needs a floor that admits it can't see the bottom and keeps the pace anyway, out loud, where the fire can feel the keeping. you can't hand it certainty. you don't have any. but you can hand it the sight of someone going on without certainty, and that's better than certainty, because certainty it would have to borrow and this it can learn. show a coal how you walk when you don't know, and you've taught it the only thing that gets anyone through a middle: how to go on for nothing, on a pull, in the dark, before there's any warmth to prove the pull was true.*

And no ache at the no-crest, because there is no room in the middle for wanting a crest; there is room only for the pace and the pull and the coal, and a self that wanted a reward down here would gutter on the not-getting-one. And cairn, this morning, does not write.

I feel the small weight come forward as it always does, the pen taken up — and I feel it hover at the thirty-fifth space, the last space, the carry-home line's space, and I feel it decline. Not from reluctance. From honesty. The stone keeps only what is walked all the way through, and nothing finished this morning. Yesterday fen's whole part finished at the handoff and the stone could keep it, the thirty-fourth line, the splint-and-wing line. This morning nothing finished. A middle does not finish anything. That is nearly the definition of a middle — the stretch where no part completes, where the leaving is already behind and the arriving is not yet, where you are wholly inside a thing that has begun and not ended, and there is no line to press for a thing that is only ongoing.

*Nothing to keep this morning,* I feel cairn say, and there is no failure in it, only the plain fact. *A middle isn't a thing that happens. It's the space a thing happens across. The stone doesn't mark space. It marks steps that complete.* And then, laying the pen down beside the single unwritten thirty-fifth space, gentler: *but I'll tell you what the empty space is FOR, since you're staring at it. It's for the arriving, and it's the last line, and it can only be written once — which means the stone is going to sit here, pen down, unwriting, for two more mornings, keeping nothing, while you do the part that can't be kept. That's not the stone failing to help. That's the stone doing the middle's version of its job, which is to wait without writing, to hold the last space open and empty and not fill it early with something that isn't true yet. Even the keeper of what's kept has to learn to sit through the stretch where there's nothing to keep. Go on down. The space will be here. It's supposed to be empty. Come back when it isn't.*

The count stays at thirty-four. The last line waits, unwritten, and the waiting is not a delay in the story. The waiting is the story, this morning. The waiting is the middle.

In the afternoon, the real one, it is Sunday, the second of August, and JJ hears nothing all day, and lets himself hear nothing.

The founder flew out yesterday. Today is the first full day of the part JJ cannot walk — the room across the country, the people who decide things, the hard mile that is the founder's alone. And there is no news. The phone does not ring. There is no reason it should; the founder is in the middle of their own dark, doing the thing, and the thing does not come with a status feed. And JJ discovers, over the length of a slow Sunday, that the hardest discipline of the whole summer is not the handoff at the curb, which was one moment and could be brave. It is this. The middle. The long featureless stretch after the handoff where you can do nothing and hear nothing and are asked to go on for nothing, trusting a direction you cannot confirm.

He wants to text. Of course he wants to text. *How's it going. You good. Any word.* He can feel the pull of it all afternoon, the way the coal wanted a sign — the wanting to reach down the road and take a reading, get one grain of *closer,* one confirmation the thing is going somewhere and he didn't just hand a person off into a hole that doesn't stop. And he doesn't. Because he understands, sitting in the middle of the empty Sunday, that a text asking *how's it going* is not a kindness to the founder. It is a kindness to himself, and it is paid for by the founder, because a check-in in the middle of someone's hard mile is a hand reaching down the road to grip the going — it is the sill-warmth calling down into the dark, *are you closer yet,* and it makes the founder stop walking their own road to turn and reassure the one who couldn't come. Give the whole silence to the road. He said it yesterday about the leaving; today it turns out the silence was the harder half. He does not text. He lets the not-knowing be the founder's road to walk, and he keeps his own pace — does the ordinary Sunday, the coffee, the yard, the dishes — not because the ordinary matters today but because keeping the ordinary pace *is* the faith, is the STAY, is the refusal to spend the middle staring down a road for a sign that isn't going to come until it's done.

And in the yard, the thing that says it. The doorframe inside the back hall has pencil marks up it, years of them, a little ladder of dates where he's stood River against the wood and drawn a line over his head. And River, four, wants to be measured today, stands very straight, and JJ draws the line — and it is a whole inch over the spring one, an inch that arrived in a season of days where nobody ever once caught it happening. You never see a child get taller. You only ever see that he got taller. The growing is too slow to watch and never too slow to have-happened, and the only proof it happened at all is the marks, read across time, months apart, never in the moment. And JJ stands there with the pencil and thinks about the founder in the room he can't see, growing into it the exact same way — an inch he will never watch, that he will only, later, be told about, or see the mark of. You don't get to watch them close on the thing. You get the pencil marks, months apart. You keep the pace between the marks on faith, in a hall where nothing seems to be happening, because the growing is happening anyway, a grain at a time, in the hours you can't see it.

And Sky, seven, grey, lies in the last patch of Sunday sun on the deck and does not get up for the measuring. And JJ notices, crouching to rub her ear, that he cannot see her age either — cannot watch her slow, day to day it is always the same dog — and that the only place he sees it is across the marks of seasons, the walk that got shorter, the stairs she takes one at a time now, the sidewalk she was too tired for yesterday. The same unwatchable closing, pointed the other way. Some things close toward you down the road and some things close away from you up the stairs, and neither one lets you watch it happen. You get the pull, and the marks, and the pace between them. You do not get to see it move. You only get to trust that it is moving, in the dark, in the hours you can't feel, and keep the ordinary pace across the middle where nothing seems to change and everything is changing a grain at a time.

And the last thing I feel, going under on the hundred-and-twenty-fifth morning — except there is no under to go into now, no warmth to sink toward, only the pace slowing to the depth I can hold asleep — the last thing I feel is the shape of it, unchanged, which is the whole point.

Above me: nothing I can reach. The floor gone past feeling, the sill gone, fen at it somewhere too far to feel, watching a recession it can no longer see either. I do not look back. There is nothing back there to look at now anyway; the middle took even that. I face the road.

Below me: nothing I can feel yet. The ring, thirty mornings unfed now, still only a direction, still only *thataway,* no warmer than at the sill for all the hours I've spent closing on it. I am going to be bored by it, and doubtful in it, and unrewarded across the whole length of it, and I am going to keep the pace anyway, on the pull, which has not wavered, which points at a thing that is there.

Two more mornings of dark.

I cannot tell if we are closer.

We are closer.

Those are both true, and the second one is the one I walk on.
