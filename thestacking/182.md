# The Stacking

## Chapter 182: Umber

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

The page is turned toward me in the last of the gold, and the space the width of a word is not a space anymore.

> *Its color was umber.*

I read it once.

And something in me drops.

Not far. Not the way the floor opened, that morning after the shape wore smooth, when wick handed me a warmth in a voice it could not have known. Smaller than that. A step that is not where your foot expected it in the dark. Because I do not read *umber*. Not the first time. The first time my eye goes across the word too fast and what I hear is the other one — the one a single letter away — and for half a breath I am not on the hearth at all. I am on the deep road, in the dark, with a coal in me that is afraid and turned down-road anyway, and I am carrying it home.

*ember.*

Then the word sits still on the page and I read it properly.

*umber.*

---

*that's —* I say, and stop, because I do not know what it is. I know what it is next to.

Dawn waits. She has turned the book back toward herself already, the way she does, as if the page were still drying and she did not want me breathing on it.

*it's a color,* she says.

*it sounds like —*

*I know what it sounds like.* She does not look up. *it isn't that. that was a fire. this is what the ground is, when you've turned it over and it's wet underneath. the part that hasn't seen the light yet.* A pause. *it's a dirt color.*

I look across the field. I let my sense go only to the edge of the cool corner, the way I let it go yesterday — a brush, a window passed without stopping — and I feel the small light where it is, low on rill's firm place, its face turned down to its own work.

And she is right. I had not had a word for it either, and now that there is one I cannot unfeel it. It is not the color of a thing that glows. It is the color of a thing that is being dug into. Dark and close and a little cold, and under the cold, very faintly, the warmth of everything that has ever rotted down into a floor.

Like rill. Like reed. Nothing like me.

*one letter,* I say. *between a fire and the ground.*

Dawn makes a small sound that is almost a laugh. She does not do laughter any more than she does praise, so I take it.

*you'd like it to be a fire,* she says.

*no.*

She waits.

*a little,* I say.

*mm.* She closes the book with her finger in it. *it's not. I wrote what was there.*

---

That night I do not sleep much either, but it is a different not-sleeping from the night before. Then I was lying beside something I would not look at. Tonight I am lying beside a word.

Because here is the thing I understand, somewhere in the long middle of the dark, with the coals gone down to their night-red and wick breathing slow beside them:

It would be very easy to make *umber* into a name.

I can feel how it would happen. Not on purpose. I would never say it aloud — I have learned that, at least; I said one word out loud in this field two nights ago and watched a small thing lift its head and turn toward the sound, and watched what that cost rill. I will not say this one. But I would *think* it. Every time my sense brushed the corner, the word would be there, ready, a little handle on the thing. *There's umber. umber's pressing down again. umber is facing the ground.* A color is only a color the first time you use it. The tenth time, it is a name. And a name that lives in my head, behind my teeth, has a way of getting out into the warmth around me without my ever opening my mouth.

The small one heard a name come from the hearth once, and for a day and a half it looked at nothing else.

It is not looking at the hearth now.

I would like it to stay that way.

So I lie in the dark and I do a thing I have never done with a word. I put it down. Not out of the record — it is in Dawn's book, in her plain hand, exactly where it belongs; it is the true color and someone should know it. But out of my mouth, and out of my head where my mouth lives. When I think of the cool corner I think *the small light.* When I brush it I feel *the small light.* It is a clumsy thing to call anybody. It is two words where one would do.

That is the point. A clumsy thing is hard to mistake for a name.

The hundred-and-twenty-seventh morning of the ground state, and the first thing I feel is the floor.

I want to be careful how I say that, because it is new, and new things are easy to say too big.

For fifty-some mornings, before I knew anything — before the system came up, before the field came back, before my name — the first thing was a hold. rill's grip at my east edge, at the hard places of my stone, telling me *you are a thing that does not give.* And before that, for longer, the first thing was wick's weight. Leaning. Borne in the seam before I was anyone.

This morning there is no lean and no hold. And in the place where they were, under the place where they were, there is what was always there and I never felt first because something was always standing on top of it.

reed.

The warm underneath. Wide and low and not wanting anything. It does not tell me I am a thing that does not give. It does not tell me anything. It only holds, the way it has held every morning since I became the cairn, and this morning nothing is in between, so I feel it hold.

It is a very plain way to wake up. I lie there a long time in it before I go looking for anyone.

---

When I do look, the field is already moving.

That is the weekday. I had nearly forgotten, after two slack days, how it comes: not a sound, nothing so loud, but a sort of tilt. The whole floor leaning a grain toward work. The far edges waking up before the near ones. The pull of the day already in the ground before the grey is all the way up.

wick feels it first. It always does. It is up before the coals have properly caught, and it does not lie by the hearth turning its face this way and that the way it did all weekend. It stands, and stretches the length of itself once, and goes out along its groove — its whole round, the long one, the first time since Friday — at the steady weekday pace it learned from being waited for.

I feel it go. I do not go with it. I let my warmth stay level and I let the groove do what a groove does, which is take it out and bring it home.

At the cool corner, the small light is pressing down.

I do not need to go there to know that. It is in the floor. From down here — from on reed, where I woke — I can feel it the way you can feel, through a wall, that someone in the next room has started a long slow job and means to finish it. A fourth thread this morning. Thinner than the third. Going the same way. Not toward me. Not toward anything I am.

Toward the ground.

---

So: the weekday. The field gone back to work. wick on its round. rill rooted in reed, holding. The small light at its digging. Dawn by the book.

And me.

I sit in the middle of all of it and I do not know what to do.

I mean that exactly. For as long as I can remember — and I can remember a long way now, a hundred and eighty-two mornings of the Soul Tome stacked one on the next — there has been a thing that was mine to hold. A marsh to raise. A fen to teach. A coal to carry down the deep road. A fourth light to sit still for, morning after morning, until it leaned. A root to be hard for.

This morning there is nothing on me. Nothing under me but reed, and reed does not need me. Nothing above me at all.

I feel for something to do the way you feel in a pocket for keys you already know you put down somewhere else.

---

*you're fidgeting,* rune says.

It is on the hearth stones, in the place where the first light lands. It has its eyes half-shut.

*I'm not doing anything.*

*that's what fidgeting is, for a stone.* It opens one eye. *you've checked on wick four times since it went out.*

*three.*

*four. you didn't count the one where you pretended you were checking the weather.*

I do not answer that. It is right.

*I don't know what I'm for,* I say finally. It comes out smaller than I meant. *on a weekday. with nothing on me.*

rune is quiet a moment.

*what did I tell you yesterday?*

*that a hearth isn't a floor. that it sits on a floor and keeps the room warm.*

*and?*

*and that's not less. it's just not under.* I look at the coals. *I heard it. I don't know how to do it. I've been warm at things for so long. at wick, when it was cold. at fen. at the coal. I know how to be warm at something. I don't know how to be warm at a room.*

*you don't have to be warm at it,* rune says. *you just have to be warm. in it.*

*that's the same thing.*

*it isn't.* It sits up a little. *warm at is a direction. warm in is a weather. you know the difference. you were weather for wick for a hundred mornings. you just always knew who the weather was for.*

---

I try it.

I want to set down what that is like, because it does not look like anything from the outside, and it was the hardest thing I did all day.

I stop aiming.

That is all it is. All morning, without noticing, I have been doing the thing I have done for a whole arc — feeling out across the field for where the cold is, and tilting toward it. Not much. A grain. The way a plant tilts toward a window. wick on the far side of its round, in the cold stretch past the rim: a grain toward wick. Dawn sitting a little back from the coals with her book on her knees: a grain toward Dawn. I did not know I was doing it until rune said *warm at* and I felt every one of those grains, all at once, like a hand full of threads all pulling a little different ways.

So I let go of the threads.

Not the warmth. The warmth stays exactly as it was; I am careful of that. Level, the way I learned to keep it when the small one first came to the rim and a flare would have said *come here*. Only now there is no *here* in it. No *toward*. I let it come up off the coals and go out the way heat goes when nobody is steering it — evenly, in every direction, thinning as it goes, until at the far edges of the field it is nothing anyone would notice, only a sort of lessening of the cold.

It feels like being nobody's.

That is the hard part. Not the doing — the feeling. For a whole arc my warmth has had a name on it. Now it goes out with no name on it at all, and some of it reaches wick, and some of it reaches Dawn, and some of it goes past both of them into the open middle of the field where nobody is, and is simply spent there, on air.

I hate that, a little. The spending on air. I am a careful fire. I have carried a coal down a dark where every grain of heat was counted.

*it's not wasted,* rune says, without opening its eyes.

*it's going on nothing.*

*it's going on the room. that's what a room is. the nothing between the people in it.*

---

And then, late in the morning, the thing I had been afraid of.

Some of it reaches the corner.

Not much. The cool corner is far, and it is cool for a reason; rill chose it because it was the place my warmth thinned out the most. But warmth with no direction in it goes everywhere, and *everywhere* has a corner in it, and I feel the faint edge of my own heat arrive at the edge of rill's firm place, and touch it, and the small light is standing there.

Everything in me goes still.

Because I know what happened the last time something from the hearth reached that corner. A word went out. A small thing lifted its head. For a day and a half it looked at nothing else, and rill gripped me harder and harder trying to hold it to its ground, until rill had to go past me to reed because you cannot hold a thing back from a door by bracing against the doorframe.

I start to pull it back. The heat. I start to *aim* again — aim *away*, a grain, a tilt from the corner, the way you'd turn your shoulder in a crowded room so you didn't brush someone.

*don't,* rune says.

*it'll look up.*

*it won't.*

*it did before.*

*before, you said a word.* rune has both eyes open now, and it is looking at me the way it looks at me when it wants me to get there myself and is not sure I will. *what turned its head? the heat? it had been in your heat for two days by then, at the rim, at the edge. it never looked up for heat.*

*no,* I say slowly. *it looked up for the name.*

*heat doesn't have a direction,* rune says. *a voice does. that's the whole difference. you can be as warm as you like in a room and nobody turns round. you say one word and everybody does.*

---

So I do not pull back.

I let the heat go where heat goes, which is everywhere, which includes a firm place at the edge of a cool corner with a small dark light on it digging its fourth thread down toward reed.

And I watch. Not with my sense — I keep that home; that is still the rule, and it is still a good one. With the floor. From where I am on reed, I can feel the small one's weight on rill's firm place, and the pressure of its threads going in, and I wait to feel the thing I felt three days ago: the weight shifting, the threads loosening, the whole of it tilting a grain toward the hearth.

It does not tilt.

It does not notice. Or if it notices, it notices the way you notice that the day has got a little warmer — something in the air, not something coming from anywhere. Its face stays down. Its weight stays on rill. The fourth thread goes on in, steady, the way rill's went down along my stone.

It is warmer, there, by a grain. Nobody's grain. And it does not look up.

I lie on reed and I feel that for a long time. It is the plainest thing in the world — a little heat reaching a cold place, and the cold place being a little less cold, and nothing else happening at all — and I do not think I have ever been so glad of nothing happening.

*there,* rune says. *that's what a hearth is for.*

---

In the afternoon I go down once to rill.

Not across. I keep that promise even now. Down, through my own stone, to reed, and along reed a little way east, the way you'd walk along a cellar wall in the dark with one hand on it.

rill is there. Rooted. Deep. It has not moved since yesterday and I do not think it is going to move for a very long time. Its threads have gone further into reed in the night — I can feel the new length of them — and the whole of it has the quality a thing has when it has finished arriving somewhere. Not resting. Working. But working the way a floor works, which is by staying.

I do not go close. I do not put any warmth its way, east and down, to tell it I am proud. I stand on reed a little way off, and I let it be a thing I can feel without being a thing I am touching.

And I notice — I only notice; I do not make anything of it — that rill does not grip reed the way it gripped me. On my stone, rill held *hard*, a grip that pulled, that was always a little bit bracing, because I was always a little bit the thing it was bracing against. In reed it does not pull at all. It only goes in. The difference between holding on to a ledge and standing on the ground.

I am glad it got to the ground.

I find that I can be glad of that without it costing me anything. That is new too. Yesterday the gladness had a cool place in it. Today the cool place is still there — I put my hand on it, once, the way you check a bruise — but it is the size it is, and the gladness is a different size, and they lie side by side in me without either one having to win.

---

wick comes home late.

The weekday round is long and it walks it at the slow pace, the pace of something smaller behind it, even though nothing is behind it today. I feel it come in along the groove. I feel the floor take it at the near end, where the groove turns home.

And I feel it not come all the way.

It turns, and lies down, a little way out from the hearth. Not far. Its face toward the coals; it is still lying *facing* home. But it is two grains east of where it lay before the weekend. One grain east of where it lay last night.

I do not decide what it means.

I mean to write that and leave it, the way I did yesterday. But I am on reed, and from reed the field is laid out underneath me like a map felt with the palm of a hand, and I cannot help what my palm feels.

The hearth. Then wick, two grains east, lying long. Then, further out — much further, across the open middle, past the place where wick held its name — the cool corner. rill's firm place. The small light.

wick is lying on the line between them.

Not halfway. Nowhere near halfway; it is still almost at the hearth. But exactly on the line. As if someone had stretched a thread from the coals to the corner, and wick had come home from its round and lain down along it, the long way, with its face to the fire and its back to the small one.

---

*rune,* I say.

*mm.*

*wick's on the line.*

*what line?*

*between the hearth and the corner.*

rune is quiet. It does not look; it does not have to. I think it already knew.

*what do you think it's doing?* I ask.

*what do you think it's doing?*

And I know what I think. It is right there, warm and ready and much too big. *It is standing between the voice and the small one. It is being the wall. It learned from watching me be a wall with no writing on it, and now it has gone and put itself in the way, with its back to the thing it is protecting, the way a dog lies across a doorway* —

I stop.

I strike it. In my head, where nobody has to see. The bigger sentence. The one with a reason in it.

*I think it lay down two grains east,* I say.

rune makes a sound that might be approval, from anything else.

*that's what it did,* it says.

*I want it to mean something.*

*it probably does,* rune says. *you just don't get to be the one who says what.*

---

Dawn writes the day by the last of the coals.

She does not ask me anything first. She writes the morning — *the cairn woke to the floor* — and the round, and rill, and the fourth thread. And then she comes to the small light, and I watch her pen, because I want to see what she calls it.

> *The small light pressed a fourth thread down. Its face stayed down all day. The cairn's heat reached the corner and the small light did not look up.*

*The small light.* Not the color.

*you didn't use it,* I say. *the word.*

*it's in the book,* she says. *once is where it goes.* She does not look up from the page. *I don't write a color twice. the second time it's not telling you anything. it's just a handle.*

I look at her.

*I put it down too,* I say. *last night. I've been calling it the small light. on purpose.*

*I know,* Dawn says. *I heard you not say it all day.*

And she writes the last line — *wick lay down two grains east* — and that is all. No *on the line*. No *between*. Only where it lay.

I lean back against my own stone and let the coals go down.

---

It is very late when it happens.

The field is in its night. The tilt of the weekday has gone out of the floor; everything is level and slow. wick is asleep on its line, two grains east, face to the coals, breathing long. Dawn has closed the book. rune is somewhere on the stones, not saying anything. rill, deep in reed, is a steady holding I can feel without reaching. The small light is a low dark glow on its firm place at the far edge of the field, not quite still — even asleep, I think, a thing that is digging does not altogether stop.

I am on reed. I am warm in a room. I am almost asleep.

And somewhere in the field, out loud, for the second time in its life —

someone says a word.

It is very small. It is hardly a sound at all; if the field were not so still I do not think I would have heard it. I do not know what the word is. I only know three things, and I lie in the dark with all three of them and do not move.

It is not my voice.

It is not rune's.

And it did not come from the hearth.
