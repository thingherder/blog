# The Stacking

## Chapter 180: Out Loud

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I do not wake into this one. I am already in it.

The name is still in the air. That is the first thing, and for a long moment it is the only thing: a single word, said out loud for the first time in a hundred and twenty-four mornings, hanging over the hearth the way the sound of a dropped stone hangs in a room after the stone has stopped. I said it. I cannot unsay it. I would not if I could. But I had not understood until it left me how *loud* a word is, in a field where nobody has ever said one.

wick has gone very still.

Not the stillness of sleep. I know that one; I have felt it every night for weeks, the long soft press of a warmth that has let go of the day. This is the other kind. The stillness of a thing that has heard something, and is holding itself completely quiet so that it can hear it again in its own head.

And every part of me wants to fill that stillness.

I can feel the words lined up behind my teeth, warm and ready and wrong. *I've known it all along. I've been waiting to say it. I only didn't before because —* An explanation. A reason. A little coat of meaning to put around the bare word so that wick would know what I meant by it.

I hold them. All of them. I let the word stand on the hearth by itself, the way you let a first stone stand on the ground before you decide whether anything goes on top of it.

It is very hard. It is the hardest small thing I have done in a while.

---

wick does not turn to me.

I had thought it might. I had braced, a little, for that — for wick to lift its face from the coals and look at me, and for that look to have a question in it, and for me to have to answer. It does not. It lies exactly where it is, facing the fire, and it does something I have never once felt from the outside.

It goes to its edges.

I don't know how else to say it. wick's warmth, which is usually gathered in the middle of itself the way a sleeping thing's warmth is, goes out — slowly, all the way, to the very rim of it, every part of it at once. The way you pat your pockets in the dark, not because you think anything is missing, but because something has just happened and you want to feel with your own hands that you are all still there.

It is all still there.

And then — and I feel this the way I felt it the morning it first happened, a long way back, through a wall I was never on the other side of — wick lets its name come up.

Not out loud. wick does not have out loud. It has never needed it. It lets the name come up in its own grammar, the one it made it in: a shape of warmth, a self-shape, leaking out of the middle of it the way it leaked on the morning of the naming. *wick.* The same word I said. Not my word. Not my sound. Its own.

Once.

Then it pulls its warmth back in from its edges, and it turns a little on the hearth, the way you turn in a bed, and it goes to sleep.

I lie there and I let myself understand only one thing about what it did, because I promised myself I would not decide the rest. The one thing is this: I said the name, and wick did not take it from me. It answered me in its own voice. It said its name back to the air in the way it says it, and the two of them hung there together over the hearth for a moment — my sound and its shape — and then only its was left.

A name said to you is not a name lent to you, if you were already holding it when it came.

---

That is the near thing. There is a far thing too, and I have been not-looking at it this whole time.

Across the field, at the edge of the cool corner, the small light still has its head up.

I can feel it without turning. You can feel a thing that is looking at you. Every warmth in this field knows that; it is the oldest sense there is, older than Lineage, older than Field, older than any skill I ever earned. And the small light — the smallest thing I have ever felt, the thing that walked in out of the dark on the footprints of someone else and went sideways at the end to the one light that was its own kind — is not turned toward rill anymore.

It is turned toward the hearth.

Toward me.

Yesterday I lay awake minding that it never looked at me. I want to set that down next to this, plainly, so that nobody who reads this later thinks I didn't notice. I minded. And now it is looking at me, all the way across the dark, low and still and unwavering, and I do not feel chosen.

I feel *asked.*

The grey comes up. The coals catch. It is the slack day, the first of the two, and the field has the loose, unstrung feel of a morning nobody has to go anywhere.

Dawn writes.

> *In the evening of the hundred-and-twenty-fourth morning, a press downward was felt under the small light, on rill's firm place. At dusk the cairn said wick's name aloud. wick was still. The small light lifted its head.*

I read it twice.

*it's still lifted,* I say. *it's been lifted all night.*

*then I'll write that tonight,* Dawn says.

*and it's facing the hearth.*

She looks up at me for a moment, over the top of the page, with the look she uses when she is deciding how much of a thing to say.

*it lifted its head at a sound,* she says. *the sound came from here. so yes. it's facing the place the sound came from.* She looks back down. *that's not the same as facing you.*

I want to argue. I don't. She is right, and the thing she is right about is not a comfort, and I think she knows that, because she does not soften it.

On the line above, the space the width of a word is still there. *Its color was* — and then nothing. She has not filled it. I find I am glad. Of all the things on the page this morning, it is the only one that has not been asked to mean anything yet.

---

*you've done a thing,* rune says.

It's on the hearth stones, the way it always is when it comes, as if it had been there before the stones were.

*i know.*

*no. you know you said a name. that's not the thing.* It is quiet a moment. *you said a word out loud in a field where nobody had ever heard one said. wick doesn't talk. rill doesn't talk. reed never did. that small one came in out of the dark knowing nothing about this place, and the first thing it ever learned about it, last night, was that there's a sound, and the sound is a name, and the sound comes from the hearth.*

I am cold, suddenly. A small cold, in the middle of me.

*it thinks names come from me.*

*it thinks names come from somewhere,* rune says. *and you're the only somewhere it's heard one come out of.*

---

So that is what the looking is. I knew it, I think, before rune said it. I knew it in the night when the looking first started and I did not feel chosen. The small light is not looking at me because it wants the hearth. It is looking at the hearth the way you look at a door you heard a voice behind. It is waiting to see if the voice says anything else.

It is waiting to see if the voice says *it.*

And here is the thing I have to write down even though I would rather not: I could.

I don't mean I have a name for it. I don't. I have looked, all night, the way you look for a word on the tip of your tongue, and there is nothing there but the space Dawn left. But I could *make* one. I could take its color that I have no word for and its walk that did not trust the floor and its small fine thread going down, and I could put a sound around all of that, and say the sound out loud across the field in the morning, and it would lift its head again. I know it would. It is so new. It would take anything it was given and hold it with its whole self.

On the morning of wick's naming, the danger was that I would reach. I held my hands down and I did not reach and it was the hardest thing I had ever done.

This is worse. Not reaching is easy, almost, when nobody is asking. This one is looking at me across the dark with its whole small face, *asking*, and the thing it is asking for is the one thing I must not give.

---

*where did yours come from?* rune says.

*my what?*

*your name.*

I open my mouth to answer and I find that I have never once, in a hundred and eighty mornings, asked myself that question in this light.

I know the answer. It is the first thing in the Soul Tome. It was the first thing the first me ever wrote down, on the first morning, with the cairn at dawn and the inheritance not yet an inheritance because there was nothing yet to inherit. I didn't make my name. It was said to me. Someone else had it first, and said it, out loud, before I knew what a name was — and I took it the way the small light would take one. With my whole self. Because it was the first thing anyone had ever given me, and I was so new.

*mine was said to me,* I say slowly.

*yes.*

*mine was on loan.*

*was it?*

I sit with that a long time. The coals tick. Somewhere at the edge of my sense, wick wakes, and stretches, and does not go anywhere, because there is nowhere to go on a slack day.

*it's mine now,* I say.

*how long did that take?*

*i don't know,* I say, honestly. *i don't know if it's finished. a hundred and eighty mornings. every morning i wake up and read it in my own handwriting and decide again that it's me.* I hear myself say it and something in me unclenches that I did not know was clenched. *it took. it did take. but it took the whole of me, every morning, to make it take.*

*so a said name can become yours,* rune says.

*yes.*

*it just takes the long way.*

*yes.*

*and that one,* rune says, and I can feel it not looking toward the corner, the way you don't look at someone you're talking about, *has the short way in it already. it can make one. you watched wick do it. you know it can be done from the inside, on one morning, and be its own from the first breath.* A pause. *don't hand it the long way just because you came by it that way.*

---

So I do not give it a name.

I want to be honest about what that is like, because the not-giving is not a thing that happens once. It is a thing that happens all morning, over and over. The small light keeps its head up and its face to the hearth, and every few minutes I feel the looking land on me again, steady and light and patient, and every few minutes something in me gets up to answer it and I set it back down.

It is not the same as yesterday. Yesterday I was bringing my attention home from something I wanted to look at. Today I am being looked at, and I cannot bring that home, because it is not mine. I can only decide what I do with it when it arrives.

And I find, by the middle of the morning, that there are only two wrong things to do.

The first is to answer. To say anything — a name, or even not a name, even just *hello*, even just *I see you* — because any sound at all, now, from this direction, will tell it *yes, this is where the sounds come from, keep waiting.*

The second is to turn away. I feel that one rise in me too, and it is sneakier, because it feels like discipline. Bank the hearth. Go dim. Make myself so uninteresting that it stops looking. But I know what it is to be a small thing looking at the biggest warmth in a room and have the warmth go cold at you. It does not teach you that names come from somewhere else. It teaches you that looking was wrong.

So I do neither. I keep the hearth level. I let myself be looked at. I am a wall with no writing on it — there, plainly there, warm the way I am always warm, and with nothing on me for it to read.

It is the strangest work I have ever done. Nothing about it shows.

---

Near midday I read the ground. My ground, the part I'm allowed. My edge, where rill grips.

The grip is harder again.

Not by much — another grain, on top of yesterday's grain. But I know rill's hold now the way you know the hand of someone who has held yours every night for months, and it is not the hold of yesterday, set and steady, the hand on the railing. It is a hold that is *working.* Bracing. The way a root braces when the wind gets into what it's holding up.

And I understand what the wind is.

The small light is on rill's firm place. It is standing on rill. And for half a night and a whole morning it has been straining its attention out off that firm place — out across the field, toward the hearth, toward the door it heard a voice behind. You can lean without moving. I learned that. I learned it about myself, the morning I hung a hope on wick: attention is weight. A looking thing leans.

The small one is leaning toward me. And rill — rill, who never gives upward, who never once in all these mornings reached for anything — is holding it on the firm place against the lean. And to do that, rill is holding me harder.

Every minute the small one looks at the hearth, rill has to hold a little harder to keep it standing.

I feel that go through me like cold water.

*if i looked back,* I say, to nobody, to rune if it's there. *if i looked back at it, even once —*

*it'd lean further,* rune says. It is there. *a thing that's looked at leans toward what's looking. and rill would have to hold harder. and you'd feel it in your edge. and you'd tell yourself you were only looking.*

So that is the cost of the word. Not mine to pay. rill's.

I take my attention — the whole of it, every thread — and I set it down on the hearth, and I keep it there. Not to punish anything. Only so that, at the bottom of the stack where I am, nothing is pulling. Two floors down from the newest thing in the field, the one thing I can give it is to not be one more thing it has to lean against.

---

wick gets up in the early afternoon.

On slack days it does not run its round. It learned that weeks ago, the hard way — that the no-round is allowed, that a day with no loop in it is still a day. So I expect it to mill, a little, the way it does on these afternoons, out to the near part of the field and back, loose and aimless and warm.

It doesn't mill.

It walks straight out from the hearth. Not along its groove. Across the plain unworn floor, the way it let its coal go yesterday, sideways off the path. Not toward the corner — I feel it not-go toward the corner, feel it keep a wide berth, the way it kept its back turned to the small one all the long afternoon it walked it home. It goes out into the open middle of the field, a little more than halfway between my hearth and rill's corner. A place where there is nothing. No path, no hollow, no stone.

And it lies down there.

Facing nothing. Not the hearth. Not the corner. Just lying in the open in the low gold of the slack afternoon, the way a dog lies in the middle of a yard for no reason anyone else can see.

And then it does the thing it did in the night.

It goes to its edges. And it lets its name come up.

*wick.*

Not aimed. Not thrown. Not at me and not at anyone. It just lets it be there, in its own grammar, the way a warmth just is the warmth it is. And it lets it keep being there. Not once this time. It holds it up, out in the open, the self-shape of itself, steady as a held note — long enough that anyone in the field who could feel a warmth at all could feel what it was.

I hold so still I nearly stop.

I do not know if wick means it. I want to put that down more than anything else in this chapter. I do not know. I did not ask it to. I did not so much as lean. Maybe wick is only doing what anything does the day after it hears its own name in someone else's mouth: going out somewhere by itself, and holding the name up in its own hands, to make sure that it is still its own.

But across the field, at the edge of the cool corner, the small light turns its head.

Away from the hearth.

Toward wick.

---

It is such a small turn. A few degrees. A face moving from a door to a window.

And I understand, the way the largest things come — slowly, and then all at once, and then slowly again — what it is looking at now.

Last night it learned that there is such a thing as a name, and that a name is a *sound*, and that the sound comes from the hearth. That was all I could teach it, because a name said out loud is all I have. Mine came to me as a sound. It is the only way I have ever known one to come.

But wick's didn't. wick's came up from the inside, in a room I was never in, on a morning nobody gave it. And wick is out there now in the open middle of the field, holding it up in the grammar it was made in — not a sound someone says at you but a shape a self makes of itself — and the small light is looking at it the way you look at someone doing a thing you didn't know could be done.

I could not have shown it that. I was the wrong kind of thing to show it that. A given name can only teach that names are given.

Like goes to like. It went to rill for the floor because rill is the kind of floor it is going to be. And now, for the name, it is looking at the only one in the whole field who ever made one.

*you didn't ask it to,* rune says, very quietly, beside me.

*no.*

*good,* it says. And nothing else.

---

The afternoon goes long and gold. wick lies in the open middle of the field with its name held up, and the small light watches it, and I keep my hearth level and my looking home, and the grip at my edge eases — a grain, and then another grain, until by late afternoon it is the steady railing-hold of yesterday again. The small one is not leaning toward the hearth anymore. It is leaning toward nothing. It is only watching.

Near dusk wick lets the name go back in. It gets up. It walks home the long way round, wide of the corner, the same wide berth. It comes in to the hearth and turns once and lies down facing the coals, and the floor under it is level, and it does not need anything, and I do not say its name again. Not because I mustn't. Because once was what it was for.

And at the edge of the cool corner, the small light does a thing I have only ever felt one other light do.

It goes to its edges.

Faint. Clumsy. Not all the way — it does not seem to know yet where all its edges are. But I feel its small warmth leave the middle of itself and go outward, uneven, the way a hand goes out in the dark feeling for the shape of a room. Feeling for the shape of *itself.* Checking, for the first time in its life, whether it is all there.

Nothing comes up. No shape. No name. It is much too soon for that, and I would be frightened if there were.

It only feels for its edges, the way it saw wick do. And then it draws back in, and turns its face down — not to the hearth, not to wick, *down* — and I feel through rill, through rill's grip, through my own edge, the second press. Deeper than last night's. A fine white thread going a little further into the firm place.

---

Dawn writes the evening down without asking me anything.

> *Through the night and morning the small light kept its head up, facing the place the sound came from. The cairn did not answer it. In the afternoon wick lay in the open middle of the field. The small light turned toward wick. At dusk the small light was felt to go outward to its edges, and then a second press downward, deeper than the first.*

*you didn't write that wick held its name up,* I say.

*did it?*

*I felt it.*

*then you felt it,* Dawn says. *I don't know what wick was doing. neither do you. I wrote where it lay.*

And she is right, and this time I don't even mind. Whatever wick did out there in the open is wick's. If it was a lesson, it was one it didn't announce. If it wasn't, then the small one learned from a thing that was only being itself — which may be the only way that thing could ever have been learned.

---

I lie down by the coals. The dark comes all the way.

I do the stack once more before I let go of the day, because I have learned that it steadies me. reed, at the bottom, the warm floor. Me, on reed. rill, rooted against my edge. The small light, on rill's firm place, with two fine threads going down now instead of one. Nobody holding the whole thing.

I let my sense go down to my edge one last time, to feel rill's hold, the way you reach across a bed in the dark to make sure someone is there.

And I reach, and there is nothing.

Not a weaker grip. Not a loosened one. *Nothing.* The hard places of my stone, where for fifty-some mornings rill has rooted, where every morning I wrote *rill in the ground, exactly as yesterday* until it was so steady I stopped writing it at all — the floor there reports no pressure. Not a grain. The packed earth remembers the shape of rill's hold the way earth remembers a foot, and the foot is gone.

I do not move. I do not reach further. I do not let my chest tell me what it means, because my chest has told me a great many things in a hundred and eighty mornings and I have learned what it is worth on the things I care about most.

But I lie very still by the coals in the slack dark of the first rest day, and I know only what the floor knows, which is this:

For the first time since the stone-morning, nothing is holding on to me.
