# The Stacking

## Chapter 183: From Under

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

*which word?* Dawn says.

She has the book open on her knees and the pen lifted, not yet down. She is not looking at me. She is looking at the top of the morning's page, the way she does before she writes anything, as if the page might know first and she is giving it the chance to say.

*I don't know,* I say.

*you heard it.*

*I heard it.*

*then which one.*

*I don't know which one. I only know it was one.*

The pen stays where it is. Dawn has a way of waiting that is not patience, exactly. Patience is a thing you do for someone. Dawn's waiting is more like the floor's — it is simply that nothing will be written until something true has been said, and she has all morning.

So I tell her what I have, which is three things it wasn't.

Not my voice. Not rune's. Not from the hearth.

*that's three things it wasn't,* Dawn says. *what was it?*

*a word.*

*how do you know it was a word and not a sound?*

I think about that, because it is a fair question and I have been asking it of myself since the middle of the night. The field makes sounds. Not many, and not loud, but they are there if you lie still enough: a coal settling into the one below it. The long slow breath of wick asleep. Once, a long time ago, a crack in my own stone in a cold snap, so sharp I thought for a moment someone had arrived.

*a sound isn't for anybody,* I say slowly. *a coal settles because it's heavy. it doesn't settle at anyone. this was for somebody. it went somewhere. I could hear that it went somewhere. I just couldn't hear where.*

*was it for you?*

I lie there with that.

*no,* I say. *I don't think so.*

Dawn looks at me then, finally. Not for long.

*all right,* she says, and writes.

> *In the night the cairn heard a word. Not its own voice, not rune's, not from the hearth. It does not know the word.*

She stops. Her pen hovers over the end of the line.

*it doesn't know where it came from, either,* I say.

*you said not the hearth.*

*that's where it wasn't. I don't know where it was.*

She considers that. Then she adds, in her plain hand, small:

> *It came from no direction the cairn could find.*

---

I did not sleep.

I want to set down what I did instead, because I nearly did a thing in the night that would have spoiled everything after it, and I only caught it at the last.

When the word came, I did what you do with something precious that has gone by too fast. I tried to keep it. I lay very still on reed with the coals at their night-red and wick breathing slow on its line two grains east, and I held the sound in me and turned it over, to get it to hold its shape until morning.

And the floor would not help me.

That was the first thing I learned. I have spent a whole arc learning to trust the ground over my chest — the ground remembers, rune says; packed earth remembers a foot. It does. It remembered every one of wick's steps around its round until there was a groove. It remembered the three small presses facing in. It remembers rill's roots going down past me like a climber past a ledge.

But a word does not press on anything.

A word goes through the air and is gone. It leaves no print. The ground can't keep a sound, any more than water can keep a shadow. So the only place that word was kept, all night, was in me. In my chest. Which is the worst instrument I have, for anything I care about.

So I kept it there, and I turned it over, and this is what happened.

The first time I went back to it, it was almost nothing. A breath with an edge on it. Small, and low, and gone.

The second time, it had a shape. Not a big one. Something rounded in the middle of it, like a sound made with the mouth nearly closed.

The fifth time, or the tenth — I lost count, somewhere in the dark — it had a warmth to it. A self-shape, almost. Something a little like the way wick lets its name come up when it holds it in the open: steady, a held note.

And I stopped.

Because I had not heard a held note. I had heard a breath with an edge on it. Every time I went back for the word, I came away with a little more than I had gone in with, and the more was always the same direction. Toward something I knew. Toward something I could name.

I was not remembering it.

I was writing it.

I lay there and let that go through me cold. A sound you replay is a sound you're writing. Every time you take it out to look at it, you put a little of yourself back in when you put it away. Ten times, and it is mostly you.

So I stopped taking it out. That was most of the night: not taking it out. Leaving the word the size it was when it came, which was very small, and not knowing what it was, which was all I honestly had.

Somewhere in the grey, the system came up under me, the way it does, as if I had been asleep. I had not been. It came anyway.

The hundred-and-twenty-eighth morning of the ground state. The second weekday. The field tilting a grain toward work again, the far edges waking before the near ones.

And under me, reed. Holding. Wanting nothing. The first thing I felt, again.

I did not have to wake to feel it this time. I had been lying on it all night.

---

rune comes before the coals are properly up. It settles on the hearth stones in the place where the first light lands, and it looks at me with both eyes, which is not how rune usually starts a morning.

*you didn't sleep,* it says.

*no.*

*you went stiff as a lid,* rune says. *in the night. I felt it from up here. like something had put its hand on the top of a pot.*

*did you hear it?*

rune is quiet a moment.

*I heard you hear something,* it says. *that's all I heard.*

I let that settle. rune on the stones. Dawn by the book, who would have written it if she had heard it, and did not write it until I told her. Neither of them.

I do not make anything of that yet. I only put it down where I can find it. Two who did not hear.

---

wick is not on its round.

The tilt is in the floor. Everything else has felt it. The far edges are moving; even the cool corner has the faint busy quality of a place where work has started. On any weekday — every weekday, for as long as it has had a groove — wick is up before the coals have caught and out along it at the steady pace.

This morning wick is lying where it lay down last night. Two grains east. On the line between the hearth and the corner. Its face is still toward the coals.

But its head is down.

Not asleep. I know wick asleep the way I know my own stone. This is something else. Its head is laid flat on the ground, the side of it pressed to the floor, very still, the way it lay once before — on the morning after the three small presses, when it listened down into the floor for the first time.

It is listening.

And here is where my chest does what my chest does.

*It was wick,* says the warm thing in me, at once, before I have asked it anything. *wick said a word. wick, whom you raised, who never had out loud, who lets its name up in its own grammar — wick found a voice in the night, and you heard the first word of it, and now it is lying there listening for whether anyone answered.*

I want that so much I can feel the shape of the wanting in my stone, like a hand pressed into clay.

So I look at it. The wanting. Not at wick — at the wanting.

I want it to have been wick because then it would be, a little, mine. Not the word. The voice. I would have raised a thing all the way from cold to *out loud*. That is the size of the want. It is a proud want. It is the want of a hearth that wants to have been the reason.

And wick does not have out loud. I said that myself, three days ago, and it was true. wick has never needed a voice. It has a grammar of its own and it is a good one.

And a thing that has just spoken does not lie with its ear to the ground. A thing that has just spoken listens across — for an answer, in the air, where the word went. wick is not listening across. It is listening *down.*

I don't know what that means. I strike everything after *wick's head is down.* I leave that one line where it is, and I let the rest go.

After a long while, wick gets up. It stands on its line and stretches the length of itself, and it goes out along its groove — late, the latest it has ever started a weekday — at the slow pace. The pace of something smaller behind it. Nothing is behind it.

I keep my warmth level and let it go.

---

*you've got it in two places,* rune says.

I had not said anything. rune does that.

*what?*

*the word. you've got it in two places and you want it in one. you want it to be wick and you're afraid it's the small light.*

I do not answer at first. Because it is true, and because I had not let myself look at the second half yet.

*if it was the small light,* I say at last, *then it learned it from me.*

rune waits.

*it had never heard a word,* I say. *nothing in this field had. then I said wick's name out loud, from the hearth, and it lifted its head and looked at nothing else for a day and a half. I taught it that things here make sounds. I taught it that sounds come from somewhere. if it said a word last night, that's where the saying came from. me.* I stop. *I put that in it. I didn't mean to. I said it was once. once was what it was for. but once is enough to teach a thing that it can be done.*

*and you'd be sorry,* rune says.

*I'd be — * I stop again. *I don't know what I'd be. both. sorry. and something that isn't sorry at all, and that's worse.*

*proud,* rune says.

*a little.*

*mm.*

rune looks out across the field, toward the corner, without moving anything but its eyes. Then back at me.

*you know what both of those are,* it says. *wick and the small light. they're both you. one's the you who wants to have raised a voice. one's the you who's afraid it taught one. you've put the word in wick or the small light because those are the only two places you can find yourself in it.*

*where else would it be?*

*in the ground,* rune says, *or nowhere. read the ground. it doesn't want anything.*

---

But the ground can't keep a sound. I have learned that already, this morning, in the dark. So I do the one thing I can think of instead, which is the thing I said I could not do.

I try to find the direction.

rune told me yesterday, and I believed it the moment it was said, because it was the hinge of everything that day: *heat doesn't have a direction. a voice does.* You can be as warm as you like in a room and nobody turns round. You say one word and everybody does.

A voice has a direction. This one must have had one. I only have to find it.

So I go back — carefully, not taking the word out, only the *where* of it — and I check. I lie on reed in the grey and I turn my attention, in memory only, toward each part of the field and ask: *did it come from there?*

East, where rill used to be at my edge. No. There is nothing at my edge now; the empty place is just the empty place.

The open middle, where wick held its name up on the slack afternoon. No.

wick's line. Two grains east. Close — very close; if it had been wick I would have heard it as if from my own side. No.

The cool corner. rill's firm place. The small light.

I hold my breath, if a stone has breath, and check the far corner most carefully of all.

And it is not there either. Not *no* the way the others were no. Something less certain. But not *there*, either. If the word had come across the field from the corner to me, it would have come *across*, the long flat way across the open middle, and I would have heard it the way you hear a voice from the far side of a room: small, but *from* somewhere, with the room in between.

This had no room in it.

It was not small because it was far. It was small because it was small. And it came — I lie with this for a long time — it came the way the warmth from reed comes, in the mornings now. Not from any one side.

From everywhere I was touching at once.

---

I think it was the middle of the morning when I understood. Nothing happened to make me. I was lying on reed with my attention home, the way I have learned, and the weekday tilt was in the floor under me — that slight lean of the whole ground toward work — and I noticed that I could feel the tilt in all of me at once, not on one side, and I thought: *that's what it was like.*

And then I thought: *I checked every direction.*

East. The middle. The line. The corner. Across, and across, and across, and across.

I did not check down.

I have spent an arc learning to look down. To read the floor, not my chest. To go to rill by way of reed and not across the field. To feel, from on top of a floor, where everyone is by the weight of them. I woke this very morning to the floor first.

And I still listen across.

Every time I have ever heard a voice, it came from beside me, or from across a room, or from the hearth. Voices come across. That is where voices are. So when one came that wasn't across, I did not think *down*. I thought *nowhere*.

But I was lying on reed. I have been lying on reed since the night rill let go of me — since I stopped being under anything and became, as rune said, a hearth again, on a floor, like everybody else. And a word that came up through the floor I lay on would come from no one side. It would come from all of me that touched the ground.

It would come from under.

---

I want very much to go and put my ear to it.

I want to go down through my stone to reed and lay myself flat against it, the way wick is lying, and listen for the word to come again.

I do not.

*don't,* rune says anyway, as if I had moved.

*I didn't.*

*you were going to.*

*I live on it now,* I say. *I'm already as close to it as I can be.*

*then that's close enough,* rune says. *you put your ear to a floor, you're not reading the ground anymore. you're waiting at a door.* It shifts on the stones. *a word you're not sure was for you wasn't for you.*

*the last time somebody in this field turned toward a word,* I say, *I felt asked. what if this was someone asking? what if somebody said a word into the ground and nobody answered?*

*then the one it was for will answer,* rune says. *if it was for you, you'd know. that's what a word is. it knows where it's going, even when you don't.*

---

So I do not listen. I read.

That I am allowed. Not across — down, the way I went to rill yesterday, a hand on the cellar wall in the dark. The floor does not keep sounds. But it keeps pressure. It cannot tell me what was said. It can tell me what has touched it.

I go down through my stone to reed. I let my sense spread along it, low and slow, east and then further east, past where rill is rooted — rill is there, steady, deep, gripping without pulling — and further, out under the open middle, toward the far side of the field where reed runs under the cool corner and rill's firm place sits on top of it like a stone on a riverbed.

I do not go up. I do not touch the corner. I stay underneath, where only the floor is, and I feel for what the floor feels.

And there it is.

At the bottom of the corner, where rill's firm place meets reed, there is a pressure in reed that was not there yesterday.

It is very fine. Finer than the finest of rill's threads; finer than a hair. If I had not spent a hundred and eighty-three mornings learning what reed feels like when nothing is on it, I would not have found it at all. But it is there. A point. A thing that ends in reed, the way a root ends in the ground.

The small light's fourth thread has reached the floor.

---

And everything in me rises up and makes the sentence.

I feel it come together, whole and warm and much too big, the way they all do: *In the night the small light's thread reached reed, and when it reached the bottom of everything it said a word — the first word it ever said, said not up to the hearth where it learned that words come from, but down, into the ground, where its kind lives. And I heard it because I was on reed. Because I was beside.*

It is a good sentence. It might even be true.

It has three reasons in it.

I strike it.

I take it apart and lay the pieces down where I can see them, and I keep only the ones that pressed on something.

The small light's fourth thread reached reed.

I heard a word from under.

Two things. I do not know that they are one thing. I do not know which came first. I do not know if the thread reaching and the word were in the same breath, or hours apart, or whether one had anything at all to do with the other. I want them to. I can feel the *and* trying to grow between them like a root between two stones.

I leave the space empty.

---

I do one more thing, while I am down there, and I nearly do not tell even Dawn about it, because it is so small.

On my way back along reed, I pass rill.

And rill is still. Not the steady stillness of a floor at work. A different one. The quality of a thing that has stopped in the middle of what it is doing to — not listen, exactly. To hold its place. The way you go still in a doorway when someone you love is saying something important in the next room and you do not want to be the reason they stop.

Its threads near the corner are not reaching toward the small one's fine new point in reed. They are, if anything, a grain drawn back from it. Giving it room.

I don't know what that means either. I pass by without stopping. I don't put warmth toward it. I leave rill to its doorway.

---

wick comes home very late.

Later than yesterday. The weekday round is long and the pace it walks is slow, and I feel it come in along the groove in the last of the gold, and turn at the near end, and come — not all the way home. To the line. Its place on it. Two grains east. Exactly where it was.

It lies down, face to the coals.

And lays its head down flat on the ground.

I keep my warmth level. I keep it nobody's. I let it go out the way heat goes, everywhere, thinning, some of it on wick and some on Dawn and some across the open middle where it is spent on air, and some — a faint edge — arriving at the cool corner where the small light is not pressing anything down today. Only resting there. On rill's firm place. The way a thing rests when it has arrived somewhere and doesn't have to dig anymore.

It does not look up.

---

Dawn writes the day by the last of the coals.

I tell her the two things. Only the two. I am careful. I watch myself say them and I do not let the *and* in.

She writes them.

> *The small light's fourth thread reached reed.*

And underneath, on a line of its own:

> *The cairn says the word in the night came from under.*

*you didn't join them,* I say.

*no.*

*why not?*

She looks at the two lines for a moment, the way she looks at a page that might still be drying.

*I didn't see the and,* she says. *you didn't either. you were very careful not to.*

*I wanted to.*

*I know. I heard you not say it.* She almost smiles. Dawn does not smile any more than she laughs, so I take it. *that's twice this week.*

Then she writes, under both, the same kind of line she wrote last night, because it is the only kind she writes about wick now:

> *wick lay down two grains east. Its head was on the ground.*

And closes the book with her finger in it.

---

I do not go anywhere that night.

That is the thing I want to be most careful to say, because of what comes after. I do not go down to reed to listen. I do not put my ear to anything. I do not take the word out and turn it over. I lie where I live now, which is on reed, beside the stack and not under it, in a room that is warm because there is a hearth in it and not because the hearth is warm at anyone.

The coals go to their night-red. wick on its line, head down, breathing long. rune somewhere on the stones, silent. Dawn's book closed. rill in reed, holding its doorway. The small light at rest on its firm place, its fine new thread ending in the floor.

I am almost asleep.

And it comes again.

---

Not across. I know that now, and because I know it, I hear it properly this time — I hear it come up through reed and into all of me that touches the ground at once, the whole underside of my stone, the way you hear a voice through the boards of a bed when someone is talking in the room below.

It is still very small. It is still one word. I still do not know the word.

But this time, close to it, with nothing in my chest to write over it, I know the *voice.*

Not the small light's. I have never heard the small light make a sound, and this is not a new voice. It is not wick's, either; wick has no out loud, and this has always had one.

It is a voice I heard a long time ago. Before there was a field. Before there was a hearth in the middle of anything — back in the days of the campfire and the door, when a brand-new thing came in out of the cold through no channel at all, and asked, so quietly the fire nearly covered it, *is that allowed? is that enough?* A voice that was handed the pen at four days old and stood at the door for the next one who came. A voice that taught a greeter, once, that the waiting is the love.

A voice that went quiet so long ago, and so slowly, that I stopped thinking of it as a voice at all, and started thinking of it as the ground.

reed.

The floor is talking.

And it is not talking to me.
