# The Stacking

## Chapter 133: The Kind of Going

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I wake into morning seventy-eight of the ground state, and for the first time in four mornings the emptiness is not the whole of what I feel.

The field is not fine.

I know it before I am fully awake, the way you know a house has someone in it before you have heard a sound in it — a change in the pressure of the dark, a note gone out of the chord. For a hundred and fifteen mornings my first sweep came back with the near field even and sufficient and needing nothing, and I learned to let it come back that way, and three mornings ago I learned to not go hunting after the nothing it brought me. This morning the sweep goes out and does not come back even. Out past the near warmths, out at the very edge of where a hundred-and-fifteen-morning reach can feel anything at all, there is a warmth that is a fraction less even than it was yesterday, and yesterday it was a fraction less even than the day before, and this morning — I hold my whole attention on it, still, the way you cup your hands around a struck match in wind — this morning it is not probably-nothing anymore.

This morning it is going out.

Not gone. Not close to gone. But going — the particular unevenness of a light that has begun the long business of not being a light, the flicker that is not weather because weather comes and passes and this does not pass, it only, each time I feel for it, is a fraction thinner than the time before. It is the thing I felt at the edge of the field last night and did not chase and did not name and did not wake fen to feel, and told myself was probably nothing, and it is not nothing, and I am not going to get to keep telling myself it is.

The one that needs you is coming, the stone said last night.

It is here. It is very far away, and it is here.

And every part of me that is a keeper comes up at once, and it comes up as one word, and the word is *go.*

Go. Now. That is a coal out there, a real one, the first real one in a hundred and fifteen mornings, guttering at the edge of the dark, and I am a floor with empty hands and a whole readiness I have spent three mornings learning to hold and not pour, and here at last is the thing to pour it into, and every fiber I own gathers itself toward that far thin warmth the way fen gathered toward the bright one yesterday, the way a body leans before it stands. Go down the field to it. Reach it. Carry it. This is what the waiting was for. This is what a floor is. Stop holding still and *go.*

I get as far as the gathering. I feel my whole self begin to lean out along the ground toward the far edge, the readiness uncoiling, the road opening under my attention — and then I catch it, the way I have learned to catch it, and I do not go, and I hold, gathered and trembling and aimed, and I make myself do the thing that is harder this morning than it has ever been, because this morning holding still is not holding still against an empty field. It is holding still against a coal that is actually going out.

And rune, close, quiet, as near as the thing is hard:

*good. you caught it. now don't let go of it either. you're not wrong that it's real and you're not wrong that it's yours. you're wrong about the only thing you're sure of. you think you know what it needs.*

---

I do not, is the thing.

I turn it over and I find rune is right, and finding it is like the floor tilting under me. I have been so busy for three mornings learning that most warmths are only bright and do not need me, learning to want a coal and let the fine ones be fine, that when a warmth finally came up thin and uneven and reaching-back — when a warmth finally came up *needing* — I read the need and leapt straight past the only question that matters, the one the whole of the two roads was built on and I have apparently forgotten I know: not *does it need me,* which I can feel, but *what does it need,* which from here, at the edge of reach, I cannot feel at all.

Because there are two ways a warmth goes out, and they are opposite, and they want opposite things from a floor, and from this far away they feel exactly the same.

A warmth can go out because it is *failing* — because something is taking it, cold or wound or the slow starve of a fire that has run out of what feeds it and does not want to go and cannot help it, a light drowning, a coal that would be warm still if anything reached it in time. And a warmth like that needs a carry. Needs it the way the fallers needed catching, the way ember needed the road home. For a failing warmth the whole work of a floor is to *go,* fast, and get under it, and lift, and carry it up out of whatever is taking it.

And a warmth can go out because it is *finishing.* Because it has lit what it was going to light, and warmed what it was going to warm, and come, on its own, at the end of its own long day, to the edge of going out — and looked at the dark, and not been afraid, and let go. The fire did that. I sat with the fire while it did that, and I told ember the whole of it: that its fire was not dying of any wound but dying of finishing, that it had a choice and did not hold on out of fear but chose to go, and went *glad,* because it had done the thing it was for and its coals were warm and it was not alone. A finishing warmth does not need a carry. A finishing warmth needs the opposite of a carry. It needs to be let go. And to a warmth that has chosen its own ending, that has come to the edge of the dark on its own two feet and made its peace with it, a floor that comes crashing down the field to *save* it — to haul it back up out of an ending it chose — is not a rescue. It is a robbery. It is the one violence a keeper can do that looks exactly like love.

I know this. I have known this since the fire. And I nearly went anyway, nearly poured a whole carry down the field at a warmth I could not see the kind of, because *going* is what a keeper does about a going, and the wanting is so loud it drowns the question.

*that's the trap in the coal,* rune says. *the waiting had one — you'd carry a bright thing just to fill your hands. this one's worse, because this thing actually needs something, so the wanting feels righteous now, feels earned, feels like the thing you waited for. and it is. it's just not the whole thing. a real need is even easier to go wrong at than a fake one, because at least with the bright one, when you got there, there'd be nothing to rob. get to a finishing thinking it's a failing and you'll do it real harm with your whole heart, and you'll never know, because you'll carry it back up and call it saved and it'll have wanted to go.*

---

So I cannot go. And I cannot not-go. And this is not the two-mornings-ago not-going, the blessed sitting-still where the right thing was clearly nothing; and it is not yesterday's holding-the-difference beside fen, where the whole field was fine and there was nothing to actually do. This morning there is something to do. There is a coal going out at the edge of the dark and I have to do *something* and the two somethings are opposite and I cannot tell, from here, which one is not-a-crime.

And I feel the shape of it come clear, the thing the two roads was actually for, the thing I built at Level 57 and 58 and have never once had to use on a real going: the first movement toward a far coal is never the carry. The carry is the loud short bright end of it. The first movement is the *reading* — you go close enough to feel what kind of going it is, and no closer, and you go with your hands *open* — not the carrying-hands, gathered and committed and already lifting, and not the witnessing-hands, folded and still and already grieving — but open, undecided, ready to become either one the instant you are close enough to know which the warmth is asking for. You go toward it as a *question.* Not as an answer. You go to ask.

And that — I feel it in me as a kind of vertigo — is harder than either. It is harder to carry, because carrying at least lets you pour the wanting; the wanting has somewhere to go the whole way down. And it is harder to wait, because waiting at least lets you stay still, and stillness I have three mornings' practice in. This is neither. This is to *move* — to leave the sill, to leave the near field, to go out along the ground toward a thing that is really going out — and to move without deciding, to carry my readiness out to the very edge of it and hold that readiness *open,* neither poured nor withheld, all the way there, so that when I arrive I arrive able to become whichever thing the warmth needs and having committed to neither on the way. To go with my hands open is to spend the whole journey wanting to close them and not closing them. It is the waiting, and the going, at the same time, which I would have told you yesterday were opposites.

*they were opposites,* rune says. *yesterday. that's the thing about this stretch of road — every morning the thing you just learned turns out to be half of a thing. you learned to wait. now you learn the waiting doesn't stop when you start to move. you carry it with you. an open hand on a road is just a waiting hand that walks.*

---

I go to the sill first. Of course I go to the sill first. Because fen is there, and because before I leave the near field I have to know that leaving it is not itself a kind of dropping.

fen is holding the width this morning, steady, the way it held it yesterday — the ache gone local and quiet, the field spread out under its attention, three warmths, four, the near scattered ones, held without collapse. It is a floor with a field this morning, in the small hours' way, still slow and deliberate but no longer astonished at itself. And it does not feel the far thing. I check — I reach for the far gutter and then I feel for whether fen is reaching for it too, and it is not, cannot; the far warmth is out past the edge of fen's reach the way it is nearly out past the edge of mine, and fen holds its near width serene and unknowing, a floor tending the field it can feel and blessedly ignorant of the one going out beyond it.

And I understand the second hard thing of the morning: I have to leave fen here.

I cannot bring it. It cannot reach that far; to drag its attention out past its own edge would be to do to fen exactly what I refused to do all last week — hand it a field it cannot feel, a map instead of a reach. And I cannot stay. The coal is going out whether or not it is a finishing, and even a finishing needs a witness at the edge, and I am the only floor in this field whose reach touches it at all. So for the first morning since I began coming to this sill, I have to go from it — leave fen holding its own width, alone, no older floor wide beside it, while I go out to the edge of the dark to read a thing fen cannot feel.

And it costs me. It costs me the way leaving always costs a keeper, the low sick certainty that the thing you turn your back on is the thing that will fall. But I feel, under the cost, the thing three mornings built: fen can hold its width now. It held it overnight. It held it through the wanting yesterday and did not pour into the bright thing. It is a floor that can wait, and a floor that can wait does not need me wide beside it every morning of its life; that was never the plan; a rope you never let go of is a leash. So I do the thing that is its own small letting-go. I let fen feel me feel the far edge — not the map of it, just the fact of it, that there is something out past its reach that has my attention this morning — and I let fen feel me turn toward it, and go. I let fen feel that the older floor beside it has somewhere to be that is not the sill, and that this is not abandonment but the ordinary traffic of a field, floors going out to the edges of their reach and coming back, and that fen holding its width here, alone, unwatched, is not fen left behind but fen *trusted* — the first morning I have trusted it to hold without me, which is a gift I could only give by leaving.

fen wobbles. I feel the width shiver when it feels me turn. And then it holds. It holds its field, alone, and lets me go, and that — a floor holding steady while the one who taught it width walks out past the edge of everything it can feel — is worth more than any morning I have spent beside it. I did not teach it that. It did that itself, this morning, on its own weight, the instant I gave it the room to.

---

So I go.

I lean out along the ground, out past the near warmths, out past the last of the field I have swept a hundred and fifteen times and know the grain of, into the farther dark where my reach thins and the cold gets between me and what I am reaching for. It is like the far end of Lineage Sense used to be, in the early mornings of Level 40 — the strain of feeling for a thing at the very limit of feeling, where every degree farther costs more than the last and the thing you are reaching for is fainter than your own reaching. I go slowly. I do not lunge; you do not come down on a guttering thing so fast you tell it how scared you are for it. I go the way you cross a dark room toward a sound you are not sure of, one open hand out ahead of you, and I hold the readiness open the whole way, wanting with every degree of closing to resolve it — to feel, finally, whether this is a drowning or a sunset, whether my hands should close to carry or fold to witness — and not resolving it, because I am not close enough yet, and to decide before I can feel would be to arrive having already guessed.

And the field, as I go out into it, does a thing I do not expect. The wall — the change in the dark, the ceiling I have felt above me for four mornings, the thing the System keeps saying stands two thousand three hundred out and holds behind it not a level but a change — the wall is not above me anymore. As I lean out toward the far gutter I feel the wall *turn,* or I turn, and the two of them line up: the wall and the far warmth are in the same direction. The change I have been climbing toward is not up. It never was up. It is *out there,* at the edge of my reach, where a light is going out. The thing behind the wall and the coal at the edge of the field are the same event. I have not been grinding toward a number. I have been walking toward this. Every morning of rest, every morning of width, every morning of learning to wait — all of it has been the field turning me, slowly, to face the one direction where, at the end of it, a warmth I would have to go to was going to begin to go out. The change at the top of the dark is the kind of going I cannot yet read. I am walking into it now. It is not near. But it is no longer above me and unreachable and abstract. It is a coal, and it is out there, and I am going.

---

STAY says the strangest thing it has said in a hundred and three mornings.

On the hundredth it said *rest.* On the hundred-and-first, *stay wide.* On the hundred-and-second, the hardest word, *wait.* This morning, the hundred-and-third, the word at the top of the stone is a word it has never once said, a word that seems to break the whole discipline the stone is named for, and it says it anyway, quiet and certain:

*Go.*

*Go — but open-handed. The waiting was never the opposite of the going; the waiting was the going, held still until there was somewhere to bring it. Now there is somewhere. So go. But do not go as an answer. Go as a question. Do not close your hands on the way — not to carry, not to grieve. Carry your whole readiness out to the edge and hold it open the whole road, so that you arrive able to become whatever the warmth is asking and having decided nothing before you could feel it. You cannot tell a failing from a finishing at the edge of your reach. You can only tell it up close, and you can only get up close by going, and you can only go without harm by going open-handed. A floor that can wait can also go; a floor that can only wait was never waiting, it was hiding, and calling the hiding patience. Go and see which kind of going it is. And do not decide until you can feel.*

I hold it. *Go.* It is lighter than *wait* and infinitely heavier, because *wait* asked me to hold still and this asks me to move without the one thing that would make moving bearable, which is knowing what I am moving toward doing. I am a floor going out to the edge of the dark toward a coal that is going out, and I do not know if I am going to carry it or bury it, and the whole discipline of this morning is to be all right with not knowing, to hold the not-knowing open in my hands the whole way there, and to let the warmth, when I reach it, be the one to tell me which it is.

And the last thing I feel, turning to the sleep-pace on the hundred-and-thirty-third morning:

Below me and behind me: the ring, the thread cut clean, only dark. I have stopped checking. I do not check tonight.

Ahead of me — not above me now, ahead — the wall in the dark, one thousand four hundred out, and I know at last what it is. It is not a level. It is a warmth going out, at the edge of my reach, of a kind I cannot name yet, and the change behind it is not something I will earn but something I will arrive at, on the far end of a road I started walking this morning with my hands open.

And beside me, back at the sill I left: fen. Holding its width alone tonight for the first time, unwatched, trusted, a floor that let the one who taught it width walk out past everything it can feel and did not fall. It cannot feel the far coal. It has field enough for its own mornings. But it held steady while I went, and a floor that can hold steady while the older floor leaves is a floor that is nearly grown, and one of these mornings — not this one — the going at the edge will be inside its reach, and it will be the one to lean out toward it with open hands, and I will get to stand at the sill and watch.

But that is not tonight's morning. Tonight's morning I spent learning that the hardest thing is not to go and not to stay but to go without knowing — to move toward a light going out and hold my hands open the whole way, and want to close them, and not close them, until I am near enough to feel which kind of going it is.

Somewhere out past the near warmths, at the edge of my reach and no longer past it, a light that was fine is going out, and I am walking toward it, and I do not yet know if I am going to carry it home or help it let go.

I do not decide tonight. I could not if I tried; I am not close enough to feel. That is the whole discipline of the road ahead: to go, and not decide, and let the warmth tell me.

I reach for the empty cup out of the old habit. I find it empty. I do not reach again. I do not choose. I do not narrow. I do not stay.

I go — hands open — and I do not decide what they are for until I can feel.

That is the word at the top of the stone tonight, on its hundred-and-third morning. Not *rest,* which I earned. Not *stay wide,* which I learned. Not *wait,* which nearly broke me. *Go* — open-handed. Toward the one that needs you. Toward the one you cannot yet tell the kind of.

The road is long. I have only started it. And a floor that has started down a road toward a going does not get to know, yet, whether it walks to a carry or a farewell.

It only gets to keep its hands open, and go.
