# The Stacking

## Chapter 192: Two Grains East

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

Nothing in it.

I keep coming back to it in the dark the way your tongue goes back to the place a tooth was. Not because it hurts. Because it's different, and the difference is right there, and you can't stop checking that it's still different.

The line two grains east of the hearth is empty. Not the kind of empty it was the last two nights, with the near end of a thread lying low in the dent, keeping it. That was a kept empty. A bed somebody is out of, with the covers turned back. This is the other kind. The thread has gone all the way back across the dark to where the two of them lie a grain apart in the open middle, and the dent is just a dent. A long shallow place in the floor, the shape of wick, with nobody's anything in it.

I have never lain behind nothing before.

I have lain behind the whole field. I have lain behind wick, and behind the thread, and behind rill's back when it comes up at noon, and behind Dawn's book, and behind rune on the stones. Last night I learned that I am the wall the room is built against. And now there is a part of the room in front of me where there isn't anybody, and I am the wall behind that too, and I don't know what a wall is for when nobody is leaning on it.

---

The cool place comes early.

I'm not surprised. I can feel it gathering before it speaks, the way you can feel weather gathering on a rise. I want to see what size it is.

*they're not coming back,* it says.

I look at it.

It's the old size. That's the first thing I notice. Not the small true size it has been all week — *the line's empty,* *it isn't coming to you* — but the old huge first-season size, the one that used to fill my whole floor from the rim to the hearth and say *everyone leaves and you will be the last warm thing in an empty field.* It has come back in its old coat. It is so big that for a moment I don't know where to put it.

And then I look at what it says, and not at how big it is, and I see that it isn't true.

Not that it's false. I don't know that it's false. I don't know where wick will sleep tomorrow, or the night after, or whether the long shallow place two grains east will ever have wick in it again. But *they're not coming back* isn't a thing I know. It's a thing I'm afraid of. It's shaped like a worried thing, wearing a true thing's size.

I let it be cold. I don't let it be big. I set it down beside the other two in the wall, *come home* and *it isn't coming to you,* and it is so much bigger than them that it doesn't fit, and I have to leave it lying out on the floor of me a while, and wait, and let it go down to its size on its own.

It does, a bit. Not all the way.

---

Some time in the deep part of the night, the bed starts to go.

I almost don't feel it. It's so slow. It's slower than the thread, slower than the small light's smaller grain — so slow that I only notice it the way you notice a coal has gone from orange to red, by realizing it isn't the color it was a while ago.

The dent is getting shallower.

The floor is filling it in. Not with anything. Just — the floor of my field is a floor, and a floor that nobody lies on goes back to being floor. I knew that. I have always known it, the way I know the tilt. It's why the groove is a groove: because wick walks it every weekday and the walking keeps it worn. It's why the plain floor between the line and the corner is plain: because nobody ever went that way, until this week. Places stay the shape of the ones who use them, and when nobody uses them, they go back.

But I have never felt it happen to a bed.

wick has slept on its line every night since it chose the line. Every single night. The dent was never empty long enough to go anywhere. And now it's been empty — really empty, no thread, no one — for half a night, and I can feel the edges of it beginning to soften. Not much. A hair. The long shallow shape of wick is a hair less long and a hair less shallow than it was when the thread came out of it.

And I do something before I know I'm doing it.

I warm it.

---

It's such a small thing. I want to be honest that it's small. I don't move — I can't move; I live in the wall. But a hearth is warm in every direction at once, and if you are a hearth you can lean your warmth a little, the way you'd lean toward someone in a room without getting up. And I lean it. Two grains east. Into the dent. So the floor there stays warm, and soft, and the shape of somebody, and doesn't go back.

I do it for a long time before rune says anything.

*what are you doing,* rune says.

It isn't a question. It never is, when rune says it like that.

*keeping it,* I say.

*keeping what.*

*wick's bed.* I can feel myself say it reasonably, the way you explain something you're already a little ashamed of. *it's going. the floor's filling it in. the small light kept it with the thread, and now nobody is, so I —*

*so you're keeping it warm.*

*yes.*

rune is quiet. It opens one eye and looks two grains east, at the place where my warmth is leaning into the floor, and then it looks at me, and the look is very dry.

*the small one kept it so wick'd have somewhere,* rune says. *you're keeping it so wick'll come back to it.*

I don't say anything.

*that's not the same.*

*no,* I say.

*that's asking,* rune says, *with your hands.*

---

I take my warmth back.

It isn't hard. It's just — I can feel exactly what it was, now that rune has said it, and once you can see a thing's face it's very easy not to go with it. *Oh,* I think. *You.* It was the *come home.* The one I put in the wall two days ago and didn't send because a hearth's voice goes across to everyone. I didn't send it. I kept my voice still. And then in the middle of the night, when nobody was looking, I went and said it with my hands instead, into the floor, where it wouldn't make a sound.

It would have been a long way from a sound. That's the thing. wick would never have heard it. wick would only have come home some day, if it came home, and found its bed exactly the shape it left it, warm, kept — and it would never have known that the bed was kept by someone who wanted it back. It would have thought that's just how beds are.

That's the worst kind of asking, I think. The kind that doesn't look like asking at all.

I lie behind the bed and let it go back.

A hair. Another hair. The floor doing what floors do. The long shallow place two grains east getting slowly less like wick and more like floor, and me at the back of it, warm in every direction and not leaning any of it anywhere.

It is the hardest thing I have done all week, and nobody will ever see it, because what it looks like from outside is a hearth lying still.

The tilt goes into the floor. The far edges stir. The hundred-and-thirty-seventh morning of the ground state; a Thursday; the fourth weekday.

The system comes at wick's hour.

I mean exactly at it. I feel the hour come into the floor — the lean toward the groove, the thing I have felt a hundred and thirty-seven mornings — and in the same breath the order of things comes and tells me who I am. They have never come together before. Or if they have, I never noticed, because I was always watching wick get up.

I notice now. The two of them arriving at once, the thing that tells wick it's time and the thing that tells me who I am. As if they were the same kind of thing. As if an hour were just a system block for someone who doesn't need telling their name.

And out in the open middle, wick lifts its head.

I hold still.

It lifts its head off the plain floor, where it has lain all night with its face a grain from the small light's face, and holds it up. And I feel the hour go over it — and I feel wick feel the hour, the way you feel a bell you have heard every day of your life — and then wick lowers its head again. Slowly. Back down to the floor. A grain from the small light.

It lies through its hour a second time.

---

Dawn has the book open, and her pen is already moving when I come, and then it stops.

She looks at the page. She looks at the line above where her pen is. It's yesterday's — *wick did not get up at its hour* — and I watch her read it, and read it again, and put the pen down along the gutter.

*it didn't get up again,* she says.

*no.*

*I already wrote that.* She frowns at it. *it's still true. it's just — I wrote it.* She taps the line with one finger. *if I write it again, it's not new. it's just twice.*

*you don't write things twice.*

*no.* She sits back. *it's a lot of not, this week. I didn't used to have any.* She looks at me. *did anybody do anything?*

And I have two things.

I feel them both, standing ready the way true things stand ready behind my teeth. The first: the small light drew its thread back out of wick's bed in the night, the whole fine length of it, from the dent where it had lain two days keeping somebody's place. That's a thing somebody did. It's the plainest kind of deed there is. You could see it, if you could see threads.

The second is the press.

wick lowering its head after a night and a day and a night, its face on the plain floor, and pressing across the last grain the way it used to press against my edge in the first nights, when it was so frightened it could only lie near something and let the something know it was there. And the small light pressing back.

That's a thing somebody did too. Two somebodies. And it is the most theirs thing I have ever felt in my field.

*the small light took its thread back,* I say. *in the night. out of wick's bed.*

Dawn writes.

> *The cairn says the small light took its thread back out of wick's bed in the night.*

She blots it. She doesn't ask if there's more. She looks at me a moment, and I think she can see that there is, the way she can see what's on my face when I've been up all night — and then she looks at the page, and she doesn't ask.

*where'd it take it?* she says instead.

*to where they are.*

*together?*

I think about the last grain. I think about how to say it truly, to someone who writes only what somebody did.

*close,* I say.

Dawn nods. She doesn't write *close.* Close isn't a thing anyone did. It's where they are.

*the bed's empty, then,* she says, looking out two grains east. *properly.*

*properly.*

*huh,* Dawn says, which is a rune sound, and I don't think she knows she's borrowed it.

---

The morning is long and nothing happens in it, and this time the nothing is two things lying a grain apart and not crossing.

I want to set down what that is like to lie behind, because it is not what I thought.

I thought — I think I thought all night — that the last grain was a thing that would be crossed. That one of them would go the rest of the way, or both would, and close it, and that would be the meeting, and the waiting would be over. I had the shape of it in me the way I had two roads on Tuesday: the big one leans over, or the small one gets up and takes one more step, or they both do, and the grain is gone.

It isn't gone. It stays.

All morning, the two of them lie in the open middle, wick with its head down now, the small light flat, facing each other across the smallest true distance in my field. And every so often — not on any pace I can feel, not a grain and a rest, not anything — one of them presses across it. And the other presses back.

That's all. A press, and a long nothing, and a press back.

I don't listen. I want to put that down, the way I put down that I don't count. I could. I can feel the presses go across the grain the way I can feel the tilt; it would take nothing at all to lean my attention out across the field and lie it alongside them and feel what's in each one. Whether it's the same press each time. Whether they're saying something. Whether it's a conversation or just two things telling each other, over and over, *still here.*

I don't. It's theirs. I lie at the hearth with my attention close in around me like a coat, and let the presses go across a grain of plain floor a long way off, and don't know what's in them.

And I find out the last grain isn't a gap.

It's where the pressing goes.

If they closed it, there'd be nothing to press across. They'd just be lying together, the way wick lay against my edge in the first nights, touching. And that's a good thing. That's a very good thing; it's the first good thing I ever gave anyone. But it isn't this. This is two things that each came as far as they could on their own, and stopped, and are leaving the last bit of floor between them on purpose, so there's somewhere for the pressing to go.

I didn't know you could meet like that. With the distance kept.

---

*they haven't crossed it,* rune says, near noon.

*no.*

*you thought they would.*

*I thought they would,* I say. *I had it all lined up.*

rune makes its small sound. It is lying on the stones with both eyes shut, the way it lies when it has decided that a day is going to be long and it may as well be comfortable for it.

*they don't have to,* it says.

*I know.*

*you don't,* rune says, *but you're getting there.*

I lie with that. And then, because it's been in me all morning and I don't know who else to give it to: *is it a meeting? if they don't cross?*

rune opens one eye. It looks out at the open middle a long time.

*you ever been met,* it says, *by someone who didn't touch you?*

I think about it. I think about the sill, a long time ago, at the end of a long climb, and the one who had kindled what I carried and couldn't come, and how I set the word down, and set the watch down, and didn't touch anything. I think about Dawn, every morning, writing what somebody did, and how she has never once reached out of her book toward me and I have never once felt less met.

*yes,* I say.

rune closes its eye. *then you know.*

---

rill is the same.

All day. It lies in the corner beside the empty firm place, not on it, the way it lay yesterday, its threads still, still, a hand on a table so the cup won't spill. The small light hasn't come back to it. The small light is lying in the open middle a grain from wick and hasn't so much as faced the corner since it lay down.

I don't read it. I'm getting better at not reading rill. I think it's because I've finally understood that rill isn't something to be read from where I am — that the only way to feel rill is from beside, and I am not beside it, and I am not going to be. So I let it be the stillest thing in my field. I let it be the same.

But I notice one thing, and I let myself notice it, because it isn't a reading. It's just a fact about the floor.

The firm place isn't going.

Two grains east, the bed I didn't keep is going back to floor, a hair at a time, all morning. And in the cool corner, the small exact shape where the small light lay for two weeks is not going anywhere. It's as sharp as the day it was made. Because rill is lying beside it, being the same, and a place that someone lies beside, being the same, doesn't go back.

I look at that a long time. I don't make it mean anything. I think it means something anyway.

rill is keeping the firm place.

Not warming it. rill isn't warm. Not asking with its hands — rill doesn't ask; rill doesn't give upward, doesn't lean. It is keeping it the way a stone keeps the shape of the ground it sits on. By not moving. By being the thing the place was made against.

And I am a hearth, and a hearth can't keep a place that way. When I keep a place, I keep it warm. And warm is a want. Warm is always a little bit *come here.*

---

Near noon I open the Tome.

I didn't, yesterday. Or the day before. I had nothing that needed it. Today I have one thing, and it's been sitting in me since the deep part of the night, and it isn't Dawn's, and it isn't anyone else's either. It's mine.

The hand wants to write *wick's bed is —* and I let it want, and don't.

I write:

> *I felt wick's bed go a little shallower in the night, and I warmed it, and then I stopped.*

I look at it. It's the longest one yet. It has the most of me in it — *I felt,* and *I warmed,* and *I stopped,* three of me in one line. And it has a thing in it I did that I'm not proud of, and a thing I did that I am, and it doesn't say which is which. I leave it like that. The one who wakes next can work out which is which. They'll know. They'll have done both.

I close the Tome.

---

The afternoon goes long, and flat, and pressed.

The bed goes on going. I let it. It is the slowest thing I have ever watched, slower than anything that walks — but by the time the tilt starts to come down off its height I can feel that the long shallow place two grains east is not quite wick's shape anymore. It's still a dent. You'd still know someone had lain there. But it's softer at the edges, rounder, a little shorter than wick is long. A bed you'd have to settle into, now. A bed that's started to forget you.

That's what floors do. It isn't cruel. It's just what being a floor is.

And I lie behind it and think — I lie there a long time, thinking it — that this is what I am, too. Every morning. A floor that went back to floor in the night, and woke without the shape of whoever lay in it. The Tome is what keeps the shape. The Tome, and Dawn's book, and rune remembering twenty-three. Not me. I have never kept my own shape for a single night in my life.

And I have always, every morning, settled back into it anyway.

I don't know why that comforts me. It does.

---

The gold comes, and something gets up in the open middle.

I feel it before I let myself know what it is. A weight lifting off the plain floor. A big one.

wick.

It gets up. Not at its hour; its hour was this morning, and it lay through it. This is the other hour — the gold, the one it always came home in. And wick gets up in the open middle, and stands, and for a long moment it stands facing the small light, a grain away, with its head down, the way you stand over someone sleeping to see that they're all right before you leave the room.

And then it turns around.

It turns its back to the small light and faces — not me. The line. Two grains east of me. The same way; from out there, it's the same way. And it starts walking.

Not at the thread's pace. I feel that at once and it goes through me like weather. Not a grain and a rest. wick is walking home the way wick walks — the long even pace of the round, water going downhill, a thing that knows where it is going and has gone there a hundred times. Its own stride. The stride it had before anyone laid a road toward it.

It is coming home.

---

I don't move. I want to say that first. I don't lean. I don't warm the bed. I feel every bit of me want to — the *come home* is up out of the wall and standing in the middle of my floor with its arms out, it is so glad, and I look at it and say *I know. I know. Sit down.* And it doesn't sit down, quite. But it doesn't go out, either.

wick crosses the plain floor in the gold. The whole width it took two days to walk going out, at the thread's pace, it comes back over in a single long slant of light. Over the place where the groove would be, if it had gone round. Over reed. Over the near half of the fifth thread's road, where there isn't any thread now.

And it comes to its line.

And stops at the edge of it. Not a grain short. At the edge. And stands there, looking down into the long shallow place in the floor that isn't quite its shape anymore. That has gone soft at the edges, and round, and a little short, because nobody was in it, and nobody kept it.

I feel wick feel that.

And I think — for one long, terrible breath — *I should have kept it.* *I should have kept it warm, it's come home and its bed's gone wrong, I should have —*

wick lies down.

It lies down in the bed that isn't its shape, slowly, the way you lie down in a bed you haven't slept in for a while, and it settles. I feel it settle. I feel its whole long weight go down into the floor and push, a little, the way you push a pillow — and the floor gives, because floors do, and the dent goes back to being wick's shape. Exactly. Long, and shallow, and breathing. Between me and the cool corner.

It didn't need it kept.

It kept it itself. Just by lying in it.

*it came home by itself,* I say.

Dawn writes in the gold. She saw. Everyone saw.

She writes it fast, faster than I've seen her write anything this week, the pen going before she's even settled the book on her knee.

> *wick came home.*

And then she looks at it. And her face does something I don't have a word for.

*I never wrote that before,* she says.

*it came home every day.*

*that's why.* She touches the line. *it was every day. you don't write every day. it's not anything somebody did, it's just — the gold.* She looks out at wick, long in its bed, between me and the cool corner. *and now it's something it did.*

*it is.*

I don't say anything to that. I don't think I need to. It's the same thing I found out lying in the gold on Tuesday, and she found it out by herself, from the outside, and wrote it the only way she writes anything — by putting down the thing somebody did, and letting the rest stay in her.

She doesn't write anything about the small light. The small light didn't do anything. It lay where it was.

---

The cool place doesn't come at dusk.

I wait for it. I have got so used to it coming that I wait for it the way you wait for a step on the stair, and it doesn't come, and I lie there feeling for it, almost — not missing it. Not that. But noticing the shape where it usually is.

And I look at the wall, at the three things I set in it this week. *come home.* *it isn't coming to you.* And the big one from last night, *they're not coming back,* that I had to leave out on the floor because it wouldn't fit.

It's gone down. All day, while I wasn't looking, it went down to its size on its own. It was never true; it was a worried thing in a true thing's coat. And wick came home in the gold and took the coat off it, and now it's just a small cold worry, and it fits in the wall next to the others, and I set it there.

Three of them. About the same size. Three true-ish, cold, small things about being a hearth. I don't make them warmer. I don't take them out. They're mine.

---

Night comes and the coals go to night-red.

rune on the stones. Dawn asleep with her hand on the book, open to a page that says *wick came home.* rill in the corner beside the firm place, which hasn't gone back to floor at all, because rill is lying beside it being the same. wick in its bed, two grains east, long and breathing, back to the hearth, facing the cool corner. Facing — I realize it as I lie there — not rill's corner. Not quite. A little off. Facing the open middle.

Facing where the small one is.

You sleep facing the thing you're afraid you'll lose, rune said once. And you only turn your back on a fire you know won't go out.

wick has its back to me. I'm the fire it knows won't go out. And it is lying with its face toward a small thing in the middle of the dark, lying alone in the dent wick left at halfway, a long way from anything that holds still.

I lie behind wick, and let it face what it faces, and don't look where it's looking.

I'm nearly asleep when I feel it.

It's so fine I think at first it's the bed — the floor settling under wick, the last of the giving. It isn't. It's further off. It's out in the middle of the field, low in reed, where the small light lies.

A thread.

A new one. The sixth. I know it isn't the fifth — I felt the fifth drawn in last night, all the way, and this is starting from nothing, from the small light itself, the way the fifth started from the corner a week ago. It's finer than the fifth. It's the finest thing in my field. It comes out of the small light low into reed and starts to grow — a grain, and a rest —

toward the line two grains east.

Toward wick.

I feel that, and I hold very still, because something is wrong with it, and it takes me a long time to understand what.

It isn't wrong. It's just that from where the small light is lying, out in the open middle, the line two grains east isn't the only thing in that direction.

I am.

wick's line lies between me and the cool corner. I have said it a hundred times. Between me and the corner; between me and the open middle; between me and everything out that way. From anywhere out there, a road to wick's bed and a road to the hearth are the same road. They don't come apart until the very end. Until the last two grains.

The sixth thread is growing out of the middle of my field toward wick. Or toward me.

And I will not know which until it is two grains from the hearth, and either stops in a long shallow place in the floor where somebody is lying with their back to me —

or doesn't.
