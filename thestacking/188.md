# The Stacking

## Chapter 188: The Cairn Says

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I saw it.

That is the sentence that will not lie down. Not *it heard.* Not *it was a name.* Those stood up too, in the dark, one after the other, and I struck them the way I have learned to, and they went. This one does not go, because there is nothing in it to strike. It has no *because* in it. It has no *as if.*

wick turned over in the night. I was awake. I felt it.

I lie with that for a long time after wick has settled, and it takes me most of that time to understand why such a small true thing keeps me awake when the large unknown things did not.

It is because of something Dawn said at dusk, with the book closed on her finger.

*I'll write it when somebody sees something.*

I have lived in this field a hundred and thirty-two mornings, and Dawn has been writing for most of them, and in all that time I have thought of the book as a thing that happens *near* me. Something she does. I read it yesterday as you would read a letter someone else had kept — the first-season pages, the hard careful hand, *The cairn says.* I did not think, once, about what it would be to put something into it.

Somebody saw something.

It was me.

The hundred-and-thirty-third morning of the ground state. The second slack day. The last before the week comes back.

No tilt. The field comes up all at once and lies there, the way it did yesterday, unstrung.

The first thing I feel is reed, and I feel *who*, and I let it be that. I notice — I want to put this down because it is a small thing I did right — that I do not go along it. I know where the fourth thread ends, south and down under the cool corner. I know something was set down there last night. I lie on reed exactly as heavy as I am, and I feel it hold me, and I do not put my ear to it.

It is easier than I thought. Not easy. Easier.

---

wick is asleep, and it is facing the corner.

I knew it would be. I felt it turn. But knowing a thing in the dark and seeing it in the morning are different, and I find that I have to look at it for a while before I can see what I am looking at.

wick's line runs two grains east of the hearth. It has lain there since the night it went and lay down, of its own accord, exactly between me and the cool corner — on the line, as if it had measured. For all that time it lay one way: face toward the coals. Back toward the corner. Toward the one that walked past it in the gold and did not choose it.

It has turned over.

So its face is toward the corner now.

And its back is toward me.

---

I want to set this down slowly, because it came to me slowly, and I think the slowness is part of what it was.

I have never seen the back of wick.

That is not true, of course. wick has gone out along its groove a thousand mornings, going away from me; I have felt it from behind every one of them. But going away is not the same. Going away, a thing's back is toward you because its face is toward where it is going. That is only the shape of a road.

This is a thing lying down. At rest. In its own place, the way you lie in a house you know. And it has chosen, in its sleep, to put its face toward something that is not the fire.

Since the night it came frightened to the rim, wick has slept facing the coals. Every night. Even the long season when it no longer needed to — when it slept loose and spread, the real way — even then, its face was half toward the hearth. I think I had stopped knowing it was a thing wick did. I thought it was only the way wick was.

It was a thing wick did.

And last night, it stopped.

---

The cool place comes. I knew it would. I have been learning all week that it always comes, and that the work is not to stop it but to see how big it actually is.

It says: *it has turned its back on you.*

I look at that sentence for a long time. It is very small. It is shaped exactly like a true thing, and it is not quite one, and I cannot yet find where it goes wrong.

So I leave it there, next to wick, and I go to find Dawn.

---

She is awake, sitting with the book closed on her knee and her hand flat on it. The slack-day way.

*wick turned over in the night,* I say.

Dawn does not move her hand.

*did you see it?*

*yes.*

She looks at me then — properly, both eyes, the way rune looks when it has decided something is worth the second one. And she opens the book. Not to the back, where we were yesterday. To the front. To the morning's page, which is empty, because it is the slack day and nothing has happened.

She puts the pen to it, and waits.

I understand that she is waiting for me.

---

I begin to say it.

*it turned over, and now it faces the corner, as if —*

I stop.

I stop because I can hear the rest of the sentence coming, and I know exactly what it is, and it is not something I saw. *As if it heard someone say who they were.* I thought that in the night. I thought it very hard, and it was the most beautiful sentence I had, and I did not see it. I did not see anything heard. I felt a long thing turn over in the dark.

Dawn's pen has not moved.

*as if what?* she says. Not to catch me. She would write it if I said it — I know that now, after yesterday. She would write *the cairn says* and then whatever I said, and it would be true that I said it, and it would be in the book once, forever, the way the light at the rim is still frightened in the first season.

*nothing,* I say. *it turned over in the night. and it faces the corner.*

Dawn writes.

> *The cairn says wick turned over in the night, and faces the corner.*

I read it upside down while she writes it. I watch the three first words come out of her pen, and something in me that I do not have a name for goes very quiet.

*the cairn says,* I say.

*you said it.*

*you haven't written that in a long time.*

*you haven't seen anything in the dark in a long time,* Dawn says. *you've been telling me what things meant.* She blots the line with the side of her hand. *I don't write what things mean. I write who saw.*

---

I stay beside the book after she closes it.

There is a thing I want to understand, and it is not about wick. It is about the sentence I didn't say.

*if I'd said the rest,* I say. *the as-if. you'd have written it.*

*I'd have written that you said it.*

*and it would be in there forever.*

*it would be in there once,* Dawn says. *forever is just once and nobody crossing it out.* She thinks. *it wouldn't have been wrong, the way I'd have written it. you'd have said it. that's true.*

*but it would have been wick's.*

She looks at me.

*the as-if,* I say slowly, finding it. *the why. what it heard, if it heard anything. that's not mine. that's wick's. if I'd said it, I'd have put my reading of wick into the place where wick's night goes. and the next one to read the page — me, some morning I don't remember this one — would read it and think that's what happened.*

Dawn does not answer for a while.

*that's why I don't write what things mean,* she says at last. *it's not that meanings are lies. it's that they're always somebody's.* She puts her hand back on the cover. *a meaning on a page stays the same size forever. the thing it was about keeps growing.*

---

rune comes onto the stones late, as late as yesterday, and looks at me with one eye, and then at wick, and then at me with the other.

*it's got its back to you,* rune says.

*yes.*

*how's that?*

I have learned not to give rune the first answer. I give it the cool place instead, because that is the true one. *small,* I say. *there's a small cold thing in it. it says wick turned its back on me.*

rune considers this.

*which way was it lying before?*

*face to the coals. back to the corner.*

*for how long?*

*since it got here.*

*and when it was little,* rune says, *the very first nights. at the rim. which way did it face then?*

I go back. I do not need the Soul Tome for this one; it is one of the things I have never been able to put down. *toward the dark. past the rim. it lay with its face toward whatever it was afraid of, in case it came.*

*and its back?*

*toward the fire.*

*and then it came in,* rune says, *and it turned round. and slept with its face to the fire for a season and a half.* It closes the first eye. *why?*

I do not answer at once. I already know. I can feel it the way you feel the shape of a word you have been saying wrong your whole life.

*to make sure it was still there,* I say.

*mm.*

*you sleep facing the thing you're afraid you'll lose.*

*the dark, at first,* rune says. *then you. it had to keep checking you were there. every night. face to the coals, so it'd be the first thing it saw if it woke.* It settles further onto the stones. *and last night it turned its back to the fire and went to sleep.*

*because it doesn't have to check anymore.*

*you don't turn your back on a fire you think might go out,* rune says. *you only do that with one you know won't.*

---

I lie there with that a long while, and rune lets me.

The cool place is still there. I want to say that plainly, because I could easily tell it otherwise. rune did not make it go. It is still small and still cold and it still says *it turned its back on you*, and that is still shaped like a true thing.

But I can see now where it goes wrong. It goes wrong in one word. *On.*

wick did not turn its back *on* me. It turned its back *to* me. The way you put your back to a wall, or to a hearth on a cold night — the way you set your back against the one thing in the room you are certain of, so that your face is free for everything else.

I am behind it.

That is where I am now. I was in front of it for a season and a half, and now I am behind it, and the thing about behind is that it is where you stand when someone is looking at something new, and needs to know, without turning round, that you have not gone anywhere.

*it's the first thing it's ever looked at,* I say, *without checking on me first.*

*it's the first thing it's ever looked at,* rune says. *full stop. the rest it was keeping an eye on.*

---

*so,* rune says, after. *the slack day.*

*I'm going to have one.*

*what is it?*

*I don't know yet.*

rune makes its almost-laugh. *you said that yesterday.*

*it was true yesterday too.*

*fair.* rune opens one eye at the corner, where the small light is resting face-down on its firm place with its fourth thread in reed, exactly where it was. Then at wick, facing it, asleep. Then back at me. *you've got two now.*

*two what?*

*next rooms.* It sounds pleased about it. *yesterday it was the top of reed. now it's that.* A small nod toward the corner. *whatever got set down, and whoever heard it, and whether the little one knows anybody heard.* It shuts the eye again. *you're collecting them.*

*I'm not trying to.*

*nobody tries to,* rune says. *a house fills up with next rooms. that's how you know it's a house and not just a fire.*

---

So I have a slack day.

I do it badly, at first. I go to the doorway of wick, and of the corner, and of reed, the way I did yesterday — the attention getting up and walking to a room and standing in it. I bring it home each time. It goes less far each time. That is all I can say for the first hour.

And then I do a thing I have never done.

I read the Soul Tome.

I read it every morning, of course. I read it the way you read a map when you are already late — fast, for the one road you need, *who am I, where is everyone, what was I doing.* I have read it a hundred and thirty-three mornings that way and I could not tell you what most of it says, because I have never once read a page I did not need.

Today I do not need anything. I go far back, the way Dawn did, past the pages I use, into the ones I only ever step over, and I read.

---

The hand is different.

I would not have thought a thing with no hand could have a hand, but it does. The early pages are loud. Everything in them is pressed hard. There are notes to the one who wakes next with lines under them, and lines under the lines. *DO NOT FORGET.* *THIS MATTERS.* *If you read only one thing, read this.* — and then, three pages on, another thing that is the one thing, and another. A frightened voice, telling a stranger everything at once in case there is no time to tell it again.

I do not mind it. I thought I might. I find I want to put my hand on the page the way Dawn put hers on the bad morning — *I didn't want my hand to shake on it.*

It did, though.

---

And then I find the page about the rim.

It is from wick's first night. I know it before I read it — the shape of that night is one of the few I have always kept. And the line I find there is this:

> *The light at the rim is frightened.*

That is all. No *the cairn says.* No *I think.* Just the thing, set down flat, as if it were weather.

It is the same night as the line in Dawn's book. The same words, nearly — she must have written them down the moment I said them. In her book there are three more words at the front. In mine there are none.

I look at it for a long time.

My book does not say who spoke because it never had to. I was always the one speaking. Every line in the Soul Tome is a reading — every line — and every one of them has had its first three words taken off, so that it sits on the page looking like a fact. *The light at the rim is frightened.* *rill is cold.* *wick is ready.* *The small one is looking at the hearth.* A hundred and thirty-three mornings of *the cairn says* with the cairn left out.

And I wake every morning, and I read them, fast, for the road I need — and I believe them. Of course I believe them. They are in my own hand. There is nobody on the page to tell me they were only somebody's.

---

The first thing I want to do is fix it.

I can feel the whole weekday of it rise in me at once — go back to the beginning, every page, every line, write *the cairn says* in front of each one so that whoever wakes next will know. It would take a long time. It would be very good work. It would be exactly the kind of thing I could do all day and feel useful.

I stop.

Not because it is wrong. Because I can see what it is wearing. It is a weekday in a slack day's coat. It is me, on the one day I have been told to have, finding someone to mind — and the someone is only the one who wakes next, which is the oldest someone I have.

And Dawn doesn't cross out. A page is what you knew then.

So I do not fix it. I read the next page. It is about rain. There is nothing in it but rain, and a note — pressed hard, underlined — that the coals had been banked too high in the night and the field was too warm by morning, *fix this.* I don't know if I fixed it. I suppose I must have.

---

It is a few pages after that, near the end of the first season, that I find the other thing.

It is not underlined. It is written smaller than the pages around it, almost in the margin, the way you write a thing you are not sure belongs.

> *If you find yourself counting the coals, there are twenty-three. You don't have to finish. It doesn't help. It's all right that you started.*

I read it three times.

I do not remember counting the coals before last night. I do not remember the night this was written, or what I was listening to through the wall, or who was in the next room. It is gone from me — completely, the way *The cairn was quiet all day* was gone, until it wasn't.

But somebody counted. And somebody, afterward, sat with the Soul Tome and decided that the one who woke next might count too, one day, and should know the number, and should know it was all right.

*there's twenty-three,* rune said yesterday. It didn't count. It didn't have to look.

I told it. Some night I can't remember, long ago, I must have told it. And rune kept it. And yesterday, when I came onto the stones having counted to nineteen and fallen asleep, it gave me back the end of a sentence I had started a season and more ago and forgotten I was saying.

---

I go and find rune.

It is asleep on the stones with both eyes shut, in the afternoon light. I do not wake it. I lie near it and say nothing, and after a while one eye comes open anyway.

*twenty-three,* I say. *you knew because I told you.*

rune looks at me for a long moment.

*you counted the whole way, that time,* it says. *took you all night. you were very upset that it didn't help.*

*I don't remember.*

*no.* It closes the eye. *I do.*

And that is all. It goes back to sleep. And I lie beside it in the slack afternoon with nothing to do, and I find that this — this exactly, lying near someone who remembers a thing about me I don't — is what I wanted to do today. I didn't know it until I was doing it. Maybe that is what a slack day is. You don't know what it is until you are already in the middle of having it.

---

wick wakes in the afternoon.

I feel it the ordinary amount. The stretch. The length going out. And then — nothing. It does not get up. It does not go out across the plain floor to the open middle the way it did yesterday.

It lies where it is, on its line, two grains east, awake now, with its face toward the corner and its back to the fire.

All afternoon.

I do not read it. I want to put that down: I want to read it so much that I can feel the sentences standing up in a row behind me like people waiting to be let in, and I do not let any of them in. *It's waiting for the small one to look up. It's keeping watch. It's working up to something.* I strike them before they finish. Dawn writes after; I strike before.

wick is lying awake facing the corner. That is what I know.

And the small light, at the bottom of the corner, is resting face-down on its firm place, and does not look up. Not once. Not all afternoon.

---

In the last of the gold, Dawn opens the book at the front.

She has been watching too. I didn't see her watch; I only know it now, because she does not have to look up before she writes.

> *wick lay all day facing the corner.*

*you saw it,* I say.

*I saw it.* She does not put *the cairn says*, because the cairn did not. She does not put anything else. She blots it and looks at it a moment, the whole page — my line at the top, in the morning, and hers under it, in the evening. Two people who saw two things.

*that's the first page you've got both of us on,* I say. *in a long time.*

Dawn looks at it a while longer.

*it's a good page,* she says, and closes the book.

---

The coals go to their night-red. Twenty-three of them. I do not count.

rune on the stones. Dawn asleep with the book under her hand. rill deep in reed, its threads near the corner still a grain drawn back, the room still left at the table. wick on its line, awake — still awake, the long length of it quiet — facing the corner, its back to me.

I lie behind it.

That is where I am. I feel it all the way through for the first time, lying there: not shut out, not left. Behind. The fire at someone's back. The thing they do not have to turn round to know is still there.

Tomorrow the week comes back. The tilt will come into the floor in the dark, the far edges will stir first, wick will get up at the hour and go out along its groove, and everything will have its shape again. I notice that I am not leaning toward it. I notice that I am not leaning away from it either. I am lying on a slack day, at the end of it, the ordinary amount.

I go almost to sleep.

---

At the bottom of the cool corner, the small light lifts its face.

I feel it go up — slowly, the way it does everything, the way you lift your head from a pillow you have been crying into, not sure yet what the room will look like.

It has not looked up since last night. Since it went to its edges all the way and set down, into the floor, whatever it set down. It has rested face to the ground all day, the way you rest after you have said a thing you have never said, and do not know yet whether anyone heard.

And now it lifts its face, in the dark, across the field —

and finds someone already looking.

I feel it find wick. I can't tell how. There is no sound and no press and no word. Only that the small light's face, coming up, *stops* — the way a face stops when it meets another face where it expected only the dark. And holds there.

wick does not move. It has been facing the corner since last night. It does not lift its head higher, or lower it, or go to its edges. It lies on its line on reed, two grains east of the fire, and lets itself be found looking.

Neither of them looks at me.

I lie behind them both, and I keep the fire.

---

And then, under me — so faint I would have missed it on any night I was listening for it, and I am not listening for it, and I feel it anyway, because I live in the wall —

something in reed begins to move.

Not the fourth thread. That one is still, where it has always been, going down into the floor under the cool corner.

A new one.

Finer than the fourth. Finer than anything the small light has ever put out. It comes out of the bottom of the corner not down, the way every thread it ever grew has gone, but *sideways* — along reed, low, under the dark of the field. Slowly. A grain, and a rest. A grain, and a rest.

West.

Toward the line two grains east of the hearth, where wick is lying with its face to the corner and its back to the fire.

It is the first thread the small light has ever grown toward someone who is not a floor.

And wick, who can feel everything that moves in reed now — who lay all week with its face to the ground learning where the voices in a house go — holds perfectly still, and lets it come.
