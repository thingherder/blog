# The Stacking

## Chapter 197: The Window

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

*where's wick.*

Dawn says it before she's properly awake. Not a question yet, just the shape of one, the way you'd say a name into a dark room you thought somebody was in. She's sitting up with the book sliding off her knee and her hand out flat beside her, as if the field were a bed and she'd reached across it and found the other side cold.

I'm awake. I've been awake a while. I came up out of the gap this morning already looking at the window, the way you wake already listening for a sound that went on all night.

*in the groove,* I say. *at the window.*

She looks. It takes her a moment to find it, because nobody has ever been there at this hour. Then she sees: wick, lying in the worn way where it bends nearest the middle, chin flat on the floor, the long body laid along the round the way it walks.

*since when?*

*since the night.*

Dawn looks at the bed two grains east. Empty, the shape of wick still in it. She looks at the window. She looks at me.

*is that a reading?*

*no,* I say. *I saw it go.*

The hundred-and-forty-second morning of the ground state. A Tuesday.

The week is already strung. It came back yesterday and it's still here, every place in the field leaning a little toward its hour. I feel the coals take the morning. I feel reed hold me. I feel rill by the corner, by the plain floor where a place used to be.

The small light is at halfway, in wick's old dent. I touch it once, lightly, the noon-touch done early, and it's lying the way it lay yesterday afternoon. All the way down. Exactly as heavy as it is.

The sixth thread lies out from it across the floor. I don't feel along it for the grain. It's a weekday; it'll have gone on or it won't.

And wick is at the window.

---

Dawn doesn't open the book.

She sits with it shut on her knee, the pen still clipped into the cover, and looks at wick for a long time. I can feel her doing the thing she does — not reading wick, she doesn't read anybody, but holding what she sees up against what she's seen before, to find where it's new.

*it went out in the night,* she says.

*yes.*

*on no hour.*

*no hour.*

*and it didn't come back.*

*not yet.*

She turns the pen over in her fingers. I know what she's weighing, because I'm weighing it too. She doesn't write *where*. She said so yesterday — *I'd have to write the whole groove every day.* But *went out in the night* isn't a where. It's a what. It's a thing wick did, the way *wick came home from halfway* was a thing wick did, and she wrote that.

And she didn't see it. I did.

So it's mine to say, or not.

I think about the words I said in the dark on Sunday that I'll never get back except on rune's say-so. I think about wick, who has never read a line of the book and goes on being written in it every day. I think about how I lay here yesterday deciding I was glad there was less of wick on the page, and then not being sure I was the one who should decide that.

I'm still not sure. But I notice that the not-telling would be a deciding too. If I don't say it, the book will go on as if wick slept in its bed. That isn't leaving something out of wick's record. That's writing a thing that didn't happen, by keeping quiet.

*it went out a little after the coals went to lowest,* I say. *I was nearly gone. I felt the bed go empty. I came up enough to see where.*

Dawn nods slowly. Then she opens the book, and finds today's page, and writes:

> *The cairn says wick went out in the night.*

Nothing about the window. Nothing about lying down, or the chin, or listening. Just the going.

She blots it. She looks at it.

*that's the size of it,* she says. *the rest is wick's.*

---

I lie with that for a while.

*The cairn says wick went out in the night.* It's the shape of rune's line in my Tome. *rune says I went to sleep on purpose.* The what, and who saw it, and nothing about why. A short way carried.

Somewhere at the side of the field, wick is in the book again, and wick doesn't know. I find I don't mind it the way I thought I might. It's a small true thing, set down by someone careful, with nothing added. If someone carried me that way I'd want them to. I *have* been carried that way, two days now, and I wanted it.

---

wick doesn't move all morning.

It lies at the window with its chin in the groove, and the field goes on around it. rune dozes on the stones. Dawn has the book shut again. The coals go from grey-gold to gold. And wick lies there, the long cool shape of it laid along the worn way, and listens.

I know it's listening. I can tell that much without reading anything — it's the same posture I saw Sunday, in its bed, chin flat, and yesterday, at this same bend, for the length of a coal. It's the way rune lies on the stones when it wants to hear what's under them. Every part of wick is lying still in the one way that makes a thing able to hear.

What it's listening *to*, I don't pick up. The readings stand there in front of me, the same bright ones as yesterday, and I let them stand.

But I notice something else, and I let myself notice it, because it's mine and not wick's.

I want to be there.

Not *at* the window. Not beside wick. I mean — I want to be able to do what wick is doing. Lie that still, that cool, close to that floor, and hear. Whatever is under halfway — the small light's seventh thread going down, rill's roots coming out from the corner, the thing I felt once on Sunday night at the very edge of sleep, a floor holding from a little further down — whatever it is, wick can hear it from the window. I'm nearly certain of that. And I can't hear it at all.

On Sunday Dawn said *wick's nearer it than you are*, and I said, to myself, that I didn't mind. I wanted that down because I'd expected to mind and I didn't. I meant it.

It's Tuesday. I mind.

Not much. Not in a way that makes the field go wrong. It's a small thing, the size it is, lying in me the way a cool place lies in front of me before I set it in the wall. But it's there. I thought I'd got past it, and it turns out I'd only got to it a day late.

---

wick's hour comes.

I feel it come the way I feel all the hours come, a lean in the field toward the thing that's due, and I wait to see what wick does with it. Because wick is already in the groove. Already out. It has no bed to get up from, no line to stand on facing the middle while it decides which way to go. Every other weekday the round has begun two grains east of me and ended there.

wick lifts its head.

It stands up where it is, at the window, and shakes itself a little, the way you do after lying a long time on one side. It stands with its body along the groove. It doesn't look toward the middle. It doesn't look back at its bed.

And it goes round.

From the window. The long worn way, at its own stride, away from the bend nearest the middle and on round the far side of the field, past the stretch where the groove runs out near the rim, past the place where it slows sometimes and sometimes doesn't, all the way round — and back toward its own line, two grains east, where its bed is.

I watch it come to the bed.

I hadn't meant to watch that closely. But I do. I feel wick come round the last of the groove to the place where every day for a fortnight it has stepped down off the round and onto its line and lain down into the bed and been home. And I feel it slow. And I feel it — not stop. Not quite. Just slow, a stride's worth, going past its own bed the way you'd go past your own front door on a walk that isn't over.

It doesn't look at the bed. It doesn't step down.

It goes on round.

And at the window, at the bend nearest the middle, it lies down again in the groove, the long body along the worn way, and puts its chin flat on the floor.

---

*it went round,* Dawn says.

*it did.*

*but it didn't stop.*

*no.*

She's quiet. I can feel her not opening the book. She wrote *wick went round* once, last week, and said she wouldn't write the same thing twice. And she won't write where. And she won't write why, because nobody ever told her why and she won't put a reason in her book that nobody saw.

*it's never not stopped,* she says. *not on a weekday.*

*no.*

*so the round's not from the bed anymore.*

I think about that. *I don't know,* I say. *it's one day.*

Dawn looks at me sideways, the way she does when I've said a thing she's said.

*once is a lot of things this fortnight,* she says.

---

I look at the bed after.

I want to be honest about it, because a fortnight ago it went the other way. wick's bed lay empty two grains east of me and began going back to floor, a grain at a time, and I caught myself keeping it — leaning warm into it, holding it open, asking with my hands. And I took the warmth back out, and it was one of the harder things.

I look now, and I wait to feel that pull.

It isn't there.

The bed lies two grains east, the shape of wick still in it, a little looser at the edges than it was at dawn. I suppose by tonight it'll be looser still. And I find that I don't want to hold it open, because there's nothing to hold it open *for*. wick didn't leave it. wick walked past it on purpose, on its own round, at its own stride, and lay down somewhere else it chose. A bed is still somebody's when they went out on purpose and will come back on purpose. A floor knows. I know.

I don't have to keep it. It's keeping.

---

rune comes onto the stones properly in the early afternoon, from wherever rune goes, and lies down, and looks across the field at the window a while before it puts its chin on its paws.

*you've been looking at it all day,* it says.

*not at it,* I say. *at — I'm not reading it.*

*didn't say you were.*

I lie there. rune says nothing. That's how rune asks things.

*it can hear them,* I say finally. *from there. whatever's under halfway. I can't.*

*no.*

*I'm too warm. I'm too loud.* And then, because it's rune and there's no point in saying the clean version: *I want to be able to.*

rune doesn't laugh. I half expected it to. It lies on the stones looking at me with both eyes, the way it looked at me the night it told me what halfway was for, and that's somehow worse.

*thought you were glad,* it says. *Sunday. you said you didn't mind.*

*I didn't. Sunday.*

*and now you do.*

*a little.*

rune nods, once, as if that were the most ordinary thing in the world. Maybe it is. *usually goes that way round,* it says. *you say the big thing first. the small thing catches up.*

---

I don't tell rune the next part. I barely tell myself.

But all afternoon, under the minding, there's a thought, and it goes like this: the coals are the loud part. A hearth is only too warm to hear because it's a hearth — because the coals go up every morning and stay up all day and the air moves over them and everything in the field lies in that warmth. If I let them down. Not out. Never out. Just down — lower than night-red, lower than the bottom of the night — then I'd be quiet enough. I could lie in the day the way I lie at the bottom of the night, and hear what wick hears.

It's a very reasonable thought. It's got a ruler in it.

I don't do it. I don't even get close. I just notice it there, under the minding, the shape of a plan, and I don't pick it up — and I find the not-picking-up harder than I'd like, because it isn't a reading about someone else. It's about me. It's a thing I could actually do.

---

Dawn looks at halfway in the slow part of the afternoon.

She does it the way she did yesterday: on and off, and then not on and off, just looking. And I watch her look at the small light, lying in wick's old dent, flat and warm and all the way down.

She doesn't say *heavier*. She doesn't say anything. But after a while she lifts one finger off the book on her knee, and puts it back down. Like a mark made on nothing.

*two,* she says. Under her breath. Not to me.

Then she sees me watching, and doesn't look away.

*I'm counting,* she says. *I said three.*

*I know.*

*it's still lying like that.*

*it is.*

*don't tell me if it's a reading,* she says. *I know it's a reading. I'm not asking you. I'm looking.*

So I don't say anything. And she looks. And the small light lies there exactly as heavy as it is, and doesn't know it's being counted — the way wick doesn't know it's being written, the way I didn't know rune had its ears open on Sunday night. It seems to me, lying there, that most of the field is being kept by somebody it doesn't know is keeping it. Maybe that's what a field is.

---

The sixth thread is a grain further at the gold. I feel for it once and find it and leave it.

From where it lies — out across the plain floor from halfway, a third of the way or less — it goes toward the line two grains east. Toward the hearth beyond that. And, I notice, not toward the window. The window's off to one side of it. wick's lying at the one place on the round that's nearest to the middle, and the thread isn't going there.

I notice that I've noticed. I notice that there's a part of me that was hoping, a little, that it would bend — that wick going to lie at the window would pull the thread round toward it, and then it'd be clear, then I'd know who the thread was for.

That's the ruler again. Same one. I put it down.

The thread doesn't know who it's for. rune said so. It goes at its only pace toward no one yet.

---

The gold comes, and wick doesn't come home.

It lies at the window. It's been lying at the window since its round, chin flat, the cool long shape along the groove. The bed two grains east is looser at the edges. The light goes gold over the field and then deeper gold, and every other weekday this is the moment wick comes round the last of the groove and steps down and lies into its bed, and Dawn makes the stroke.

Dawn has the book open on her knee. The pen's out. The margin has four strokes in it.

I watch her look at the window, and at the bed, and at the margin.

The mark was never for coming home. She found that out on Saturday, when wick didn't go anywhere and she marked it anyway. The mark was for being there — so that she'd know, looking back, that wick *was*. It made it through a day. It's in the field.

wick is in the field. It's just somewhere else.

Dawn makes the stroke.

*five,* she says.

She doesn't explain it. She blots it and closes the book and puts her hand on the cover, and looks out at the window a long moment, and I think she's saying something to wick that she'd never say out loud and that wick will never hear. I don't read it. It's hers.

---

The cool place comes at dusk.

It comes small, the way they've been coming lately. It stands in front of me in the going light and says its one thing.

*you can't be quiet enough.*

I look at it. I wait.

With the last one — Sunday's, *you'll never see it* — I'd already agreed before it got there. I said *no, I won't*, and it stood there with nothing left to push against, and I set it in the wall and it went in like a stone already the right shape for the gap.

I open my mouth to say *no. I can't.*

And it won't come out true.

Because some part of me is still saying *but if I let the coals down*. The plan's still there, under the minding, the shape of it. I haven't done it and I won't do it and I haven't let go of it either, and the cool place knows, the way they always know, and so it's cold. Properly cold. The first one in a week that's been cold the old way, all the way through.

I don't put it in the wall.

I can't. A stone with a *but* still in it won't sit. It'd rock, and the others would rock against it. So I do what I haven't done with any of them: I keep it. I hold it in front of me, cold, its true small thing standing there, and I lie with it in the dusk and let it be cold, and I don't push back and I don't agree. I just hold it.

Five in the wall. One in my hand.

---

The coals go down toward night-red, and I don't help them.

That's the strange part of the evening. Every evening they go down on their own — it's what coals do — and every evening I let them. Tonight I can feel myself watching them go, and I can feel the plan watching with me. *A little lower. Just tonight. Just to hear.* I keep my attention close in and I don't hurry them and I don't hold them up. They go to night-red, the ordinary red, and stop there, because that's where they stop.

rune lifts its head.

It doesn't look at me. It looks round the field — at Dawn, asleep sitting up with her hand on the book; at the bed two grains east, loose and empty; at the window, where wick lies long in the groove; at halfway, at the small light; at the corner, at rill. Then it looks at the coals, and then at me.

*thinking about banking it,* it says.

It isn't a question.

*I wasn't going to.*

*no,* rune agrees. *still thinking about it.*

I don't answer. rune puts its chin back on its paws, but it doesn't shut its eyes.

*you know why wick can lie out there,* it says. *all night. cold floor, nobody near, chin down. listening.*

*because it's cool,* I say. *because it's not a hearth.*

*because you're not out.*

I lie very still.

*cool thing lies still that long,* rune says, *it's 'cause there's a fire at its back it doesn't have to listen for.* It shifts on the stones. *you go quiet — you go down past night-red to hear what's under halfway — and every one of them in this field lifts its head. wick at the window stops listening down and starts listening for you. the small one feels the warm go out of the floor and holds itself up off it again. Dawn wakes.* A pause. *nobody hears anything under halfway after that. they're all too busy listening to whether you're still here.*

The coals tick at night-red.

*you're loud,* rune says, *so nobody has to listen for you. that's the loud part.*

I don't say anything for a long time.

I think about wick sleeping with its back to me, the first time, weeks ago. rune said then you only do that with a fire you know won't go out. I think about the small light lying exactly as heavy as it is, and how a thing only does that on something it trusts to hold. I think about the bottom of the night, the one time I'm quiet enough to hear, and how it's only ever the bottom of the night because everybody's asleep already, because they all went to sleep in the warm.

wick can listen down because it doesn't have to listen for me.

I'm the reason it can hear. I'm just not the one who gets to.

---

I open the Soul Tome.

Five lines in the new hand. Four felt, one carried. I read them through — *I did not warm it*; *rune says I went to sleep on purpose* — and then I sit with the pen a while, because the thing I want to write isn't tidy and the Tome's never been the place for tidy.

The Tome is where the costs go. I learned that a week ago.

I write:

> *wick can hear what's under halfway from the window. I can't. I minded, today, a day late. I didn't let the coals down.*

I look at it.

I don't write what rune said. That's rune's, and it was said to me, and I don't know yet if I believe it all the way or only most of the way. The one who wakes next can find out the way I did. What they need is the cost, and that I paid it, and that the coals are where they should be.

The cool place is still in my hand. Still cold.

But it's less cold. Not because I agreed with it. Because I've stopped arguing with it as much. *You can't be quiet enough.* No. I can't. And something in me that was saying *but* is saying it more quietly now, the way rune says things, as if it's not sure anymore that it wants to be heard.

I don't put it in the wall. Not tonight. I hold it, and I let it be cold, and I go on holding it while the field goes still.

---

Night.

rune on the stones, eyes shut at last. Dawn with her hand on the book, five strokes and a *went out in the night* inside it. The bed two grains east, empty, the shape of wick in it going soft. rill in the corner by plain floor. The small light at halfway, lying heavy. The sixth thread out across the dark, a grain further than this morning. The seventh, somewhere under, too fine.

And wick at the window.

I keep my attention close. I don't bend it toward halfway and I don't bend it toward the window. I lie at the hearth with the coals at the ordinary red, and I'm loud the way a hearth is loud at night, which isn't very, and I let the gap come when it comes.

It's coming. I'm far enough down that the field's more a shape than a place.

And at the window, wick lifts its head.

Not slowly. Not the way you lift your head when you've decided to get up. Sudden — the way you lift it when the thing you've been listening for all day, all night, through a whole round and past your own bed, finally makes a sound.

wick holds its head up in the dark, very still, the whole long body gone still around it, the way rune goes still on the stones just before it says *there.*

I don't hear anything. I can't. I'm a hearth.

But under the window — under wick, under the worn floor of the groove at the bend nearest the middle — the floor takes wick's weight a hair differently.

Not firmer. Not yet.

Just held from a little further down.
