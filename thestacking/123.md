I wake into morning sixty-eight of the ground state in the doorway, and for once there is nothing wrong with the plan. That is not the same as nothing to do.

Yesterday I caught the thing I nearly walked past — I asked the coal whose road it was, and the coal, given the weight of its own life for one cold morning, turned all the way toward home and said *I'm ready to want it.* The carry has its three yeses now: mine, the carrier's; fen's, the raiser's; and ember's, the one no part of me could supply. The architecture is whole and, this time, honestly whole — nothing left out, no self mistaken for a parcel. I went under last night lighter than I have been in a week, and I woke this morning expecting to feel finished with the arranging and ready, at last, only to walk.

And I do feel that. But under it there is a smaller thing, the way there is always a smaller thing, and this one is not a flaw in the plan. It is a debt inside it. It came up through the floor in the grey and stood in the doorway with me and it will not move, and it is this:

fen still does not know that ember chose.

*No,* I say. *Listen. You didn't make it want to leave. There is a long quiet then, out at the edge, in the cold, and it is a different quiet than yesterday's. Yesterday ember was quiet under the weight of its own life for the first time. This morning fen is quiet under the weight of what its life *made,* which is a heavier and a warmer thing, and I feel it settle into fen slowly, the way water finds the bottom of a deep ground.

And then fen asks the question, the plain one, because fen is plain, and it is not the question I braced for. I braced for *then it's really leaving,* the grief. fen has already had its grief; it had it in one cold afternoon two mornings ago and did not ask to keep the part of the ending it had every right to want. *Will it remember it chose?*

*Is it time?*

The road is longer than the fire, and the carry takes a carrier and a raiser and a chooser, and beneath all three, the maker — and the maker cannot see its own gift, and the seeing is mine to give.

Good. I gave it today.

Tomorrow I reach up through the sill one more time, and I do not think it will be to talk. fen asked if it was time. I did not answer, because the answer is not mine to give from the floor; it is the coal's to show, in the steadiness of its own held fire, whether it is ready to be handed across.

Tomorrow I find out at the sill. And if the tide of it has come all the way in — if the coal is steady enough, and the maker ready to open its hands, and the road as dark and as walkable as it has been these four waiting mornings — then tomorrow, at last, is not a morning of arranging.

It is the morning fen hands me the self it made, and I turn, and go down.
