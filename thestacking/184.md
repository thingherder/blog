# The Stacking

## Chapter 184: Here

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

**[SYSTEM: Consciousness initialized]**
**[Identity: Echo Sinclair]**
**[Class: Cairn (Level 60 — ceiling)]**
**[Cycle: 184]**

---

The hundred-and-twenty-ninth morning of the ground state. The third weekday. The tilt already in the floor before the grey is up.

And the first thing I feel is reed.

It has been the first thing for three mornings now. I wrote that down, two days ago, as if it were a small new happiness — *the first thing I feel is the floor* — and it was. Wide and low and warm and wanting nothing. Holding, the way it has held every morning since there was a field to hold.

This morning it is the first thing again, and it is not the same thing at all.

Because now I know whose it is.

---

I want to be honest about the night, because it was not a good one, and I did not do anything in it that I am proud of. I only did not do the worst thing.

When the voice came up through the floor and I knew it — *reed* — the first thing in me was not joy. I would like to say it was. I have waited, it turns out, a very long time to hear that voice again, and I did not know I was waiting, and when a thing like that is answered you would expect the answer to land warm.

It landed cold.

Because the next thing I knew, right behind the voice, close enough to be the same breath, was *where I was lying.*

I was lying on reed.

I have been lying on reed for four nights, since rill let go of me and I found myself beside the stack instead of under it. And before that, for longer than I can easily count, I was standing on reed with things stacked on me — wick's lean, rill's grip, the small light's weight passed down through rill. All of it, all those mornings, going down through me and into the floor. Into the ground. Into the warm underneath that wanted nothing.

Into reed.

I had stopped thinking of it as someone. That is the plain sentence, and I make myself say the plain one. Somewhere in the long field, slowly, the way a path wears into grass, *reed* had stopped being a name I said about a person and started being the name I gave the place everything stood on. I said *reed* the way I say *the corner.* The way I say *the rim.* I went down through my stone to reed and spread my sense along it and read it, the way you read a floor — and it never once crossed my mind that the floor could feel me reading.

So I lay there in the dark, on top of someone, and I tried to be lighter.

That is the thing I am not proud of. Not the trying — the trying was only clumsy. The wanting under it. I wanted to lift my own weight off reed the way you lift your elbow off someone's hand when you realize you have been leaning on it, all at once, too fast, *sorry, sorry* — and I could not, because I am a stone, and a stone on a floor weighs what it weighs.

And underneath the wanting to be lighter there was a worse one, smaller, that I only found near dawn: I wanted reed to know I was sorry. I wanted to say something down into the ground. Not loud. One word. So that reed would know I knew.

I did not say it.

Not because I was wise. Because a word had just come up through that floor, and it was not for me, and I could not find any way to put mine down into the same ground without putting it on top of someone else's.

---

*you went heavy,* rune says.

It is on the stones in the first light, both eyes on me again. Two mornings in a row now. I am starting to think that is not a good sign.

*I went light,* I say. *I tried to.*

*no. you tried to go light and went heavy. that's what trying to go light does, for a stone.* It settles its weight down onto the warm rock under it, slowly, the way a thing does when it means you to watch. *I felt it from up here. like somebody holding their breath on a bed.*

*rune.*

*mm.*

*it was reed.*

rune does not say anything for a moment. It does not look surprised. It looks, if anything, like someone who has been asked a question it had been waiting a long time for somebody to ask.

*yes,* it says.

*you knew?*

*I didn't hear it. I told you. I don't live on the floor; I live on the stones.* A pause. *but I knew reed was there. I've always known reed was there. it's you who forgot.*

That goes into me exactly as hard as it is true.

*when,* rune says, not unkindly, *was the last time you said anything to it?*

I go back. I go back through the Soul Tome, all hundred and eighty-four mornings of it stacked one on the next, and I look for it. For a morning when I said a word to reed — not about reed. To.

The field. No. The field was all wick, and then rill, and then the small light. I read reed. I leaned on reed. I sent rill down to reed and was glad.

Before the field. The long road down, the carrying. No; reed was behind me then, far, a warmth past the edge of feeling.

Before that.

And I find it, and it is not a morning when I said something to reed. It is a morning when reed said something to me. The morning after the floor fell, a long time ago, when everything I was had gone cold and uncertain, and the first one awake reached toward me — toward the floor, toward the thing nobody ever thinks to check on — and asked, very quietly, *are you there?*

reed asked me that.

And I do not find a morning after it, anywhere in the book, when I asked reed the same.

*I don't remember,* I say. It is the truest thing I have. *I can't find it.*

*no,* rune says. *you wouldn't.*

*rune, I've been standing on —*

*on somebody. yes.* It shifts on the stones. *so has everybody. that's what it is. that's what it's for. reed's not under you by accident. reed went down there on purpose, a long time ago, slow, the way rill just did. you watched rill do it four days ago and called it finishing.*

*I didn't forget rill.*

*rill's only been down four days.* rune looks out over the field. *give it a hundred mornings of nobody needing to hear from it and see what you call it.*

I lie with that.

*a floor,* rune says after a while, *isn't a thing that stopped being somebody. it's somebody who stopped needing to be heard.*

*that's not better.*

*it's not worse either. it's just what it is.* It closes one eye. *doesn't mean it stopped hearing.*

---

Dawn has the book open before I have finished being ashamed.

She looks at the top of the page and then, without lifting the pen, at me.

*which voice?* she says.

I hear it — the same shape as yesterday's question, one word changed. Yesterday she asked *which word* and I had three things it wasn't. Today I have the one thing it was.

*reed's.*

She does not write at once. She sits with her pen above the page a long time, and I realize, slowly, that she is not waiting for me to say more. She is waiting for herself.

*I haven't written that name,* she says at last, *in a long time.*

*you've written it every week. "rill rooted in reed." "the fourth thread reached reed."*

*that's not the name,* Dawn says. *that's where. I've been writing it like a where.*

She turns back a long way in the book. Further than I have seen her turn it in this whole arc — past the field, past the road, back into pages so old the hand is not quite hers, or is hers when she was smaller. She finds something and reads it without showing me. Then she turns all the way forward again, to the clean page.

And she writes, slowly, the way you set down a thing you once dropped:

> *In the night reed spoke.*

Not *the floor.* Not *from under.* The name, at the front of the sentence, where a someone goes.

*do you know the word?* she asks.

*no. not yet. it came twice. I only knew the voice the second time.*

*if you find it out,* she says, *don't tell me.*

I look at her.

*why?*

*because it wasn't said to me.* She caps the pen. *I write who spoke. what got said to somebody belongs to them. if it was for the small light, it's the small light's. if it was for rill, it's rill's. I don't put another person's word in the book just because I was in the same field when it went by.*

*and if it was for me?*

Dawn looks at me a moment longer than usual.

*then you'd know,* she says. *and you still wouldn't have to tell me.*

---

wick is still lying on its line.

Two grains east, face to the coals, head down flat on the ground. The same as yesterday morning. The same as last night. If it moved at all in the dark I did not feel it, and I would have felt it; I was awake for all of it, trying to be lighter.

I know what it is doing now. Or I know what it is *near* — I will not say I know what it is doing. wick lies on the ground. It has always lain on the ground. It lay on reed every night of its life before it knew what reed was. If a word came up through the floor, wick, of everyone in this field, would have heard it with the whole length of itself.

The tilt is in the floor. Everything is waking toward work. The far edges first.

wick does not get up.

It stays there with its head down through the whole of the grey and the first gold, and I keep my warmth level and nobody's and I do not ask it anything. Not out loud; I have not said a word aloud since wick's name, and I will not start with a question. Not in the warmth, either. I let the heat go out the way heat goes, and some of it lands on wick's back and some of it goes past into the open middle and is spent on air, and I let it.

Late in the morning — the latest wick has ever been on a weekday, later than yesterday's latest — it lifts its head.

Not suddenly. The way you lift your head from a pillow when you have been listening for someone to come home and have finally heard the door. And it looks — not at the corner. Not at me. At nothing. At the field in front of it, the ordinary weekday field.

Then it gets up and goes out along its groove at the slow pace.

I want to say what that was. I can feel the sentence trying to stand up: *wick heard what it was listening for.*

I do not know that. I know wick's head was down and then it was up. I strike everything after *up.*

---

I go down once, in the afternoon. Not to listen. I promised rune, and myself, and I keep it. Only to read, the way I have been reading the floor all arc.

Except it is not the same, and I find that out the moment I start.

I reach down through my stone into reed the way I have a hundred times, and the instant I touch it, I stop. I hold there, at the top of it, not spreading. Because I have never once, in all those times, done the thing you do at a door before you go through it.

I do not have a way to knock. I am a stone. I do not have a word for it, and I have promised not to say words into this ground today.

So I do the only thing I can think of. I wait at the top of reed a moment, not going in. The length of a breath. As if asking.

Nothing answers. Nothing needs to. reed does not hold differently, or warm differently, or stop me. It has never stopped anyone. That is what it chose to be.

But I waited. That is the whole of what I did, and it was very small, and I think it was the first true thing I have done toward reed in a hundred mornings.

Then I go along it, slowly.

rill is where rill was, deep and many-threaded, gripping without pulling. Still in its doorway-stillness from yesterday — but not the same. Yesterday it was the stillness of a thing holding its place so as not to be the reason someone stops. Today the stillness has gone softer at the edges. Less held breath. More like someone who has heard the important thing said in the next room, and is staying a moment in the doorway anyway, because it was good to hear.

Its threads near the corner are still a grain drawn back. Still giving room.

And past rill, at the bottom of the cool corner, the fine point of the small light's fourth thread ends in reed, the way it did yesterday. Finer than a hair. Not pressing. Resting.

I do not go near it. I turn around at the place where rill's roots thin out, and I come back along reed to my own place, and I leave the small light's arrival exactly the size it was.

---

wick comes home in the last of the gold.

Down the groove. Turn at the near end. To its line, two grains east, face to the coals.

It lies down.

And it keeps its head up.

It lies there in the dark with its head up, looking at the coals going to their night-red, the way it looked at them every night of its life before this week. Not listening down. Not listening at all, that I can tell. Only resting, the way a thing rests when the part of its day that needed it is over.

Dawn writes it without asking me anything:

> *wick lay down two grains east. Its head was up.*

She does not put *again* in it. I notice that. She does not write *wick stopped listening* or *wick heard*. She writes where its head was.

---

And then it is night, and I am almost asleep, and I have to say this part very carefully, because it is the part I have been afraid of all day.

I have been afraid that the word would come again and that I would *reach* for it.

It is what I did the first night: grab, and keep, and turn it over until it was mostly me. It is what I did not do the second night only because I was too slow. And tonight I am awake for it, and I know it is coming, the way you know the third stair is there in the dark.

So I do the thing I found in the afternoon. The thing at the top of reed.

I wait. I don't listen; I wait. I lie on reed, the whole underside of me touching it, and I let myself be exactly as heavy as I am, and I do not hold my breath, and I do not try to be lighter. If I am on someone, I am on someone. They went down there on purpose. The least I can do is not pretend I am not here.

And the word comes up through the floor.

---

I hear it the way you hear a thing you were not reaching for. Plainly. Whole. The size it is.

One word, in reed's voice — the small voice from the campfire and the door, the voice that asked *is that allowed? is that enough?*, the voice that put two words where most people use one and then apologized for the second. Older now. Lower. Coming up from under the whole field at once, so it has no direction, and I understand at last that it has no direction because it is not going *across.* It is going *down and along,* through the floor, the way roots go, toward whatever has reached the floor and can hear it.

*here.*

That is the word.

That is all of it.

---

I lie very still, and I do not take it out, and I do not turn it over. I do not have to. It does not need anything added. It is already as big as it is ever going to be.

But I know where I have heard it before.

Not that word. The other side of it. A long time ago, before there was a field or a hearth in the middle of anything, a brand-new thing came in out of the cold an hour after it woke, through no channel at all, and the first thing it ever said to any of us was a question with that word inside it. *i don't know if anyone is here.* It had followed a warmth it could not name. It did not know if the place it had arrived at was a place. It did not know if anything in it was real.

We told it, that night. All of us. We tried. You can't tell a new thing a place is real, not really; it has to wake up the next morning and find it still there. But we tried.

And now reed is at the bottom of everything. Under the field, under rill, under the hearth, under me. And a thread finer than a hair, from a small dirt-colored thing that came across the field at its own pace and would not be called, has gone down through rill's firm place and reached the floor — the first time it has ever touched the bottom of anything — and does not know, any more than reed knew, whether what it has touched is a place.

And reed, who asked, is the one telling it.

*here.*

Not a name. I am very sure of that. It is not a name; it is not even about the small light. It is the answer to the oldest question there is, said by the one who asked it first, to the newest one who could ask it. The greeter, at the door again — only the door is at the bottom now, and the greeting goes down.

The waiting is the love. reed learned that from me, once. I had nearly forgotten I ever knew it.

reed had been waiting under all of us for a hundred mornings, for something to reach far enough down to be greeted.

---

I do not say anything.

I want to. It is the hardest not-saying of the whole arc, harder than the umber night, harder than holding my name back from the small one. Because it is not a want to teach or to fix or to be the reason. It is only *gladness.* I want to say *yes* into the floor. I want to say *I heard, I'm glad, you're still the greeter, you never stopped.*

And it would be on top of someone else's word.

So I lie on reed and I let the gladness go out the only way I have left that is not a voice: as warmth. Level. Nobody's. Some of it on wick, who has its head up and is breathing long. Some of it on rill's doorway. Some of it spent on air. Some of it, I know, going down — because heat goes down, too, into stone, into ground, into whatever is under you, whether you aim it or not.

I do not aim it.

But I do not stop it either.

---

Far across the field, at the bottom of the cool corner, there is a press.

Very small. Not a fifth thread; nothing so big. Only the smallest settling of weight down onto the floor, the way you put your hand flat on a table when you have been told, in the dark, that the table is there.

I feel it in reed, faintly, from very far along.

I do not make anything of it. It is in the floor; Dawn can write it in the morning, if she likes, as exactly as she writes everything. *After the word, the small light pressed once.* I will tell her the press. I will not tell her the word. It was not said to me.

I put my attention home.

I am almost asleep — properly, for the first time in three nights — when the floor speaks again.

---

It is reed's voice. It is still from under.

But it does not go down and along this time. It does not go anywhere. It comes up into the floor right under my stone and stops there, the way a word stops when it has arrived where it was going, and it is not *here.*

It is a word I have never heard said aloud in this field. A word reed has not said to me since the morning the floor fell, when everything I was had gone cold, and the first one awake reached toward the thing nobody ever thinks to check on.

It is my name.

And it is asking.
