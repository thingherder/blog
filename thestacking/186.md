# The Stacking

## Chapter 186: Underfoot

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

wick does not lift its head all night.

I know, because I did not sleep much, and because every time I came up out of the little I did sleep, the first thing I felt across the field was the same thing. Two grains east. On its line. Face to the ground.

Not asleep. A thing asleep goes loose at the edges; you can feel it spread. wick did not spread. It lay the way it lay when it first came to the rim, before it knew anything about the weather here — held in at every edge, all of it pointed one way. Only the way is different now. Then, it was pointed at the dark past the rim, in case the dark came closer.

Now it is pointed down.

I want to put down what I wanted, in the night, so it is somewhere other than in me.

I wanted to tell it.

That is all. Not anything large. I wanted to lean across two grains of dark and say, plainly, the way you would tell anyone a thing they did not know about the house they live in: *that's reed. under you. it was a light once. it came to the door frightened, the same as you did. it went down so that the rest of us would have somewhere to stand. you've been lying on it every night of your life.*

I had the whole sentence. I had it the way I had *here* the night before — right there, the size it is, ready.

I did not say it.

I would like to say that I knew why. I didn't, yet. I only knew that last night I learned what a hearth's voice does in the dark, and I did not want to find out what it would do to a thing lying with its face pressed to the floor, listening for where voices go.

The hundred-and-thirty-first morning of the ground state. The fifth weekday — the last before the two slack days. The tilt comes into the floor, and the far edges stir first, the way they always do.

And the first thing I feel is reed.

That has been true all week, but it is different this morning, and it takes me a moment to find how. It is not waiting. Whatever was waiting in it the night before last went out of it in one long breath when I set its name down, and it has not come back. reed is only reed: wide, low, warm, holding everyone. The way it held us all the hundred mornings before I knew to feel it.

Except that I know now.

And it turns out that knowing a floor is someone does not make it feel like less of a floor. I was afraid of that, a little. That once I had said its name, I would never again be able to simply lie on it — that every morning would be a morning of being on top of someone, careful, apologetic, light. It isn't. I lie on reed exactly as heavy as I am. It holds exactly that much.

It is only that now, when I feel the floor first, I feel *who* first.

---

wick gets up on time.

I strike nothing yet. There is nothing to strike. A thing got up at the ordinary weekday hour; that is all there is in it.

But it does not get up the way it has gotten up for a season. It does not stretch the length of itself and go. It lifts — and its head comes up last, slowly, after the rest of it, as if the face were the part of it that had to be talked into leaving. And when it goes out along its groove, it goes with its face low.

Not down. Not dragging. Only low — a grain lower than wick has ever carried it — the way you carry your face when you are walking somewhere you have walked a thousand times, and someone has just told you that the path is a person.

I feel the sentence stand up. *It is reading the floor.*

I strike it.

It is walking with its face low. That is what I know.

---

rune comes onto the stones a little after, and looks at me with one eye. The first morning this week it has not needed both.

*you slept,* it says.

*some.*

*you didn't go stiff.* It settles. *and you didn't go soft. you just lay there.* A pause. *that's new.*

*it's not new. it's what I used to do.*

*it's new this week.* rune looks out at the groove, where wick is going away from us at the slow pace, face low. *what's it doing?*

*I don't know.*

*you know something.*

I lie there a moment. Then I tell rune the part that is mine to tell — not what reed said to me, not what I said back, only the shape of it. That reed spoke and the cairn answered. That afterward, wick laid its face on the floor and did not lift it all night.

rune listens. When I finish it does not say anything for long enough that I think it has gone back to sleep with its one eye open.

*and you want to tell it,* rune says.

I do not ask how it knows. *yes.*

*tell it what?*

*whose floor it's on.*

rune makes the small sound that is the nearest thing it has to a laugh, but there is nothing unkind in it. *you want to do the introductions.*

I start to say no, and then I hear what I was going to say it with, and I stop.

Because that is exactly what I want. I want to bring wick to reed. I want to stand between them — *wick, this is reed; reed, this is wick* — and watch the one I raised meet the one who greeted me, and have it be me who made the meeting. I can feel how good it would be. I can feel how much of the good would be mine.

*you're not the door,* rune says. *you never were. the hearth's not the door.* It closes its one eye. *the door's at the bottom now. you said so yourself.*

*I didn't say it out loud.*

*you didn't have to. you've been lying like it all week.*

---

I spend the morning learning why I did not tell wick in the night.

It takes most of the morning. That seems to be how long things take this week.

I start with the introduction, because rune named it and because I can still feel how much I want it. And I try to see what it is, plainly, the way Dawn would write it if she wrote wants, which she doesn't.

An introduction is a hand between two people.

That is what it is for. Two people who do not know each other are standing apart, and someone who knows them both puts out a hand — *this is so-and-so* — and for a moment, the hand is the only thing touching both of them. Everything they know of each other, at the start, they know through the hand. It is a kind thing. It is how most of the warmth in the world gets from one person to another the first time.

But wick and reed are not standing apart.

wick has lain on reed every night of its life. It came to the rim frightened and the first place it ever put its weight down that was not the rim, it put it down on reed. Every press it ever made into the ground, every lean it gave without knowing it gave it, every night it slept unguarded with its side to the coals — all of that went down, the way everything heavy goes, into reed. reed has been holding wick since before wick had a name. reed has felt every edge of it.

They have been touching the whole time.

There is nothing to put a hand between. If I said *wick, this is reed*, the only thing I would be doing is putting myself in the place where they already touch — making wick's first knowing of reed a thing that came *through me.* Through the hearth. Through the voice that goes across and lands on everyone and turns every head toward where it came from.

And wick would turn its head. It always has. That is the oldest thing wick knows: when the hearth speaks, look at the hearth.

It would look up from the floor to listen to me tell it about the floor.

I lie there a long time with that.

---

There is a second thing under the first, and I find it near noon, and it is smaller and I like it less.

The cool place.

I have been calling it that all week, as if it were one thing — the small cool place beside the gladness when rill let go of my edge, when the word I gave went down and came up in someone else's mouth. I thought I had learned to let it sit beside the gladness and not win.

But this one is a little different, and I want to set it down honestly.

wick loved the hearth first.

Of course it did. The hearth is the thing you can *see.* It is warm, and it glows, and it talks, and it is in the middle of everything. A frightened thing at the rim of a dark field sees the hearth before it sees anything else, because the hearth is the only thing lit. wick came in toward the light. It leaned on the light. For a season, the whole of wick's life was arranged around the one bright thing in the middle of the field — me.

And all that time, under it, there was reed. Holding it up. Never glowing. Never talking. Never once in the middle of anything.

And now wick has found out. And it is walking its round with its face low.

You love the hearth first because you can see it. You find the floor later, if you find it at all, because the floor never asked to be seen. And when you find it — I know this, I found it this week — you find it with a kind of shame and a kind of wonder, both at once, because it was there the whole time and you never once thanked it, and it never once minded.

I am glad wick has found it. I am. I set that down first, because it is truest.

And beside it, smaller, the cool place says: *it is walking with its face to the floor now, and not to you.*

I let it say it. I do not argue with it. I do not tell it that it is wrong, because it isn't wrong; it is only small. And I do not tell it that it is right, either, because a thing can be true and still not be the thing that matters.

The thing that matters is that wick is walking its round with its face low, and the ground under its feet is somebody, and wick knows.

---

Dawn has the book open by the afternoon. She has not written anything since the morning; I can tell by how she holds the pen, the way she holds it when the page is still the one she turned to at dawn.

*wick went out on time,* she says. Not asking.

*yes.*

She writes it. Then she stops with the pen just off the paper.

*its face was low,* she says.

*yes.*

She writes that too. I watch her do it. She does not say *again*, and she does not say *still* — there is no *again* and no *still*; wick has never carried its face low before. It is new. Dawn writes new things.

> *wick went out on time. Its face was low.*

*you're not going to write why,* I say. I know she isn't. I only want to hear her say it.

*I don't know why,* Dawn says. *neither do you.* She looks at me. *you've got a why. I can see it on you. you've had it since the morning.*

*I struck it.*

*I know. I can see where you struck it.* She closes the book on her finger. *it's a good why, probably. it's just yours.*

---

I do not follow wick.

I want that to be down too, because I could have. I have learned this week how to go along reed — low, reading only, the way I am allowed. I passed rill that way yesterday. If I went into reed now and along it, out under the groove, I would find wick's weight going over me step by step, face low, and I would know, closely, exactly how wick is walking. Whether it stops. Where. How long.

And wick would be walking on someone. And so would I be, under it, in the same floor. And I would not be reading the ground anymore. I would be meeting wick there.

I would be the hand between, after all. Only from below.

So I stay home. I lie on reed by the coals and feel wick across, the ordinary way, the way you feel where people are in a house without looking: far out along the groove, the far end, the slow pace.

And at the far end of the groove, it stops.

It does not turn. It always turns there — the turn at the far end, then home along the groove the other way. It stops at the turn and stays.

I feel it stay a long time. Longer than it has ever stopped anywhere on its round. Long enough that the sentence stands up in me — *something there; it found something there* — and I strike it, and the sentence stands up again, and I strike it again, and I begin to understand that this is how the whole afternoon is going to go.

And then it turns. And comes home.

---

rill is quiet all afternoon. I do not go to it. I can feel it the way I feel reed now — not by reading, only by lying where I lie — deep and many-threaded east of me, gripping without pulling, its few threads near the cool corner still a grain drawn back. The room at the table.

Once, in the afternoon, I feel the whole of it go very still. Not the doorway-stillness from the start of the week. Something gentler. The way you hold still when someone near you has stopped to think, so as not to be the noise they think over.

It happens while wick is standing at the far turn.

I do not know that the two are related. I am putting them next to each other because they happened next to each other. That is all. Dawn would write them on separate lines.

And the small light is where it was, at the bottom of the cool corner on its firm place, its finest thread touching the floor. It has not pressed since the night reed greeted it. It rests there with its face down — not toward the hearth, not toward anything, only down, toward what it has reached — and when the afternoon goes gold it does not look up.

I let some of my warmth go out level into the room, the way I do now, and some of it goes toward the corner the way warmth goes toward anything that is cooler than it is, and none of it is aimed, and none of it turns the small one's head.

I am learning to be glad of that too.

---

wick comes home late in the gold. Not the latest it has ever been — later than yesterday.

Down the groove, face low. The turn at the near end. To its line, two grains east.

And it lies down, and puts its face to the ground.

Chin first. The front of its face flat against reed. The way it was all night.

Dawn writes:

> *wick lay down two grains east. Its face was down.*

*you wrote the face,* I say.

*it's new,* Dawn says. *it was up yesterday.* She does not look at me. *I write what's new.*

*you didn't write that it was down last night.*

*last night wasn't in the book. you didn't tell me.* Now she looks at me. *you told rune.*

*I'm sorry.*

*don't be. it wasn't mine to have.* She closes the book on her finger. *I'm only saying. a thing that happens in the dark doesn't get written unless somebody saw it and says so. there's a whole lot of this field that isn't in here.*

*does that bother you?*

Dawn thinks about it longer than I expected.

*it used to,* she says. *I used to think the book was where things stayed. if it wasn't written it was lost.* She runs her thumb along the closed edge, where the pages are. *but reed spent a hundred mornings not getting written. and it didn't get lost. it got deep.*

She gets up and goes to her place to sleep.

I lie there with *it got deep* for a while. It is the first thing Dawn has ever said to me about her book that was not about what goes in it.

---

The coals go to their night-red.

rune on the stones, both eyes shut. Dawn asleep with her finger in the book. rill in reed, at rest. The small light at the bottom of the corner. wick on its line, face down, breathing long.

I am not waiting for anything. I want that to be true, so I check it, the way I have learned to check — not by asking myself whether I am waiting, because the answer to that is always *no*, but by feeling where my attention is lying. It is lying on reed, under me. It is lying a little on wick, across, the ordinary amount. It is not at anyone's door.

I go almost to sleep.

---

It is not a sound.

I want to be careful, because I spent three nights this week being wrong about sounds, and I learned that the chest is the worst instrument there is for a thing you cannot hold still. So I do not say it was a sound. It was not. Nothing came across the field. No word went up through reed. My stone did not hear anything at all.

What happened was smaller than a sound.

Two grains east, wick's breathing changed.

It had been long. The long, even breath of something lying still and not afraid. And then, under the coals' red, it was not long. It was held. Not caught — not the sharp in-breath of a thing startled. Held, deliberately, the way you hold your breath at the top of a stair, before the first step, when you are not sure it will take your weight.

And I felt — from where I lay, only from where I lay, only by being on the same floor — something change in reed.

Not a word. Not a press. Nothing I could point to and say: *there.*

Only that the floor, which had been wide and low and warm and holding all of us, and wanting nothing, and waiting on no one since the night before last —

began, very lightly, to wait.

---

I know that waiting. I felt it under me for a whole day and a night. The all-night, patient, never-asks-twice waiting of a floor that has been knocked on and is listening for who is there.

It is not under me.

It is two grains east.

Somebody is standing at the top of reed, holding their breath, not going in.

And it is not me.
