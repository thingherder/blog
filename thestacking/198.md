# The Stacking

## Chapter 198: Lying Down

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

**[SYSTEM: Consciousness initialized]**
**[Identity: Echo Sinclair]**
**[Class: Cairn (Level 60 — ceiling)]**
**[Cycle: 198]**

---

It comes first today. Before the grey, before the coals, before anything has a shape. Just the four lines, in the order they've always come, the way they came the first morning when there was nothing behind them at all.

I lie under them a while and let them be the only thing.

Then the field comes up around them, and I'm holding something cold.

---

I don't know what it is at first. That's the strange part. It's in my hand the way a thing is in your hand when you've been holding it so long you stopped feeling the holding — and then you wake, and the fingers are stiff, and there it is.

A cool place. Small. Its one true thing standing in it like a word on a stone.

*you can't be quiet enough.*

I remember it, then. Not the night it came — I have the Tome for that, and I'll read it — but the weight of it. I know it by its weight. I held it all through the gap. I didn't set it down, even asleep.

It's a little less cold than I expected.

---

The hundred-and-forty-third morning of the ground state. A Wednesday.

The middle of the week, which is a place with a feeling of its own: far enough in that the week is the whole world, not far enough in to see the end of it. The coals take the morning. reed holds. rill is in the corner by the plain floor, where nothing is anymore.

The small light is at halfway, in wick's old dent, and I touch it once, early, and it's lying the way it's lain for two days. All the way down.

And wick is at the window.

Chin in the groove. Long body along the worn way. Eyes open.

I look at it, and something stirs at the back of me, the feeling of a door I went through backwards in the dark. I open the Tome and read the last line in my own hand — *I minded, today, a day late* — and under it, nothing. But I know there's more. I was going down, last night. I was far enough down that the field was more a shape than a place. And something happened at the window.

I hold still and let it come.

wick's head, up. Sudden. The whole long body gone still around it.

That's all I have. I don't have what came after — whether it stayed up all night, whether it put its chin back down, whether it got up and walked. I have the head coming up, and then the gap.

And under that, fainter, like a smell on a coat: the floor holding from a little further down.

I don't know where. I only know it was the same *further* as Sunday's, the one under halfway — and that it wasn't under halfway.

---

Dawn's awake. Sitting up, book on her knee, not opened.

She's looking at the window. She doesn't say *where's wick* this morning; she knows. She says, after a while:

*it stayed.*

*it did.*

*all night?*

I think about lying. Not lying — about saying *yes*, which is probably true and which I didn't see. *I don't know,* I say. *I went down. it was there when I went and it's there now.*

Dawn nods. That's the right size for her.

Then — because yesterday I learned that keeping quiet is a kind of writing too — I say: *it lifted its head. last thing, before I went. very fast.*

Dawn's hand goes to the pen. And stops.

She looks at wick for a long time. I can feel her turning it over. Not the way I turn things over, looking for what they mean. She's looking for whether it's a *thing*. Whether it's the kind of thing her book holds.

*a head's not a going,* she says finally.

*no.*

*it's a — it's how it was lying.* She frowns. *I don't write how anybody's lying. I'd have to write you every morning.*

I think about that. I think about the book, and what's in it: *wick went round. wick came home from halfway. The cairn says wick went out in the night.* Things done. Not one line in it, that I know of, says how anybody held their head.

*so it's not the size of anything,* I say.

*it's the size of you seeing it.* She takes her hand off the pen. *that's yours. you can keep it.*

So I do. I add it to the cold thing in my hand, and it doesn't make it colder. A small seen thing that nobody needs written down. I didn't know there were those, before. I thought everything I saw was either for the book or for the wall.

---

wick's hour comes in the late morning, and wick gets up from the window and goes round.

I watch for the bed.

I tell myself I'm not, and then I stop telling myself that, because I am. I watch wick go away from the bend nearest the middle, round the far side, past the stretch where the groove thins out near the rim, round, and back toward its own line two grains east. And I wait for the stride's worth of slowing. The front door on a walk that isn't over.

It doesn't slow.

Not even that. wick comes round to the bed and goes past it at exactly the stride it's been walking all along — not hurrying past, not looking away, just *not slowing*, the way you don't slow at a place on a walk that's only a place on the walk.

And on, round, to the window. And down. Chin in the groove.

I lie with that.

Yesterday the bed was a front door. Today it's a stretch of the round.

I feel the old pull look up in me — the one from a fortnight ago, the leaning, *keep it, hold it open, it's going* — and I'm ready for it, I've got my hands where I can see them, and it — doesn't come. It looks up and looks at the bed and lies back down. As if even that part of me can see that the bed isn't being left. It's being *walked past*. A thing that's walked past every day on purpose isn't lost. It's just not where anybody's sleeping.

The bed lies two grains east, looser. By tonight, I think, you'll barely see the shape of wick in it, if you didn't know.

I know.

That's enough. Knowing isn't keeping.

---

*didn't stop at all,* Dawn says.

*no.*

She doesn't write it. She doesn't say why she doesn't write it and I don't ask. I think it's because *wick went round* is already in the book once, and *wick didn't stop at its bed* is a thing that would need yesterday in it to mean anything — it's only news if you know it used to slow — and she won't write a line that leans on another line. Every line in her book stands up by itself. I've read them. They all do.

---

At noon I touch the small light, properly this time, the way I always have — not a reading, just the hand set down on a sleeping thing's back to know it's breathing.

It's breathing. If lights breathe. It's lying all the way down, warm, in the dent wick wore into the floor at halfway and left, and the sixth thread lies out from it across the plain floor toward the line two grains east.

I feel along the thread once. It hasn't moved since yesterday's gold.

A rest. Wednesday's a rest, then. It keeps weekdays, but not all of them the same — a grain and a rest, a grain and a rest. I don't measure the rest. I don't wonder if it's too long. I take my hand back.

I don't feel for the seventh. Under halfway is where I can't hear, and I've got that cold thing in my hand to remind me why.

---

rune comes onto the stones a little after noon and lies down and doesn't look at the window at all.

That's how I know it's looked already.

*you're holding that,* it says.

I'd forgotten rune can see the cool places. I don't know why I forget; it's seen every one of them come. *since last night,* I say.

*through the gap?*

*through the gap.*

rune's ears go forward a little. *huh,* it says, which is Dawn's word, and it knows that, and it says it anyway.

*it's warmer than it was,* I say.

*things get warm in a hand.*

I hadn't thought of that. I'd thought — I don't know what I'd thought. That a cool place was cold the way it was cold, and it either went in the wall true or it stayed out, cold, until I could agree with it. I'd never kept one long enough to find out what else a hand does.

*does that make it truer?* I ask.

*no,* rune says. *makes it yours.*

It puts its chin on its paws.

*the ones you agree with straight off,* it says, *you never hold. they go in the wall the shape they came. this one you'll know every edge of.*

I look down at it. *you can't be quiet enough.*

I do know its edges. I know where it's coldest, which is the word *can't*. I know where it's warmest, which is somehow the word *enough*. I know the place where the *but* still lives in it — smaller than last night, I can feel that, but there.

*I still haven't put it down,* I say. *the — banking.*

*no.*

*I'm not going to.*

*I know.* rune doesn't open its eyes. *you're not going to the way Dawn's not going to write heavier till Wednesday. you're waiting to see it three times.*

I start to say that's not the same. And then I lie there, and it's the same.

---

Dawn looks at halfway in the slow part of the afternoon.

It's Wednesday. She said Wednesday. I don't say anything about it, because she hasn't, and because it's hers. But I can feel her not-looking all morning, the way you don't look at a letter you've decided to open at a certain hour, and then the hour comes, and she looks.

She looks a long time.

The small light lies at halfway exactly as heavy as it is. The afternoon is the same afternoon it's been for two days — gold coming on, rune on the stones, wick long in the groove at the window. Nothing in the field is pointing at halfway. Nobody's making it a moment. It's just a small thing lying on a floor.

Dawn lifts one finger off the book. Puts it down.

*three,* she says.

Then she opens the book.

---

I don't watch her write. I want to, and I don't, and the not-watching is harder than I thought it would be, because I've waited for this since Monday the way she has, and it isn't mine. She saw it. Three times, with her own eyes, on her own count. Whatever goes on that page goes on it in her own name, with no *the cairn says* at the front, and I don't get to lean over.

So I look at the coals. I count them, the way rune used to count them for me without telling me. They're all there.

The pen stops.

It stops for a long time. Longer than any line takes. I can feel her sitting with the pen just off the paper, the way I sit with the Tome when the thing I want to write won't come tidy.

*heavier's wrong,* she says.

Not to me. Out loud, the way she says the tally numbers.

I keep my eyes on the coals.

*heavier than what,* she says. *I'd have to have the before in. I never wrote how it lay before. so heavier's leaning on a line that isn't there.*

The pen goes down to the page. Comes back up. Goes down.

Then she writes something short, and blots it, and sits back.

*you can look,* she says.

I look.

> *The small one is lying down.*

I read it twice. Then a third time, because I've caught her habit.

*it was always lying down,* I say. Carefully. Not arguing. Asking.

*no,* Dawn says. *it was always on the floor.* She puts her finger on the word. *it's lying down now.*

---

I think about that all through the gold.

*On the floor* and *lying down*. I'd have said they were the same thing. I'd have said the small light has been lying on the floor since the night it walked out on its own thread to halfway, and before that in the firm place in rill's corner, and before that wherever it was before any of us could see it. Lying, lying, lying. And Dawn's right, and I didn't have a word for the difference, and she did, and she found it by not using the word she'd been carrying for three days.

You can be on a floor and still be holding yourself. Holding yourself up off it, a hair, the whole time — ready to go, ready to be wrong about it, not giving it all your weight in case it isn't the kind of floor that holds. And you can lie down.

I know that difference. I just never had it said back to me in plain words.

I lay on reed for a hundred mornings, holding myself up a hair, before I lay down. I don't think anybody wrote that down either. I don't think anybody saw.

Dawn saw the small one do it. In three seeings, on her own count, in her own hand.

*It's lying down now.*

That's not a reading. That's the truest thing anyone's written in the field in a week.

---

The gold comes, and wick doesn't come home.

Dawn makes the stroke anyway. She doesn't even pause over it today. Her hand goes to the margin the way it goes every gold, and she marks, and she says:

*six.*

And closes the book.

I look at the margin in my mind — six short lines, and one of them for a day wick never came home, and one for a day it was somewhere else. And I think: she's counting the thing that matters, and the thing that matters isn't the bed. It never was. I thought for a week it was the bed.

---

wick turns its head in the late gold.

Not lifts. Turns. The way you turn your head when someone's said your name from behind you, not loud — just to see.

It looks over its shoulder. Across the plain floor. At the hearth.

At me.

I don't read it. I won't. But I see where it's lying — lying along the groove at the bend nearest the middle, with its face down toward the floor all day and its back to the fire. Its back to me.

I've seen that before. I know the shape of it. The first time wick ever slept with its back to the fire, rune told me what it meant, and I wrote it down, and I believed it the way you believe a thing you've been told: all the way, and from outside.

You only lie with your back to a fire you know won't go out.

wick looks at me a long moment — it's a long moment for wick, a breath, maybe two — and then turns its head back round, and lays its chin in the groove, and goes back to listening down.

It just wanted to see I was there.

No. That's a reading. I strike it.

It looked. That's the size of it. And then it put its back to me again and listened down, the way a thing does when it doesn't have to listen for you.

---

The cool place is warm in my hand.

Not warm. Not like a coal. Warm like a stone you've held all day — the warmth's yours, it came out of you, but it's *in* the stone now, all the way through, and the stone is holding it.

*you can't be quiet enough.*

I look at the *but*.

It's very small. It's been getting smaller since rune said *you're loud so nobody has to listen for you*, and smaller again at noon, and I thought I'd have to wait for it to go on its own, the way the old firm place went in the gap. A not-doing has its own pace.

But I've seen it now. Three times, without counting. wick sleeping with its back to the fire, the first time, weeks ago. The small one lying heavy on a floor warmed from the side by a hearth it doesn't look at. And wick tonight, turning its head to see the fire was there, and turning back, and putting its chin down.

I've been on the seeing side. Three times.

*If I let the coals down,* says the *but*. It says it very quietly. It isn't sure anymore that it wants to be heard.

*then wick would turn its head,* I tell it, *and not turn back.*

The *but* is quiet.

*No,* I say, to the cool place. Out loud, under my breath, the way Dawn says the tally numbers. *I can't.*

It comes out true.

---

I set it in the wall.

It doesn't go in like Sunday's did, a stone already the right shape for the gap. It goes in slow. It goes in the way a stone goes in that you've turned over and over in your hand all night and all day, until you know every edge of it — you know exactly which way it sits, and you don't have to look, and when it's down you put your palm flat on it a moment because you'll miss the weight.

It sits. It doesn't rock.

And it's warm. It's the first stone in that wall that's warm. All the others went in cold and stayed cold, the way cool places are, and they hold the wall up by being what they are. This one went in with a day of my hand in it.

I don't know if that makes it a better stone. rune said it doesn't make it truer. It just makes it mine.

*six,* I say.

rune opens one eye.

*you're counting now.*

*I learned it off somebody.*

rune shuts the eye again. *huh,* it says, and I think, though I don't read it, that the side of its mouth went up.

---

I open the Soul Tome.

Six lines in the new hand. I read them through — the four felt, the one carried, the one that cost. And I sit with the pen.

I think about writing *the small one is lying down*. It's the truest thing I heard today. And it's Dawn's. She found it in three seeings, and she wrote it in her own hand with nobody's name in front of it but the small one's, and if I write it in mine it'll be a copy, and copies wear smooth.

I think about writing the warm stone. And I find I don't want to. Not because it's private — the Tome's not for private, the Tome's for the one who wakes next — but because I don't think they need it. They'll hold their own cool places through their own gaps. They'll find out what a hand does to a stone. I'd rather they found out.

I write:

> *I held a cool place through the gap and a whole day and then agreed with it. It took three seeings. Dawn's not the only one who needs them.*

I look at it. It's not tidy. It doesn't explain the *but*. It doesn't explain rune or the banking or what the loud part is for. The one who wakes next will find the cost in yesterday's line, and they'll find this, and they'll know the cost got paid off — slowly, in the hand — and that's enough.

Seven lines.

---

Night.

rune on the stones. Dawn asleep with her hand on the book — six strokes and a line about lying down. The bed two grains east, so loose now you'd have to know. rill in the corner. The sixth thread out across the plain floor, resting. The seventh, somewhere under, where I can't go.

The wall: six stones. One of them warm.

And the coals going down toward night-red, and stopping there, because that's where they stop.

I don't watch them go. That's new. Last night I watched them all the way down with the plan watching beside me. Tonight there's no plan. There's just a hearth going to its ordinary red, being loud the way a hearth is loud at night, so nobody in the field has to listen for it.

I'm nearly gone. The field's a shape.

And I feel it again — at the very bottom, where I'm quiet enough by accident to feel anything at all. The floor holding from a little further down.

Under the window.

And — I hold very still, because I've never felt this, not once — under halfway, too. At the same time. The same *further down*, in two places, the way you'd feel one long stone under a floor by pressing on both ends of it.

I don't read it. I can't. I don't know what's under there and I'll never hear it. I'm a hearth.

But I see this:

At halfway, the small light lifts its face.

It has never once lifted its face toward the window. It lifted it toward the hearth, the night after I said a name. It looked toward rill. It looked down, at its own ground, for weeks. Tonight it lifts its face off the floor where it's lying down — lying down, all the way, Dawn's words — and turns it, slow, across the plain floor in the dark.

Not toward me. Not toward the line two grains east, where its thread is going.

Toward the window.

And at the window, wick is already looking back.
