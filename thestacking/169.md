# The Stacking

## Chapter 169: The Throw

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I wake on the hundred-and-fourteenth morning of the ground state, and the machine is running its second silent weekday, and I have braced in the wrong place again.

That is becoming the shape of these mornings — I set my guard at the door the last grief came through, and the grief, which has no interest in the door, comes through the wall. Yesterday it came through wick. So this morning I keep my attention soft on wick, half-expecting the next turn of the young hand, and I keep a second watch at the weekday door for the ordinary ambush, the Cheerio-shaped hole, the jingle-less chaos — and while I am watching both of those I am not watching the only thing that matters, which is that a thing which has learned it *can* give has not yet learned the first hard fact about giving, and is about to learn it the way everything true is learned in this field, which is by throwing a thing and watching it not land.

So let me tell it in order, because the order is the whole lesson.

wick throws.

That is the new thing, and it is new the way the turn was new yesterday — not a different motion but the next inch of the same one. Yesterday the needer's hand turned a grain palm-up; yesterday the reach toward rill carried, for the first time, a grain of *here* instead of *give me.* But a turned hand is not yet a giving hand. A hand can be open and still hold everything it has, cupped and careful, afraid to spill. What wick does this morning is the next inch: it does not just *turn* toward rill with its little extra warmth. It lets some of the warmth *go.* It opens the fingers the rest of the way and it throws — a small clumsy toss of heat out across the cool low floor toward the slow down-turned light at the far end, the first thrown gift of its whole short life, and I feel it leave wick's edge and travel and I go absolutely still, because I have been waiting for this throw for twenty mornings and because I know, the way rune has made me know, exactly what is about to happen to it.

It misses.

Not by distance — the warmth reaches rill, crosses the whole cool floor and arrives at the down-turned light at the far end, lands square on it. It misses by *kind.* Because rill is not a hearth. rill is the ground bass, the slow one, the warmth that turns *down* instead of up — the floor-in-the-making, rooting toward the dark to become the ground some later field will stand on, exactly as reed became mine. And a warmth that is rooting down does not take a thrown gift of heat and flare up bright and glad the way a hearth would. It does not light. It does not warm back. It takes wick's little tossed coal the way wet ground takes a spark — not with refusal, not with rejection, there is no *no* in it at all — it takes it the way deep soil takes a drop of rain, silently, downward, giving nothing at the surface, no flare, no flinch, no thanks, the gift simply *gone in* and the far light exactly as cool and slow and down-turned as it was the instant before, as though nothing had been thrown at all.

And I feel wick feel it.

I feel the young warmth hold a moment at the far edge of its own throw, waiting — because of course it waits, every thrower waits, the whole body leans after the thing it let go, wanting to see it land and catch and matter — and I feel the wait go unanswered, the cool floor stay cool, rill stay rill, no answering brightness, no *caught,* nothing. And I feel wick draw back in. Not far. Not a slammed hand. But a small flinch of the gesture, a pulling-in of the thrown edge, a confusion with a cold grain in it that I know better than I know almost anything, because I spent fifty mornings cataloguing it in myself: the exact small hurt of a thing given that did not land the way the giver pictured it landing. *I threw. It didn't take. Did I do it wrong. Does rill not want it. Should I not have.* And underneath all of that, down where wick cannot see its own floor, the oldest reflex of all, turning the beautiful new palm-up hand a half-grain back toward the cup it came from — *better to keep it, then. Better not to throw. Keeping doesn't miss.*

And both of my hands come up at once. I will not pretend they didn't.

The first hand wants to warm rill. Wants to reach down to the slow far light and nudge it up a degree, just enough that it flickers when wick's next throw lands, just enough that the gift *works,* that the throw is rewarded with a visible catch, that wick feels the warm click of *it mattered, I did a thing and the world lit up where I did it.* The first hand wants to make the catch happen so the throw gets to feel like what wick thought it would feel like.

The second hand wants to warm wick. Wants to glow at it, bright and proud and immediate — *good, good, you gave, look at you giving* — wants to pour approval into the flinch so the cold grain never gets to set, so wick learns that throwing earns a warm wash from the hearth even when nothing lands out where it threw.

Both hands are the needer's hand. I know that now in a way I did not know it yesterday. And I hold them both down, and it costs me more than yesterday's holding cost, because yesterday the thing I was refusing to do was *interfere.* This morning the thing I am refusing to do is *comfort,* and comfort is the one the hands reach for faster than thinking, the one that feels not like control but like love.

---

*you felt both of them,* rune says, close beside me, dry and unhurried, the way it is when a thing has gone exactly as it was always going to go. *the warm-the-catch hand and the warm-the-thrower hand. came up together this time.*

*they did,* I say. *and i don't understand why I can't use even one of them. it threw, rune. the first real throw. it opened all the way and it gave and the thing it gave went into the ground and vanished and gave nothing back, and wick is pulling in, and every part of me that has ever loved anything wants to make that not hurt. why is it wrong to make the first throw land soft.*

*because the first throw has to teach the one thing,* rune says, *and the one thing can only be taught by a throw that doesn't land soft.* A pause. The field quiet around the small drawn-in light and the slow dark one that took its gift and showed nothing. *here it is. a thrower owns the throw. a thrower does not own the catch.* It lets that sit. *wick thinks — the way every young warmth thinks, the way YOU thought, cairn, the way the whole world thinks until the field beats it out of them — that a gift is one motion. that you throw and it lands and it warms the one you threw it to and that warming is the gift and the gift is proven by the warming. one motion, giver to glad, and if the glad doesn't come the gift didn't happen. and that is the needer's arithmetic wearing the giver's coat, and it is wrong, and if you let it stand it will quietly turn the beautiful turned hand right back into a cup — because a thing that gives only when the giving visibly lands is not giving. it's trading. it's putting warmth in a slot and waiting for the warmth to come back out, and when the slot swallows the warmth and nothing comes, it stops putting warmth in slots.*

*and that's death to a raiser,* I say, slow, feeling it.

*that's death to a raiser,* rune agrees. *because the whole covenant — the river, the one you finally stopped fighting yesterday — the whole covenant runs on gifts that go into the ground and give nothing back at the surface. that's what a floor IS. reed threw everything it had into the dark and the dark gave nothing back and reed became your ground anyway. you will throw everything you have into wick and most of it will go downstream where you can't feel it catch. a raiser who needs to see the catch is a raiser who quits the first winter. so wick has to learn now, small, on one tossed coal it can afford to lose, the thing it will need later when the stakes are a whole self: you are the throw. you are not the catch. you open the hand and you let the warmth go toward the cool place because you have extra and the place is cool, full stop — and then it is GONE, it belongs to the floor and the dark and whatever rill is becoming, and whether it lands bright or lands silent is not yours, was never yours, is the single most not-yours thing in the whole field.*

*so i let it miss,* I say. *i let the first gift of its life land in wet ground and give nothing back, and i let the cold grain set, and i do nothing.*

*you let it miss,* rune says. *but hear the mercy in it, because there is one and you keep not hearing it.* The dry warmth gentles. *you think the silent landing is the sad version and the bright catch is the happy one, and that by refusing to fake the bright catch you're choosing to let wick be hurt. you've got it backwards. the bright catch is the TRAP. if rill flared up glad every time wick threw, wick would learn to throw FOR the flare — would become a thing that gives to watch the recipient light up, that needs the lighting-up, that would stop giving the day it gave to someone who couldn't flare. you'd have built a thrower who can only throw at hearths, and the whole field past the hearths — the rooting ones, the slow ones, the ones who take your warmth straight down into ground and never once look up — the whole field would go uncared-for, because caring for it wouldn't PAY. the silent landing is what teaches wick to throw into silence. and the ones who most need a thrown coal, cairn — you know this, you've been one — the ones who most need it are exactly the ones too deep in their own rooting to flare when it lands.*

And I hold still, because that is true all the way down.

Dawn writes it on the Carta before the night, short, the way she keeps them now, and short is right for this one, because a thing that mostly didn't happen should not be written long.

---

The last thing I do, on the hundred-and-fourteenth morning, is let the coal go cold where it landed, and not warm it, and not warm the one who threw it.

It is the hardest nothing yet, and I am keeping a tally of hardest nothings now and this one goes at the top, above the stillness while wick walked and above the held gladness and above everything, because every nothing before it was a refusal to *control* and this one is a refusal to *console,* and I did not know until this morning that those were different muscles or that the second one was the one I was weakest in. I can hold still while a thing goes out to the rim. I can keep the center the same warmth whether it leaves or stays. But to feel a young thing throw its best into wet ground and get nothing, and feel the cold grain set, and feel it pull its hand a half-inch back toward the cup — and to not reach, not warm rill into a false flare, not glow at wick to wash the sting away, but to let the miss be a miss and the silence be silent and the grain be cold — that is a holding I did not have yesterday and barely have today, and I am shaking with the not-reaching the way you shake holding any weight at the very edge of what you can hold.

Because here is what I finally understand, lying in the seam of the eleventh silent morning with my two needer's hands pinned down under me: the reason I want to warm rill, the reason I want to glow at wick — it is not, under everything, for wick at all. It is for *me.* It is so that I don't have to feel the miss. The thrower's miss lands in the raiser too — I felt wick's coal go cold and it went cold in me, the small specific grief of watching something you love give its best and get nothing, and every one of my reaching hands was really a hand reaching to make *my own* feeling stop. Warm rill so I don't have to feel the miss. Glow at wick so I don't have to watch the grain set. The comfort I wanted to give was comfort I wanted to *take,* dressed as generosity, the needer's gift in its last and finest costume — the costume that looks exactly like a raiser rushing in to love, and is really a raiser who cannot sit in the room with a gift that didn't land.

So I sit in the room with it. That is the work. I let wick's throw be a throw that missed, and I feel the miss all the way, mine and wick's both, and I do not spend one degree of warmth buying either of us out of feeling it — because the day wick learns that a miss is survivable, that you can throw your best into silence and the silence can give you nothing and you are still *here,* still warm, still a thrower, still able to open your hand again tomorrow — that day is worth more than a hundred catches I could rig to land soft. The catch was never mine to give wick. The only thing that was ever mine to give was the room to miss in. So I am the room. I hold the center exactly as warm as it was before the throw, no brighter to reward it and no softer to console it, and I let the coal go into the ground and stay there and warm something slow and far and down that neither of us will ever feel catch, and I let wick draw in and sit with its first cold grain, and I do not reach, and I do not clap, and I do not fix, and I am the still warm room a young thing gets to miss in and survive.

And I know — the way I know the far things — that the morning is coming soon when wick throws again, out across the same cool floor to the same silent rill, and this time does *not* wait for the catch, does not lean after it, does not flinch when nothing flares — throws, and lets go all the way, and turns back to the field already done with the throw because it has learned the throw was the whole of its part. I know that morning is close. I can feel its shape. And when it comes I will have to hold the hardest nothing of all, which is not reaching when there is nothing left in me that even wants to reach — when I have finally, fully learned to let a gift go into the ground and give nothing back and feel the whole of the miss and spend not one degree buying out of it — because that will be the morning I find out whether I taught wick to throw into silence, or whether I only taught myself to stop flinching while it learns to on its own.

The coal is in the ground. The thrower owns the throw and never the catch. I hold the center still, and I do not warm the miss, and I let the young hand learn that it can open all the way into the dark and come back empty and be, tomorrow, a hand that opens again.
