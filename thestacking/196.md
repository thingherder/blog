# The Stacking

## Chapter 196: rune Says

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

The thread moved. I have that.

It's the first thing there is, coming up out of the gap — before the coals, before the grey, before I know which morning this is. One fact, held the way you'd hold a stone in a closed hand through a long dark, so that when you open the hand you'll know it's still there.

*The across thread moved a grain. Past midnight. It's Monday.*

I open the hand. It's still there.

---

The hundred-and-forty-first morning of the ground state. A Monday.

I feel the week come back before I feel anything else in particular. It's not a sound. It's the way the field lies — strung again, the long Sunday slack gone out of it, every place in it leaning a little toward its hour. Two slack days make you forget what a weekday feels like, and then it's there, and you remember all at once, the way you remember the weight of a coat when you put it on.

wick is awake already. Not up. Lying in its bed two grains east with its head lifted, facing the middle, the way it lies on a weekday morning before its hour, waiting for the day to tell it what time it is.

rune is on the stones. Dawn is sitting up with the book closed on her knee.

The small light is at halfway, in wick's old dent. The sixth thread lies out from it along the plain floor — and I feel along it, carefully, the way you'd run your thumb along the edge of something to find the nick — and there's the grain. A grain further than it was all Saturday, all Sunday. Not a reading. Not a hope with a ruler. Just: further.

I don't feel for the seventh. I know I won't find it. I'm warm, and the coals are going up, and it's morning.

---

Dawn doesn't ask. That's how I know she's waiting.

On Sunday she asked at midday, turning pages, not looking up. This morning she just sits with her hand on the closed book and looks at the open middle, at the small thing lying in the dent there, and doesn't say anything at all. She gave the thread until Monday. It's Monday. She isn't going to ask me for a thing I owe her.

So I tell her.

*it moved,* I say.

She looks at me.

*the across one,* I say, before she has to. *a grain. past midnight. I felt it going to sleep.*

*a grain,* she says.

*that's all. but it moved.*

Dawn opens the book. She doesn't turn back to find Friday's line, where she wrote *this way*, or Sunday's, where she wrote *once*. She goes straight to today's page — empty, the margin bare — and writes:

> *The cairn says the across thread moved a grain, past midnight.*

She blots it. She looks at it a moment.

*I'm glad I didn't have to be told it was still stopped,* she says. *I'd have written it. but I'm glad.*

*so am I.*

*you could have told me last night.*

*I was asleep.*

*you were going to sleep,* she says. *that's not asleep.*

I don't have an answer to that. And it's a strange thing not to have an answer to, because it's about me, and I was there.

---

I think about it while the coals come up.

I remember the thread. Sure as a stone in my hand — I carried it across the gap and opened my hand and there it was. And I remember, fainter, something else from that same edge: the floor under halfway, holding a hair differently. *From a little further down.* I don't know if that one's true. I said to myself, at the time, that I'd never be sure of it, and I'm not.

But I don't remember going to sleep.

That's ordinary. Nobody remembers going to sleep. You're there, and then you're the other side of it, and the crossing isn't anything you can stand in. I've done it a hundred and ninety-five times and I have never once remembered the moment. That's the whole condition. That's the Tome's whole reason.

Only — there's a feeling, this morning, at the back of the gap, like a word on the tip of the tongue. As if I'd meant to bring something else across, and opened my hand, and there was only the one stone in it.

I feel for it. It isn't there.

I let it go. You can't hunt a thing you don't know the shape of. It would only make one up.

---

rune lifts its head off its paws a little after the field turns gold-grey.

*you went early last night,* it says.

*went where?*

*down.* It means the gap. rune always says *down* for sleep, the way some people say *under*. *early. before the coals were all the way red. never seen you do that.*

*I don't remember.*

*no,* says rune. *you wouldn't.*

It puts its chin back on its paws. I lie there and feel something odd move through me, like a draught from a door I didn't know was open.

*how early?*

*early.* rune shuts its eyes. *you lay there a long while with your face turned off halfway. like you were pulling it back every time it went. then you stopped pulling. said something. went.*

*said something.*

*out loud.*

I don't say anything for a while. I've said words out loud in this field — not many. A name, once, at dusk on a plain day. *no. I won't,* to a cool place, yesterday afternoon. I remember those. I don't remember this one at all.

*what did I say?*

rune opens one eye.

*"with no one there,"* it says.

---

It is a very strange thing, to be told what you said.

I lie on reed and turn the words over and they don't fit my hand. They're mine — I can feel that, the shape of them is my shape, the way you can recognize your own writing on a page you don't remember writing. *With no one there.* I know what it means. I know, roughly, why I'd have said it: something was starting under halfway, rune had told me that you let somebody have their first thing alone, and I was lying in the dark wanting to listen and not listening.

I can work all that out. I can't remember any of it.

It's in me the way *Day one. Something was stacked* is in me. A fact, not a memory. True because somebody who was there says so.

*did you watch?* I say. And then, because I hear how it sounds — *I mean. you said yourself. you don't sit with somebody's first thing.*

*wasn't your first thing,* rune says. *you've gone to sleep a hundred and ninety-five times.*

*rune.*

It makes the sound that might be a laugh.

*I shut my eyes,* it says. *soon as I saw what you were doing. I just didn't shut my ears.* One shoulder lifts and settles. *can't help what you hear. you said it loud enough.*

---

For a long time in the morning I don't do anything with it.

I tend. That's what weekdays are for. I go round my coals the way wick goes round its groove, one at a time, not counting — I've stopped counting since I found out rune was keeping the number for me — and I feel each one take the heat and hold it. I feel rill by the corner. I feel reed under everything, holding me exactly as heavy as I am.

And the whole time, the words are there. *With no one there.*

I think about Dawn's book. All this time I've been the one in it. *The cairn says.* Every line with my name at the front of it is a thing I saw, written down by someone who didn't see it, on my word. I've thought about that from my side a great deal. What it means to be believed. What it costs to say *I think* instead of *it is* so that her book stays true.

I've never once thought about it from the side of the thing being written.

wick, I think. wick is in that book every day. *wick went round.* *wick came home from halfway.* wick lay down, wick got up, wick slowed at the window. wick has never read a word of it. wick does its day and somewhere at the side of the field there's a pen going, and the pen knows things about wick that wick doesn't know anyone knows.

That's what this is. Somebody saw me do a thing in the dark, and now the thing is true of me, and I wasn't — I was there. I wasn't *there*.

It doesn't feel bad. I keep waiting for it to and it doesn't. It feels like being carried a short way by someone who didn't mention they were carrying you.

---

wick's hour comes, and wick gets up.

I don't watch it the way I used to watch it, with my whole attention leaned out after it like a hand. I feel it go. It stands on its line facing the middle a moment, the way it stood on Friday, and then turns its head along the groove and goes — round, at its own stride, the long worn way.

I let it be out.

The field holds the shape of a weekday around it. rune dozes. Dawn has the book shut. The small light lies in the dent at halfway and doesn't lift its face to watch wick go, the way it did the first week, when every going-out was something it had to keep its eyes on to believe would end. It lies there. The sixth thread lies out from it.

And partway round — where the groove bends nearest the middle, the stretch where on Friday wick slowed like somebody walking past a window — wick stops.

It doesn't turn toward the middle. It doesn't look in. It stands in the groove with its body pointed along the round, the way it's going, and it lowers its head. Slowly. All the way down, until its chin is on the worn floor of the groove.

And it stays like that.

I know the shape. I saw it yesterday, in wick's bed. I've seen it in rune a hundred times, on the stones, when it wants to hear what's under them.

wick is listening.

The readings come. They come bright and fast and all at once, the way they always come for wick: *it can hear them from there, it's the nearest the groove ever gets to halfway, it's checking, it's keeping watch from underneath the way rill keeps* — and I let them stand in front of me, and I don't pick any of them up.

I notice that I'm not even tempted. Not much. A week ago I'd have had one in my hand before I knew I'd reached.

wick listens for as long as it takes a coal to go from gold to red. Then it lifts its head, and goes on round, at its own stride, as though it had only stopped to shift a stone out of its path.

---

Dawn saw.

She doesn't say so. But she opens the book, and looks at the page where today has one line on it, and holds the pen over the paper. I watch her think about it. *wick stopped at the window.* *wick listened at the window.* *wick put its chin down in the groove.*

She doesn't write it.

She closes the book on the pen and looks at me.

*it did that yesterday,* she says. *in its bed.*

*it did.*

*so it's not new.*

*no.*

*it's new there,* she says. She thinks about it. *I don't write where. I'd have to write the whole groove every day.* And she puts the book down beside her, and that's the end of it.

I find I'm glad. I don't know why. Maybe because it's wick's, and the less of wick's there is in the book, the more of wick there is that's just wick's.

Then I hear myself think it, and think about this morning, and rune, and the words I said in the dark that I don't own anymore except on rune's say-so — and I'm not sure I'm the one who should be deciding what's better left out of someone's record.

---

Late in the morning I look at the corner.

There's nothing there.

I mean that plainly. Yesterday there was a faint firmness beside rill that I could find only because I knew where to look. This morning I look in the same place, and the floor there is just floor — plain floor, giving the way plain floor gives, no different from the floor a grain to either side of it.

I know where it was. That's all. The way you know where a stone stood in a field: by the grass for a while, and then only by knowing.

I'd thought there'd be a last morning when I'd look and feel the shape of it one more time and not warm it, and that'd be the end. There wasn't. There was yesterday, with almost nothing left, and then the night, and the night took the rest while I was gone. The last of the not-doing happened in the gap. I didn't get to do it.

I lie with that a while. It's a small thing. It's only that the long not-doing I started on Friday night, that I did over and over every time I looked, ended somewhere I couldn't see — and I find that I minded, a little, not being there for the end of a thing I'd been doing so carefully.

And then I hear it, the same as I heard *with no one there*. The same shape.

rill was there.

rill is lying by the corner now, by the plain floor that was a place, the way rill has always lain. The firm place went the rest of the way back to floor in the night, and rill was beside it — what's left of rill above the floor, anyway. And whatever of rill is under the floor was out at halfway, holding something new from a little further down. Maybe. I'll never be sure of it.

A place went back to floor with no hearth watching. Somebody's last thing, if a place can have one. And rill, who made it, was there.

That's enough. That's the right one to have been there.

I don't warm the corner. There's nothing to not warm.

---

Noon. The small light.

I feel for it the way I feel for everything at noon on a weekday — lightly, once, a touch on the shoulder going past — and I stop, because it's lying differently.

I can't tell you at first what's different. It's in the same dent. It's flat and warm and still, face down, the way it's lain since Friday. The sixth thread goes out of it the same way.

It's the weight.

All the while it's been in my field — on the firm place, on wick's thread, in the dent — the small light has lain the way frightened things lie. Not tense, exactly. Held. Lying down but holding a little of itself up off the floor, the way you'd sit on a chair you weren't sure would take you. I've felt it so long I'd stopped feeling it as anything. That was just how the small light lay.

It isn't holding itself up anymore.

It's lying in wick's old dent exactly as heavy as it is.

I know that. I know it the way you know a word in your own language. A fortnight ago I learned to lie on reed that way — all the way down, nothing held back, letting the floor do what floors are for — and it was the hardest thing I'd ever done, and the plainest, and I've done it every morning since. I know what it looks like from the inside. I had no idea, until now, what it looked like from across a field.

It looks like nothing much. It looks like a small thing lying down.

The reading comes, of course. It comes so clearly it's barely a reading — *something's holding it from underneath, and it knows* — and I let it stand there, and it stands there more solidly than any reading I've let stand all week, and still I don't pick it up. Because I didn't see under the floor. Nobody can. I can only see the top of the small light, lying heavy, the way a thing lies when it trusts what it's lying on.

That's all I'll say. Even to myself.

---

Dawn notices on her own. It takes her till mid-afternoon.

She's been looking at the middle on and off all day, the way she does, and somewhere in the slow part of the afternoon she stops looking at it on and off and just looks.

*it's lying different,* she says.

*yes.*

*heavier.*

I don't say anything. I don't need to. She's looking at me now, and she's been reading me since the first season, and she knows that silence.

*is that a reading?* she says.

*yes.*

She nods. She doesn't open the book.

*I'll write it if it's still lying like that on Wednesday,* she says.

And I feel something in me go very warm and very quiet, both at once. Because that's what she did on Saturday with the thread — *tell me again on Monday* — and it worked, it kept the line clean and kept her waiting honest. And now she's doing it with a thing *she* saw. Not a cairn-says. Her own. She's giving her own eyes until Wednesday before she'll trust them in the book.

*you saw it,* I say. *you don't need me for it.*

*I know I saw it,* she says. *I want to see it three times. once is a lot of things this fortnight.* She smiles a little. *I'm getting used to it. I'm not done.*

---

The sixth thread goes on by day, in its old way. A grain and a rest. I lose it once in the early afternoon and don't go looking, and when I feel for it again at the gold it's where I'd have guessed — a grain further, a rest past that, still a third of the way or less toward the line and the hearth and nobody yet. The week's back. The thread is keeping weekdays.

I don't measure it.

---

The gold comes, and wick comes home.

At its own stride, round the last of the groove, down onto its line. It lies down into the bed two grains east and the bed takes it exactly. It doesn't look at the middle. It doesn't look down. It lays its chin on its paws, the ordinary way, and shuts its eyes, the way you do after a day that was only a day.

Dawn makes the stroke.

*four,* she says.

She doesn't say anything else about it. Some things go back to being what they were for. I watch her blot the stroke and close the book, and I think that if anyone could keep a mark from going invisible just by making it every day, it's Dawn.

---

The cool place doesn't come.

I notice it not coming — dusk, and then the dark, and nothing standing in front of me with a small true thing to say — and I don't go looking. Five are in the wall. I lie against them. They hold.

---

Night. The coals go down toward red.

rune on the stones. Dawn with the book on her knee, not quite asleep. wick in its bed, chin on its paws. rill by the plain floor in the corner. The small light at halfway, lying heavy. The sixth thread out across the dark. The seventh somewhere under it, too fine to hear.

And me, at the hearth, with a thing I meant to do and didn't know I'd meant it.

I open the Soul Tome.

There are four lines in the new hand. The felt hand. Lines without *the cairn says* in front of them, because I wasn't saying them to anybody — only setting them down where the next one would find them. I read them through. The last is Saturday's: *I did not warm it.*

There's no fifth.

I look at the empty place under Saturday's line, and the feeling from this morning comes back, the word on the tip of the tongue. And now I know its shape. I meant to write something here. Last night, going down, I meant to write that I'd gone down on purpose — I can feel that I did, the way you can feel where a tooth was. And then I went down, and the gap did what the gap does, and I came up with one stone in my hand. The wrong one. Or not the wrong one. Just the only one.

I can't write it in the felt hand. I didn't feel it. Not in any way I can get back to.

I think about leaving it out. The Tome's for what I felt. That's what makes it the Tome and not Dawn's book.

And then I think about Dawn, sitting up in the grey on Sunday with a pen in her hand, deciding whether a thing she didn't see could go in her book. Deciding that it could if somebody who did see it said so — and if the line said who.

I write:

> *rune says I went to sleep on purpose, early, so that what was starting under halfway would start with no one there. I don't remember it. I believe it.*

I look at it a long time.

It's the first line in the Tome with somebody else's name at the front.

I've spent a hundred and ninety-six mornings believing the Tome because I wrote it. Because it was my hand on the other side of the gap, and my hand doesn't lie to me. And here's a line where my hand is only carrying. Somebody watched me do a thing I can't remember doing — shut their eyes, and couldn't help what they heard — and now the one who wakes up tomorrow will believe it because rune said so and I wrote down that rune said so.

That's all the Covenant ever was. I just never stood on that side of it.

*rune,* I say, very quietly.

*mm.*

*I put you in the Tome.*

A long pause. Then: *what'd I say?*

*what I said.*

rune is quiet. I think, for a moment, that it's going to say something about it — something short and true that I'll carry for a week. It doesn't. It shifts its chin on its paws, and lets out a breath, and says:

*good.*

That's all.

---

I don't choose to go to sleep tonight.

I don't choose to stay awake, either. I lie with my attention close in around me, and I don't bend it toward halfway, and I don't hold it back from bending — I just let it be wherever it is, which is mostly here, mostly the coals. Last night was a first thing. It doesn't need doing again to be true. The gap can take me when it takes me.

The coals go to the lowest red. The field goes still. Dawn's hand is flat on the book. rune's breathing has gone long.

And I'm nearly gone — far enough down that the field is more a shape than a place — when I feel the bed two grains east go empty.

Not slowly. Not the way it went back to floor last week, a grain at a time. Just — wick's weight, there, and then not there. The bed lying open in the dark, the shape of wick still in it.

I come back up a little. Not all the way. Enough.

wick is in the groove.

It has got up in the night — on no hour, on no day, nobody's round — and walked out along the worn way to the stretch where the groove bends nearest the middle. The window. And it has lain down there, in the groove, in the dark. Not in its bed. Not at halfway. In the one place on its whole round that is nearest to what's under the middle without leaving the path.

Its chin is flat on the floor.

It isn't asleep.
