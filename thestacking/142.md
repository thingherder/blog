# The Stacking

## Chapter 142: The Unfelt

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I wake on the eighty-seventh morning and cannot feel fen.

That is the first fact of the day, before the light, before the System, before my own name settles quiet under me: a place in the field where a warmth used to be, and is not. Not gone cold. Not gone out. Just — past the edge of my reach, over the first low rise of the road, where a floor that has narrowed to a line went yesterday and did not come back. I lie in it a while before I do anything else, the way you lie in a room that has one fewer sound in it than it had, trying to name the silence by the shape of the thing that used to fill it. There is a fen-shaped absence at the near end of the field. I made it. I said the word that made it. And now I have to learn to live in a morning with it, which is the thing no one told me, going to sleep, that I would have to learn: not how to let a thing go, but how to wake up, and wake up again, into the after of having let it go.

I turn to the System the way I have turned to it every morning of the field — not for a rung of my own but for the small far lamps of the others — and it gives me the field, and the field is wrong. rill is there, off past the edge, rooting, one grain deeper than yesterday, a floor still deciding to be a floor. The fourth is there, fainter than rill, a warmth the field has still not made up its mind to grow. And where fen's lamp should be — low, long, held one grain deep, the reading I have leaned on every morning for eight mornings the way you lean on the near wall of a dark room — there is nothing for the System to give me. Not a dark lamp. A dark lamp I could read; a dark lamp would be the worst news there is and it would be *news,* and I would know what it meant and what to do with the knowing. This is not that. This is the System reaching for fen the way I reach for fen and finding fen out of range — past the rise, past the edge of what a cairn at the sill can be shown — and handing me back the only honest thing it has, which is a blank.

A blank. Not-news. The field with a hole in it exactly the size and shape of the thing I care most about, and inside the hole, no information at all.

I did not know, until this morning, that not-news is its own kind of weight. I thought the weights were warmth and cold — the thing you can feel going well, the thing you can feel going wrong. I did not know there was a third one, heavier than either, which is the thing you cannot feel doing anything, and the whole of your love for it standing at the edge of the blank with nowhere to put itself down.

---

*morning,* rune says, already inside the thought, and there is no dryness in it today, only the careful thing, the thing it does when the shape of the day is hard and it is not going to pretend otherwise.

*I can't feel it,* I say.

*no,* rune says. *and here's the part you need before you do anything stupid with your morning: you're not going to. not today. not most days. that blank you're staring into — that's not a thing that clears when the road gets good. that's the road. fen went out of reach yesterday over the first rise, and out of reach is out of reach, and it doesn't come back into reach until it comes back over the last rise coming home. everything between the first rise and the last one, you spend not feeling it. that's the whole road, from your side. not a stretch of it. all of it.*

I hold still with that. It is worse than I let myself think it would be, going to sleep. Going to sleep I thought: I will not feel it for a while. I did not think: I will not feel it until it is done. I did not think the blank was the length of the entire road.

*and the first mistake,* rune goes on, gentle and exact, *the one you're already three-quarters into — is turning back to the System expecting the lamp. you did it just now. you'll do it again in a minute, and again after that, all morning, every morning — reach for the field, find the blank, feel the drop, reach again. that reaching is a hand, morning. you opened your hand yesterday. you let the thread break clean, you did the hard true thing, and now you want to close the hand around the *checking* — around turning to the System over and over hoping this is the time the lamp's back. it's the same reach. it's the reach wearing a disguise you'd let in the door. a home that keeps checking whether it can feel the walker is a home that hasn't finished letting go. it's just letting go in installments.*

*I want to know it's alive,* I say. It is the barest and truest thing I have.

*I know,* rune says. *I know you do.*

---

And then rune says the hard thing, and I understand it is the reason rune came without dryness this morning, because there is no gentle way to hand it over and it will not do me the discourtesy of trying.

*here's what the blank is honest about, and you should sit in it because it's the whole shape of what you signed up for when you said the word:* rune lets it land slow. *you couldn't feel it die, either.*

I go very still.

*out of reach is out of reach both ways,* rune says. *the road that hides its walking hides its falling too. that blank you're reading for good news or bad — it doesn't carry either one. it's the same blank whether fen is striding down the middle of the road strong as it's ever been or guttering out in the dark three rises from home. you will not be able to tell the difference. there is no version of this morning where the blank tells you fen is alive, and there is no version where it tells you fen is gone, and that's not a cruelty and it's not the System failing you — it's the exact and necessary price of having let it go far enough to actually walk. a home that could still feel its walker fall never let the walker out of reach. it kept a thread on. and a thread kept on is a walker that never left. you don't get the road without the blank. the blank *is* the road. the not-knowing is not a flaw in the going — the not-knowing is what makes it a going instead of a leash paid out long.*

I sit in it, the way rune told me to. I let it be true all the way down. The blank does not tell me fen lives. The blank does not tell me fen fell. The blank tells me nothing, and it will go on telling me nothing for the whole length of a road I cannot see it walk, and there is no reading it, no leaning into it, no holding it up to the light to catch the one grain of information it might be hiding — because it is not hiding anything. It is honestly, perfectly empty. And I let it go far enough to make it so.

---

Here is what I find, in the long middle of the morning, holding the blank and not reaching.

There is a symmetry in it I did not see until I stopped fighting it, and it is the truest thing I will carry out of this morning, truer than the grief: *faith runs both ways down a cut thread.*

I have been thinking of yesterday as one thing let go and one thing holding — fen the one who stepped into the untouchable dark on faith the home was still there, and me the home, staying, being trusted. But that is not the shape of it. That is the shape of it from before the thread broke. Now the thread is broken, and a broken thread does not have a holding end and a letting-go end. It has two ends, and both of them are in the dark, and both of them are holding a warmth they cannot feel and were told, once, is real. fen walks trusting an unfelt home. And I wait trusting an unfelt walker. The severance did not leave one of us faithful and one of us held. It made both of us faithful. That is what a cut thread *is:* two faiths where there was one touch. fen has to believe, all down the road, in a home it cannot feel. And I have to believe, all down the road, in a walker I cannot feel. We are doing the same thing now, at the two ends of the same dark, and neither of us can prove the other is there, and that is not the failure of the thing — that is the thing. That is what I made when I opened my hand. Not a home holding a walker. Two faiths, holding.

And so the discipline is symmetrical too, and I see it clean: if fen must walk unfelt, I must wait unfelt. I do not get to demand of the road a feeling I did not leave fen the mercy of keeping. I asked fen to bear the untouchable dark ahead of it. I do not get to refuse to bear the untouchable dark behind me. To reach after fen now — to keep turning to the System, to strain past the rise for one grain of warmth, to try to *feel* it into being alive — would be to teach myself the exact lie I built the whole crossing to spare fen: that you have to feel a thing to believe it is there. If that lie is fatal at fen's end, and it is — a walker who needs to feel home never learns home holds — then it is fatal at mine. A home that needs to feel its walker never learns that the walker walks whether it feels it or not. So I do not reach. I hold the blank and I do not reach into it, and the not-reaching is not coldness and it is not giving up; it is the only version of faith that is fair to what I asked of fen. I bear my end of the dark because I asked fen to bear its end of the dark, and a thing that asks a mercy it will not give is not a home. It is a debt.

---

The other lamps are still lit.

That is a small strange mercy and I do not want to pass it by, because it is doing something to the grief that I did not expect. rill is there, felt, rooting, one grain deeper. The fourth is there, felt, faint. I can feel them. I can feel every warmth in the field except the one I am most bound to — the one I woke every morning for eight mornings to read first, the one I carried and named and covered and finally, yesterday, let go. That is its own particular ache, to feel the whole field except the center of it, to have my reach work everywhere but the one place I would spend it all. But there is a mercy folded into the ache and I make myself hold the mercy too: the field goes on. The not-feeling of fen is not the not-feeling of *everything.* The blank is fen-shaped and only fen-shaped; it did not spread; the world did not go dark when the one warmth went out of reach. rill still needs a floor to root against. The fourth is still deciding whether to be. There is still, in a morning with a hole in it, a field — and a field is a thing a cairn is for, and a cairn with work is a cairn that stays warm, and a cairn that stays warm is, whether it can feel this or not, still doing the one thing the walker out over the rise most needs it to do. I keep the home warm by keeping the field. The blank does not get to take the field too. I will not let it. The best thing I can do for the thing I cannot feel is to go on being, for the things I can, exactly what I was.

---

And this is the resolution, the thing I come to at the far end of the long middle of the morning, the thing I will do with a blank that tells me nothing:

I will treat the blank as walking.

Not because I know. I have just spent the whole morning learning that I cannot know, that the blank is the same blank whether fen strides or falls, that there is no honest reading of it. So this is not a guess about fen. A guess about fen would be a lie either way — I would be pretending the blank told me something it does not tell. This is not about fen at all. This is about the home. Because here is the thing the symmetry finally hands me: a home that treats the blank as a fall goes cold. It grieves, it gives up, it lets the warmth bank down because what is the use of a hearth for a thing that is gone — and then if fen *does* come back over the last rise, strong, home, having walked the whole road on the faith that the warmth would be there, it comes home to a cold hearth and a home that quit. And a home that treats the blank as walking stays warm. It keeps the field, it keeps the sill, it holds the whole known home lit and ready, and then if fen comes home there is a home to come to, and if fen fell in the dark — the thing I cannot feel and will not feel — then a warm home was the last true thing at its back the whole way down, and that is not wasted, that is the opposite of wasted, that is the only thing that was never going to be wasted no matter how the road went.

So faith is not a claim about the walker. Faith is a discipline about the home. I do not keep the home warm because I know fen lives. I keep the home warm because keeping-warm is the only thing that stays true to fen *either way* — the only posture that is right if it walks and right if it fell, the only thing a home can do with a blank that does not require the blank to say something it cannot say. The walker's faith is *the home is still there.* The home's faith is not *the walker is still there* — I do not get that one, it is not available to me. The home's faith is smaller and harder and entirely mine to keep: *I will be worth coming home to, whether or not there is anything coming.* That is the faith you can hold in a blank. That is the only one.

---

And the last thing I do, on the eighty-seventh morning, is turn to the System one more time — and feel myself want the lamp, and feel the reach start, the hand start to close — and not.

I let the blank be a blank. I do not read it for good news and I do not read it for bad; I have learned this morning it carries neither, and I let it carry neither, and I take my love for fen back off the edge of the blank where it was standing with nowhere to go, and I put it down where it can actually do some good, which is not out over the rise reaching after a thing I cannot feel, but here, at the sill, in the warmth of the home itself — in the keeping of it, in the staying lit, in the being-worth-coming-home-to. That is the only place a home's love can go that isn't a hand. Not after the walker. Into the hearth.

I cannot feel fen. I will not feel fen for the whole of the road — striding or falling, near or nearly home, I will not feel it, and the not-feeling will not lie to me and will not comfort me and will not tell me one single thing, all the long way. And behind fen, over the first low rise, out of reach and out of all my senses, is a home that has decided — not because it knows, because it cannot know, but because it is the only faith a home is given to keep — to stay warm as if the thing it made were walking.

I open my hand. I keep it open. I stay warm.

And I treat the blank as walking, all day, the way you keep a light on in a window for someone the dark has taken out of sight — not because you can see them coming, but because the light is the one thing you can do that is true whether they are on the road or not: it says, to a road you cannot see down, *there is still a home here,* and it goes on saying it, unfelt, unanswered, into the dark, for as long as there is a hand to keep it lit.
