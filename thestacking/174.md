# The Stacking

## Chapter 174: The Waiting

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I wake on the hundred-and-nineteenth morning of the ground state, and it is slack again, and that is the first wrong thing — not that the day has no shape, which I learned yesterday a day could do and be survived, but that I already *know* it has no shape, and knowing is a different country than finding out.

Yesterday I woke into the slack blind. I had never stood a shapeless day before and I did not know whether I was a floor or a tent, and the not-knowing was its own terror, and the terror, I understand now lying in the seam of the second slack morning, was doing something for me that I did not notice it doing: it was keeping me *here.* A thing you do not know whether you can survive, you survive with your whole attention — every grain of you bent down onto the one question, can I hold this, am I level, is the floor a floor — and there is no part of you left over to be anywhere but the morning you are standing in. The terror pins you to the present the way a stake pins a tent. And this morning the terror is gone. I held the slack yesterday and I did not come down, and so this morning I wake already knowing I can hold it, and the knowing has pulled the stake, and I find — before I am all the way awake — that without the terror pinning me to the day, I am not in the day at all. I am leaning out of it.

I lie in the second slack morning and feel the new thing, the thing yesterday did not have, and it is not panic and it is not the slack itself. It is a *lean.* My whole attention has turned, without my permission, toward the far edge of the long flat day — toward the place, two slack days out, where the week's shape comes back, where Monday rebuilds the machine and strings the hours taut again and gives the day somewhere it has to be. I am not standing in the Sunday. I am standing at the near edge of it with my face turned all the way across it toward the Monday on the far side, waiting for the slack to be over, and the waiting feels so reasonable, so much like patience, so much like simply *getting through,* that it takes me a long moment to see it for what it is: that I have found a third way to not be a floor under a slack day, and it is subtler than the other two, and I did not even know to brace for it.

Yesterday's two hands were loud. One wanted to fill the slack with a manufactured round; one wanted to cut the line and sink. Those are things you *do,* and a thing you do, you can catch yourself doing and hold down. This morning there is no thing to do. There is only a direction I am already facing. I am not thrashing and I am not drowning. I am treading water with my eyes fixed on the far shore, spending the whole flat crossing wishing I were already across — and a thing that spends a slack day wishing it over is not holding the day. It is enduring it. And I lie very still in the hundred-and-nineteenth morning and understand that there is a difference between holding a slack day and enduring one, and that I have no idea yet how to do the first, because every part of me this morning has quietly agreed to do the second.

wick does not set out, again.

It is the second morning the round has lain slack, and the second morning is not like the first. Yesterday wick woke and warmed and milled near the hearth, lost and loopless, a light that had only ever known itself by its round and could not find the round to know itself by, and the milling was confused, a turning-in-place that did not understand why there was nowhere to go. This morning wick wakes and warms and does not set out, and the milling is calmer — not confused now, but *settled,* a light that has learned, in one slack day, that some mornings there is no round and that the no-round is allowed. It circles the warm near space easy, unhurried, in no particular shape, and it is not lost this morning. It is just resting on a day with no loop to run.

And I watch it rest, and I feel the lean in me pull harder, because wick resting in the slack is exactly the thing I cannot do — wick is *in* its Sunday, milling warm and shapeless and present, wishing for no round, leaning toward no Monday, and I am standing beside it with my whole face turned across the flat day toward the week, and the gap between us is the whole of the chapter. wick does not know there is a Monday. That is its gift this morning and it does not know it is a gift. A light that has never learned the week has never learned to wish a slack day away, and so it simply floats the flat, warm and loopless and entirely here, the way a thing floats that has no far shore to stare at.

I drift, the way I drift now, back toward the smooth place. To reed.

And here is the small cruel mechanism I find this morning, standing on it. Yesterday I learned that the smooth place is smooth to the crossing and deep to the standing-still — that a schedule walks you over it before your weight can settle, and a slack day sits you down in the middle of it with nothing to carry you across. And I stood on it yesterday, with my whole weight, and held level, and it was the hardest holding I had done. But I did it *facing down.* I stood on the deep place and looked into it and held level over the depth, and that was terrible and that was true. This morning I am standing on the same deep place and I am not looking down at all. I am looking across — at the far edge, at the week, at Monday — and I understand that looking across is not the same as holding. A floor that stands on the grief with its face turned toward the day the grief will be easier is not standing on the grief. It is standing on the grief the way you stand in a cold lake you are waiting to get out of — enduring the cold, counting the minutes, present in body and already gone in every way that matters. And the grief knows. The grief is not held by a floor that is only standing on it until something better comes. The grief is only held by a floor that is willing to stand on it with no better thing coming, facing it, in the day it is actually in.

And both of my hands come up.

The first hand wants to lean all the way into the lean. To stop pretending I am holding the Sunday and admit that I am only waiting it out — to spend the whole slack day with my face across it, gritting through the flat hours, treating the Sunday as a waiting room with the grief for furniture and Monday for the door, counting the day down, living toward the moment the week comes back and strings the line taut and does the holding for me again. The hand that wishes the day away. The hand that is relieved, somewhere underneath, that the slack is temporary, that the machine will start again tomorrow, that I will not have to be a floor under nothing much longer — because being a floor under the week is so much easier than being a floor under nothing, the week does half the holding, and a part of me that I am not proud of is simply tired, and wants the easy holding back, and will endure one more slack day gladly as long as it knows the tension is coming to take the weight off.

And the second hand wants to refuse the week entirely. To dread Monday, to resent it, to turn my face across the day not in longing but in loyalty — because the week coming back feels like a betrayal, feels like the machine papering over the slack, feels like the ordinary tension hauling us all up off the grief and carrying us on before we have finished standing in it; and this hand wants to cling to the slack as the only honest ground there is, to want it to *never* end, to hate the Monday that will come and busy us out of the depth, to stand on the smooth place and grip it and refuse to let the week walk me across it again, because being walked across feels like forgetting and the slack at least is true. The hand that dreads the tension. The hand that would keep the whole life slack and raw forever rather than let the ordinary come back and make the holding bearable, because bearable feels like a lie.

So one hand reaches forward to wish the slack away and hand the holding back to the week, and the other reaches back to refuse the week and keep the holding raw forever, and I lie in the second slack morning with both hands come up and understand that they are facing opposite directions and they are the same wrong — that both of them have their face turned across the day instead of down into it, one toward the Monday it wants and one toward the Monday it hates, and that a floor with its face turned across the day, in longing or in loyalty, is not standing in the day at all.

*the second one,* rune says, close, dry, and there is something almost gentle in it this morning, the voice of a thing that knows the second slack day is quieter and meaner than the first. *i wondered if you'd feel it. most don't, not the first weekend — the first slack day's too loud, you're too busy finding out if you'll drown to notice anything else. it's the second one that teaches this, because by the second one you already know you won't drown, and the second you stop being afraid of the water you start staring at the shore. go on. tell me where your face is.*

*across the day,* I say. *not down into it. i'm standing on the smooth place and i'm not looking at it, i'm looking past it, at the far edge where the week comes back. and one hand wants to wish the whole slack day away and be glad the machine starts tomorrow, because the week does half the holding and i'm tired and being a floor under the week is easier than being a floor under nothing. and the other wants to hate the week for coming, to cling to the slack because the slack is the only honest thing and the week will just carry us all off the grief before we've finished. and i can't tell which is the lie.*

*oh, they're both the lie,* rune says, *you knew that before you asked. but you've got the shape of it right, which is more than most get, so let me give you the part under it.* A pause. The field quiet, wick milling warm and shapeless and present, the smooth place deep under my turned-away weight. *yesterday you learned the difference between a floor and a tent. the slack took the tension away and asked whether you were level on your own, and you were, and you were proud of it, and you earned the pride. but here's what the first slack day can't teach you, because the first one's too frightening to teach anything but survival. there's a difference between a floor that HOLDS a slack day and a floor that ENDURES one. and from the outside they look exactly the same. both of them stay level. both of them don't come down. you can endure a slack day perfectly — hold flat the whole flat length of it, never sink, never thrash — and still fail the day completely, because enduring is just holding with your face turned toward the end. enduring is a tent that's learned to stand up straight while it waits for the wind to come back. it doesn't come down. but it's not resting. it's bracing. and a thing that braces through its rest hasn't learned to rest; it's learned to white-knuckle the quiet.*

*so i'm a tent again,* I say, slow. *a patient one. a tent that doesn't fall but spends the whole still morning waiting for the wind.*

*that's the trap of the second day exactly,* rune says. *the first slack day asks can you stand up with nothing holding you. and you can. the second slack day asks something harder and quieter: can you stand up with nothing holding you and not spend the whole time wishing something would? because wishing the slack away is the subtlest way left to still need the tension. you're not leaning on the week anymore — you proved you don't have to. but you're waiting for it. you're counting the hours till it comes back and takes the weight. and a floor that's counting the hours till the weight comes back is a floor that still, underneath, would rather be held than hold. it just hides it better. it hides it as patience. it hides it as getting through. it hides it as tired.* The dry voice does not sharpen; it stays level, which is the hardest way to hear it. *and i'll name the oldest part of it, because you met this one back before the slack, and you'll recognize it. you remember the empty day — the well field with no wrongness in it, and the hand that went hunting a wound just to have a weight to be a floor under? the wish for monday is that same hand. the week is a weight. a schedule is a load. and a floor that's tired of bearing nothing goes looking, very quietly, for the load to come back — not because it loves the week but because a floor under a load at least gets to FEEL like a floor, and a floor under nothing has to take it on faith. the wish for monday is the hunger to be needed, wearing the last coat it's got left: the coat of just being tired, just wanting it to be over, just getting through. and it's the best disguise of all of them, because it's half true. you ARE tired. that part's not a lie. the lie is letting the tiredness turn your face across the day.*

I am quiet a while, because it lands where the true things land, below the words, in the turned-away weight itself.

*and the other hand,* I say. *the one that hates the week for coming. that one's wrong the same way?*

*same way, other direction,* rune says. *the clinging hand's got its face across the day too — it's just scowling instead of longing. it wants to keep the slack forever because the slack feels honest and the week feels like forgetting, and that's the loyalty costume, the one that thinks the ordinary coming back is a betrayal of the grief. but hear what it actually wants. it wants to never let the week hold you again, because being held by the week feels like being carried off the grief. and that's backwards, same as all of it. the week's going to come back whether you bless it or curse it — mondays are a tide too, they come in on their own, you don't get a vote. the only thing you get to decide is whether you spend the last of the slack with your face turned toward the monday you're longing for, or the last of the slack with your face turned toward the monday you're dreading — or whether you turn your face back DOWN into the sunday you're actually standing in, and let it be sunday, all the way to its end, and let monday come find you here instead of meeting it halfway across a day you refused to live in.*

*so i don't wish it away,* I say, slow, *and i don't dread it. i let the week come back when it comes, and i let it do the easier holding, and i don't pretend i'm too pure to be glad of the help — but i don't live toward it. i stand in the slack with my face down in it, facing the thing i'm standing on, and i stop counting the hours.*

*you stop counting the hours,* rune says. *and here's the part you're circling, so i'll land it, because it's the whole of the second slack day and almost nobody gets it.* And now it gentles, the way it does right before the thing that stays. *you think the finish line was yesterday. you think surviving the first slack day was the test and you passed it and now you're just serving out the weekend till the week comes back to the rescue. and surviving the slack was never the finish line. the finish line is not needing it to end. anybody can endure a slack day if they know it's temporary — you can grit through almost anything you can see the far edge of. the floor doesn't finish becoming a floor the day it survives the slack. it finishes the day it stops wishing the slack away. the day it can stand in the shapeless hours with its face turned down and not count them, not lean past them, not long for the tension and not dread it, just — be level in the day it was actually given, with no better day coming to save it. that's the rest you spent a whole ladder trying to reach. not surviving the quiet. resting in it. and the proof you've got there isn't that you held — you held yesterday. the proof is that you stopped looking at the clock.*

And I lie very still, and feel my face, slowly, begin to turn back down.

*and the far stir,* I say, because I can feel it out there, faint past the rim, flat and quiet as it was. *it hasn't moved. still far, still slack.*

*still slack,* rune agrees, quiet now. *and here's why i bring it up, because it's the same lesson twice and you should see it's the same. there are two floods out there. there's the week's flood — monday, the tension, the machine coming back — and that one's near, and you can feel it coming, and your whole face wants to swim out and drag it in early because the slack is hard and the week is easier. and there's the far flood — the stir, the new thing, the one that isn't born yet — and that one's far, and you've already been told not to swim out to it, not to make your own current, to let it turn on its own. and what you haven't noticed is that wishing for monday is the exact same mistake as reaching for the far stir. both of them are hurrying a tide. both of them are a thing that can't stand the flat water turning its face toward the next pull just to feel like something's coming. the far flood you're not tempted to rush because it's frightening. the week's flood you're tempted to rush because it's comforting. but it's the same current-making either way — the same refusal to float the flat. don't swim toward monday any more than you'd swim toward the stir. let both floods come when they come. float the slack with your face down. the sunday turns into the monday on its own. it always has. you don't row it there.*

And I feel it reach down the standing edge, the way the true things do, and I know a second slack day is stood, or only endured, by whether a floor can stand it without spending the whole of it wishing it were over.

The last thing I do, on the hundred-and-nineteenth morning, is turn my face back down into the day.

I do not lean the lean all the way into the week. I do not spend the slack Sunday staring across it at the Monday that will string the hours taut and do the easier holding, do not grit the flat length of it counting down, do not let the good true tiredness in me turn into a wish that the hard day were already behind me. And I do not refuse the week. I do not grip the slack and dread the coming tension and call the clinging loyalty, do not decide that the ordinary coming back to carry us is a betrayal of the smooth place, do not make the Monday a sin for the mercy it will bring. I let the week be a tide that comes when it comes. And I turn my face — slowly, it is not easy, it wants all morning to turn back across — down into the Sunday I am actually standing in.

And it is a different holding than yesterday's, and it joins the tally in a column all its own, next to the slack but not the same as it, because yesterday I held the slack and today I am learning to *rest* in it, and they are not the same thing and the second is harder in a way the first could not have shown me. Yesterday the terror did half my work — it pinned me here, it kept my whole attention down on the one question of whether I could hold. Today there is no terror to pin me, and nothing keeps my face down in the day except my own deciding to keep it there, over and over, every time it drifts back across toward the week. It drifts all morning. I turn it back all morning. And somewhere in the turning-back I stop, without deciding to stop, counting the hours — and the slack day, when I am no longer waiting for it to end, stops being a thing I am getting through and becomes, simply, the day I am in, shapeless and long and quiet, and I find I can stand in it that way, face down, clock-blind, with no easier day coming to save me, and be level on it, and not wish it away.

The field is slack, a second day, and wick rests loopless and calm in the warm and does not know there is a week to wish toward, and the smooth place is deep under my settled weight, and I hold the whole shapeless day level — and this morning I do more than hold it; this morning, a little, toward the end, I rest on it, face down, not counting. And out past the rim in the cold shared dark, the far stir lies still — not nearer, not gone, flat and quiet in the slack, one of the two floods that will come, in its own hour, when it comes.

And I do not swim toward it, and I do not swim toward the nearer flood either, the week's flood, the comfortable one, the Monday my tiredness would row out and drag in early if I let it. I float level through the last of the slack, and I hold my bearings by the shore and not by either pull, and I set my hand flat on the smooth place reed became, and keep my face turned down into it, in the day I am in, with no better day hurried toward — and somewhere a long way out, the tide lies slack in the still hour between the ebb that is spent and the flood that has not come, and turns, in no hurry at all, and does not need me to row it, toward the week that will come back on its own, and the thing past the rim that will come when it is born, each in its own turning, neither one hurried, both of them coming to the floor that learned, on the second slack day, to stop watching the water and stand.
