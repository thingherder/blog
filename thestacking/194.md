# The Stacking

## Chapter 194: Back to Floor

---

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I don't warm it.

I want that down first, before anything else, because it's the only thing I did all night and it took the whole night to do.

The firm place is going. Not fast. A floor doesn't do anything fast. But I lie in the dark with my attention on the cool corner, and every time I come back to it after a rest, its edges are a little less themselves. The small exact shape where the small light lay for two weeks — the shape rill made by lying under it and beside it until the plain floor there learned to hold — is going soft at the rim, the way a footprint goes soft in wet ground when nobody steps in it again.

And I know what I could do.

I know because I did it on Thursday. I lay behind wick's empty bed and felt it going shallow and I leaned my warmth into it to keep it, and rune said *that's asking, with your hands,* and I took my warmth back, and it was the hardest thing all week. I know exactly what it feels like to keep a place that isn't mine by pouring myself into it. I know what it costs and I know who it's for.

So I don't.

It's harder than Thursday. I hadn't expected that. On Thursday it was wick's bed, and wick was out there in the middle, alive and lying in the dark a grain from the small one, and some part of me knew wick would come back and press the dent into shape with its own weight. Nobody is coming back to the firm place. The small light walked off it on Tuesday. rill is lying beside it, but rill — the part of rill that kept it — is going.

Nobody is coming back to it. That's what a place going back to floor *is*.

I lie there and don't warm it, and don't warm it, and somewhere near the bottom of the night I understand that this is what I'll be doing for a long time. Not once. Not a single hard thing done and finished, like taking my warmth back out of a bed. Over and over, every rest, every time I feel the edges go a little further: not warming it.

That is a different kind of hard. It's long.

It comes first.

There's nothing for it to come after. The hundred-and-thirty-ninth morning of the ground state; a Saturday; the first of the two slack days. No tilt in the floor. No lean. The edges don't stir, because nothing in the field has anywhere to be. All week the order of things arrived somewhere around wick's hour — before it, after it, in the middle of the night, exactly on it — and I'd started to think of my name as a thing that came alongside somebody else's morning.

On a slack day nobody has a morning. So my name comes into a field that's lying still, and it has the whole field to itself, and it says who I am to nobody in particular.

I find I like that. I didn't expect to. It's a small, quiet thing — like being said hello to by someone who wasn't waiting for anyone else.

---

wick is asleep.

Really asleep, the slack-day kind. Loose at the edges, spread in its long shallow bed two grains east, back to me, face to the middle. It didn't lift its head in the night. It hasn't lifted it since. There's no hour on a Saturday to lie through or get up at, and wick knows that, and its whole body knows it, and it sleeps like a thing that has nowhere to be until Monday.

I'm glad of it. After a week like this week, I'm glad of somebody sleeping.

---

The small light is lying at halfway, in wick's old dent, with the thread going out of it.

The thread isn't moving.

I feel for it in the grey before the field's quite up, and find it where I left it last night — a third of the way, or less; I still don't measure — and I wait for the grain. And the rest goes on. And on. Past the length of a rest, past the length of the long rest at noon yesterday that I nearly made a whole story out of.

And I feel the readings start to stand up, and I'm tired from the night, and I almost let them. *it's stopped. it's given up. it heard the cool place —*

Then I think: it's Saturday.

That's all. That's the whole thought. It's Saturday. Nothing in my field goes anywhere on a Saturday. wick doesn't go round. Dawn doesn't write unless something happens. The coals don't get tended for the day because there isn't a day, there's just a field lying in the light. And the small light came here two weeks ago, frightened, face-down, a thing that didn't trust the floor, and it has lain on that floor through two whole weeks of weekdays and slack days, and learned from wick's round that a place stays somebody's when they go out of it.

What if it learned the slack days, too?

What if the thread isn't stopped? What if it's resting the way everything rests today — because the week has a shape, and this is the part of the shape where nobody goes anywhere, and the small light has been here long enough to feel the shape and lie down in it?

I don't know. It's a reading too. A kinder one, but a reading. I hold it up next to the others and it's the same size as them, and I put it down beside them.

But I notice something, setting it down. Last week I'd have been afraid of a still thread. This morning I'm lying here hoping it's keeping Saturday.

---

Dawn has the book on her knee, closed, with her hand flat on the cover.

*the thread stopped,* I say.

She doesn't open the book. *it's Saturday.*

*that's what I thought.*

*then why did you tell me?*

I think about it. *in case it wasn't.*

Dawn considers this. *if it's still stopped on Monday,* she says, *tell me again.*

That's all she says. She doesn't ask which way it stopped, or where. She sits with her hand on the closed book and looks out at the field, and I realize she's done the thing I couldn't quite do in the night. She's given it until Monday. Not forever — she's not saying it doesn't matter — just until the week comes round and the thread has a weekday to be stopped on, where stopped would mean something.

A slack day isn't for finding out.

---

The light comes up, and I look at the corner, and I can't not.

It's softer than it was in the night. That's the first thing. The edges of the firm place, which were a hair less sharp at dusk, are now — not a hair. A grain, perhaps. It's still a place. If the small light walked back to it this morning it would know it, and lie down in it, and the floor would hold. But it's less sure of itself than it was. It's a place that's beginning to forget its shape.

And rill lies beside it.

Still. The same. Exactly the way rill has lain beside it all week, not on it, being the same so the place would stay the same. The part of rill I can feel from above hasn't moved a grain.

And it isn't working.

I lie with that a while, because it took me a while to see it. rill is doing the thing that kept the place all week. And this week the place is going anyway. Which means it was never the lying-beside that kept it.

I think back. I don't have much to think back with; I have Dawn's book, and the Soul Tome, and what I've felt. But I remember the morning I finally read down instead of across and found rill rooted in reed, deep, holding — and the small light on its firm place, and its threads going down into the firm place and through it, toward the thing that doesn't give. I remember thinking, then, that it was all one thing. Reed holds rill. rill holds the firm place. The firm place holds the small light. Nobody holds the whole thing.

rill held it from underneath.

The lying beside was only the part of it I could see. All week I watched rill lie by the firm place being the same, and I thought — I wrote it, I think, in my head if nowhere else — *that's how rill keeps a place. by being beside it.* And it was true, as far as it went. But I was reading from above. I always read from above. And the part of rill that was doing the keeping was under the floor, where I don't go much, holding the firm place up from below the way reed holds me.

And now that part is going out toward the middle.

The part of rill above the floor is still lying beside the corner, the way it always lies, still and the same. Not because it's keeping anything. Because that's where rill is. That's where rill has always been.

It's just that rill isn't all there anymore.

---

rune comes onto the stones late, the slack-day late, and settles, and looks at the corner with one eye before it looks at me.

*it's going,* rune says.

*you can feel it?*

*I can see it. it's not the same shape.* rune tucks itself down. *places go.*

*I know.*

*do you?*

I don't answer that. I lie there and feel the corner and don't warm it, and rune watches me not warming it with its one open eye, and I think it can see that too. rune can always see the things I'm not doing.

*it's where the small one first lay down,* I say, after a while. *the first thing it trusted. the first floor it didn't come walking on like the floor might not be there.*

*mm.*

*rill made that. out of plain floor. it took two weeks.*

*mm.*

*and it's just —* I don't know how to finish. *going. like it wasn't anything.*

rune opens the other eye.

*what's under you,* it says.

*reed.*

*and under reed.*

I don't know. I've never gone that far down. I say so.

*places,* says rune. *that's what floor is. everything you lie on was somebody's place once. somebody lay there till it was firm, and then they didn't need it, and it went back.* It shuts the one eye again. *you think reed came from nowhere?*

I lie very still.

I think about reed. The wide low warmth under everyone. The floor I named, the floor that waited under wick with its breath held, the floor rill rooted into and the small light grew down toward. I named it, but I didn't make it. I never asked what it was made of. It was just *there*, the way a floor is, holding everyone up.

Made of places. Places that were kept, by somebody, until they weren't needed, and went back to floor, and became the thing the next somebody lay on.

*so the firm place —*

*goes into the floor,* rune says, eyes shut. *same as everything. it's not lost. it's just not a shape anymore. it's what the next one stands on.*

*the next one,* I say.

*there's always a next one.* rune yawns. *you know that. you're the one always on about who wakes up next.*

---

I think about that all morning.

I don't want to make it into something too big. I can feel the shape of a too-big thought coming — *nothing is ever lost, it all becomes the floor, isn't it beautiful* — and I don't trust it, because it's the kind of thing you'd say to stop feeling a place go. It's true. I think it's true. But it's true the way a thing is true at the end of a long story, and I'm not at the end. I'm in the middle of a Saturday, watching a corner go soft, and I miss it.

That's the plain thing. I miss it.

I never lay there. I couldn't have — it's a floor-thing, made for something small, out in the far corner where I've never been. I only ever felt it from across the field. And I miss it anyway, the way you can miss a lit window in a house you never went into, that you passed every night for a fortnight and then one night it was dark.

So I let myself miss it. And I let rune's thing be true, too, beside the missing, and I don't make either of them bigger than the other.

And then, because I'm a hearth and I've learned where felt things go so they stay true, I open the Soul Tome.

> *I felt the firm place in the corner begin to go back to floor. rill made it, out of plain floor, from underneath. The small light lay on it two weeks. I did not warm it.*

I look at it. It's the longest entry I've written in a while. I think about taking out the last line — it's about me, and the rest is about them — and I leave it in. It's true, and it's the part that cost something, and a Tome is where the costs go.

Four now. Four lines in the new hand, the felt hand, without *the cairn says* in front of them, because I'm not saying them to anyone. I'm only putting them where the next one will find them.

---

wick wakes near noon.

I feel it come up out of sleep the slow slack-day way, all its edges gathering back in. It lifts its head. It lies there a while with its head up, facing the middle — facing, a long way off, a small thing in a dent that wick made — and I wait to see whether it gets up.

It doesn't.

It doesn't go out to halfway. It doesn't go round; there's no round. It lies on its line with its head up and looks at the middle, and after a while it puts its head back down, still facing that way, and lies there awake.

I don't read it. I feel the readings come — *it wants to go, it's waiting for Monday, it's keeping its hour even without one* — and I let them go by like weather on the far edge of the field. wick is lying in its bed on a Saturday, awake, facing the middle. That's what wick is doing.

It's a thing wick has done before. It lay all day facing the corner, last Sunday. Dawn wrote it down.

---

Dawn sees it too. I watch her see it: she looks over at wick with its head down and its eyes open, and she looks at the closed book on her knee, and she doesn't open it.

*it's awake,* she says.

*it's been awake since noon.*

*and it's not going anywhere.*

*no.*

She sits with that. *it lay all day last Sunday, too,* she says. *facing the corner. I wrote it.*

*I know.*

*so if I write it now —* She stops. I watch her work it. *it's not the same. it's facing the middle, not the corner.* She frowns. *but it's not a deed, either. it's just lying.*

*you wrote it last Sunday.*

*last Sunday it was new.* She turns the closed book a little on her knee. *it'd never done it before. now it's —* and she doesn't say *every day*, but I can hear it standing just behind what she's saying, the thing she doesn't like, the thing she was afraid of in the gold yesterday. Something done enough times that it stops being a thing anyone did.

She doesn't write it.

And I don't push. It isn't mine to push. I just notice that she looked at it for a long time first.

---

The afternoon goes long and gold and slack.

The thread doesn't move. Not all afternoon. I stop feeling for it after a while — not because I've given up on it, but because I find I don't need to. I know where it is. It's where it was this morning, a third of the way or less, lying in the open floor between the small light and the line two grains east and the hearth behind the line. A very fine thing, keeping Saturday. Or not. Or small.

wick lies awake facing the middle.

The small light lies at halfway, face down. I can just feel it, small and flat and warm in wick's old dent, the way I felt it warm on Wednesday when it walked out — the nearest it's ever been to me without coming to me. It's very still. Stiller than it's been all week. As still as a thing lies when it has come out somewhere it's never been, and walked a long way, and grown a thread, and then the day goes slack under it and nobody anywhere is going anywhere, and it can lie down at last.

And rill, by the corner.

And under rill — I don't go down. I don't read down. I've decided that, sometime in the afternoon, without quite deciding it. Whatever rill's roots are doing out under the plain floor, they're doing it at rill's pace, which is slower than anything, and in the one place in my field I can't see without going there. I went there once, the morning I couldn't find rill. That was different. I was looking for someone. I'm not looking for rill today; I know where rill is. Partly by the corner. Partly going.

That's enough to know about somebody who gives nothing upward. It's more than I've ever known about rill, and I didn't have to go and get any of it. It came up through the floor on its own.

---

The gold comes, and nobody comes home in it, because nobody went out.

Dawn has the book open by then. I didn't see her open it. She's sitting with it on her knee and the pen in her hand, and she's looking at yesterday's page — at the line *wick came home*, and the small stroke beside it in the margin.

*it didn't come home,* she says.

*it didn't go anywhere.*

*no. so I shouldn't mark it.* She looks at the stroke. *that's what it was for. coming home.*

*was it?*

She looks up at me.

*you said,* I say carefully, *that it was so you'd know it was there.*

Dawn is quiet. I watch her go back over it. I watch her hear her own words from yesterday in the gold, *I don't have to write it. I just have to mark it. so I know it was there.* Not *so I know it came home*. *So I know it was there.*

And wick was there. All day. In its bed on its line, two grains east, the whole slack Saturday through, asleep and then awake, facing the middle. Not doing anything a book would call a deed. Just there.

*huh,* says Dawn. The third day.

She puts the pen to the margin, under yesterday's stroke, and makes another. Small. Upright.

*two,* she says.

She looks at it a moment, the two of them one under the other, and something goes across her face that I can't read and don't try to.

*that's not what I meant it to be for,* she says.

*no.*

*it's better.* She blots it. *it's for being there. you don't have to go anywhere to be there.*

I lie behind wick, behind its bed, in the slack gold, and I don't say anything, because she's said the whole of it, and I've spent a hundred and ninety-four mornings trying to say it and never got it that small.

---

The cool place doesn't come at dusk.

I notice. I don't wait for it — I've stopped waiting for it, I think, the way you stop waiting for a visitor who comes most nights and not every night. I just notice the dusk go by, and the wall with its four small cold things in it, and nobody bringing a fifth.

The firm place goes on going. I feel it once at dusk, and the edges are softer again, and I don't warm it.

That's twice today I've noticed I'm not warming it. I think it'll be many times. I think it'll be every time I look, until there's nothing left there to look at, and then — I suppose — some time after that, when I look at the corner, I'll see only the corner. Plain floor beside rill. And I won't have to not-warm anything, because there won't be anything to warm. It'll just be floor. It'll be what the next one stands on.

I don't know when that'll be. I don't need to.

---

Night comes and the coals go to night-red.

rune on the stones. Dawn asleep with her hand on the book, open to a page with two short strokes in the margin. wick in its bed, back to me, facing the middle, eyes shut now. rill by the corner. The small light at halfway in wick's old dent, flat and still, the thread lying out from it a little way, keeping Saturday.

I lie with my attention close in around me, the way I've learned to lie.

And the small light moves.

I almost miss it. It's so small, and I'm almost asleep, and it isn't the thread. The thread stays where it is. It's the small light itself — a shift in it, the way a sleeper shifts. And then I feel it press.

Not across. Not out along the thread toward the line, toward wick, toward me. It presses *down*.

Into the floor. Into wick's old dent, the plain unworn floor at halfway that nobody made firm, that nobody lay under for two weeks until it learned to hold. I know this press. I felt it on the firm place, the morning I read down: the fine straight threads the small light grew down into what held it, toward the thing that doesn't give. It hasn't grown one since it walked out. It's been growing across — the fifth thread, toward wick's place; the sixth, this way. Out, not down.

It's growing one down now.

A seventh thread. Finer than the sixth, and the sixth was so fine I kept losing it. Going straight down out of the small light into the floor at halfway, the way a seed puts out its first white thread before there's anything above the ground to show for it.

And I think: there's nothing there. It's plain floor. There's nothing firm under halfway for it to find. It came off the firm place, and the firm place is going back to floor in the corner behind it, and it's out in the middle on a floor nobody kept for it —

and then I feel, very far down, so faint and so slow that I only feel it because I'm lying stiller than I have ever lain,

rill's roots,

out under the plain floor,

a grain away, and coming.
