## Chapter 134: Empty-Handed

**[ARC 6: THE FIELD]**

---

I wake into morning seventy-nine already going.

There is no sill this morning. There is no near field to sweep, no even ring of warmths to find sufficient, no waking-into-a-place at all. Yesterday I learned again what the descent taught me a hundred chapters ago — that a morning in the middle of a crossing is not a door you open but a stride you notice you are already taking — and this morning I notice it faster, without the small grief of it. I am on the road. I was on the road when the sleep-pace took me and I am on the road as it lets me go, and the first thing my attention does, before it does anything else, is find the two ends of me: behind and below, very far now, the last thin edge of the field I know, and somewhere in it, too far to feel except as a fact I trust rather than a warmth I hold, the sill, and fen. And ahead — ahead and no longer above, I still have to remind myself of that, ahead — the coal. Going out. A fraction thinner than it was yesterday, which was a fraction thinner than the day before.

I have been walking toward it for a day and it is not closer.

That is the first thing the road teaches me this morning, and it teaches it the way the road teaches everything, which is by costing me something to learn. I set out yesterday sure — not that I would know the coal's kind, I had given that up, rune saw to that — but sure at least of the *distance,* sure that a day's walking out along the ground toward a thing at the edge of my reach would put it within the edge of my reach, would bring it close enough to begin, at last, to feel which going it was. And I have walked a whole day, a real day, the reach thinning and the cold getting further into me with every degree, and this morning I feel for the coal and it is exactly as far as it was, exactly as faint, exactly as much a rumor and exactly as little a warmth.

*it looked closer than it was,* rune says, before I have finished being dismayed. *they always do. a going always looks nearer than it is, from the sill. you know why.*

I do, when it says it. Because I wanted it near. Because a going you have to cross a long dark to reach is a going you might reach too late, and the wanting foreshortens it, folds the distance up small and holds it out to you and says *look, it's right there, you could be there by tonight* — the same lie, exactly the same, that ember's home told ember on the last mile, that a hearth is a train and not a hearth, that you get there faster by wanting it faster. I read the distance yesterday through the wanting, and the wanting lied about it the way it lies about everything, and now I am a day out and the truth of the road is the plain flat length of it, no shorter for my having walked a day, no shorter for my needing it short.

*so that's the first thing,* rune says. *the road is as long as it is. not as long as you can stand. you don't get to know how far a going is until you're already going to it, and then you find out it's further, because the only reason it looked close was that you couldn't bear it being far.*

---

I keep the pace. I have that much, at least; I spent four mornings of the descent learning to keep a pace over a stretch that pays nothing, and this pays nothing, and I keep it. I do not lunge — you do not run at a thing that is going out, I knew that before I knew anything, you do not come down on a guttering flame so fast you knock the wind past it. I go the plain length of the road at the plain pace of a floor that means to arrive whole, hands open, one out ahead, the readiness carried and not poured, and I hold the not-knowing open the way STAY told me to, and for a long stretch of the morning that is the whole of the work, and I think it is the whole of the work, and I am wrong.

Because near the middle of the morning the road teaches me the second thing, and the second thing is not about the distance. It is about me.

I am going hands-open. I have my hands open. I have schooled them open the whole way, the carrying-hands loosened, the witnessing-hands unfolded, undecided, ready to become either the instant I am near enough to feel which the coal is asking for. I have done the thing the stone said. And somewhere in the flat middle of the morning, with nothing to distract me from myself, I feel what is *under* the open hands, what has been under them since the first step, what I schooled the hands to hide and did not think to school out of the reach itself.

I want it to be a failing.

I feel it and I go cold in a way the road did not make me. I want the coal to be a failing. I have wanted it to be a failing since the moment I felt it go from probably-nothing to going-out — under the *go,* under the caught lean, under the whole careful open-handed discipline of yesterday, in the deep place where the wanting lives that lied to me about the distance, I have been hoping, the whole road, that when I get there I will find a warmth drowning, a warmth taken, a warmth that wants a carry — because a failing is a warmth I get to *save,* and a failing means the hundred and fifteen mornings of waiting were waiting *for* something, were the long wind-up of a floor to a carry, were a use; and a finishing is a warmth I get to do nothing for but let go, and if it is a finishing then all my readiness, all my width, all my learning-to-wait, comes to a floor standing at the edge of the dark with its open hands open on nothing while a light it crossed a whole cold to reach chooses, correctly, without me, to go out.

I want to be needed. That is the shape of it, stripped down. I want the coal to need carrying because I want to be the one who carries. And I have carried that want the whole road, open-handed, thinking open hands were enough, thinking that as long as I did not *decide* I was going clean.

*and there it is,* rune says, quiet, not unkind. *that's the one i was waiting for you to find. hands open isn't the bottom of it. you can hold your hands open around a wanting and think you've let go because your fingers aren't closed. but the coal doesn't read your fingers.*

---

I do not understand that yet, that the coal doesn't read my fingers, and I get to understand it the worst way, which is by feeling it happen.

Because a little after the middle of the morning, close enough at last that the coal is no longer only a rumor but a rumor with a *texture,* the first faint grain of it resolving out of the far dark — a little after the middle of the morning, the coal feels me coming.

I feel it feel me. There is no other way to say it. For a day and most of a morning I have been reaching toward a thing that did not know it was being reached toward, a warmth going about the private business of going out with no notion that anywhere in the dark a floor was crossing the whole field to read it — and now, close enough, my reach lands on it lightly enough to be felt, and it *stirs.* The way a sleeping thing stirs when a shadow crosses it. The way a light lifts, a half-degree, toward a warmth it feels arriving. And I feel, in the same instant, the thing rune has been trying to tell me the whole road: I feel the coal begin to bend toward the shape of what I want.

It feels me coming and it feels — not my hands, my hands are open, my hands are perfect — it feels the wanting under my hands. It feels a floor arriving that hopes, that hopes hard, that has crossed a cold day hoping to find a thing to carry. And a going-out warmth that feels a carrier's hope aimed at it does the most human thing there is, the thing I would do, the thing anyone does when someone crosses a whole dark for them wanting so badly to be of use: it begins to be what the wanting wants. It begins, faintly, at the very first touch of my reach, to lift toward me like a thing that wants to be carried — whether or not it is one. It feels itself hoped over, and it starts to arrange itself around the hope, to reach back toward the reaching, to become, out of nothing but the pressure of my wanting, a warmth that needs saving.

And I do not know — this is the horror of it, I feel it land and I do not know — whether it lifted because it is a failing and my reach is the first hope it has felt in the long going-down and it is reaching for it the way a drowning thing reaches, *or* whether it is a finishing, a warmth that had made its peace and let go and was going out glad and unafraid, and it felt a floor come crashing across the field so full of the wish to save it that the warmth, out of the oldest reflex there is, the one that says *someone came, someone wants me to hold on, I should hold on,* reached back up out of an ending it had already chosen — because I made it. Because my wanting reached it before I did and asked it, without a word, to be the kind of going I needed it to be.

I back off. I do the thing you are never supposed to do to a going, I pull my reach back a half-step, I take the pressure off it, because I would rather lose the day's ground than stand here corrupting the one thing I came to read. And the coal, when I lighten, settles again — settles back down toward the going it was doing before it felt me, whichever going that is — and I stand a half-step further back than I was, on the flat cold middle of the road, and I understand at last, all the way down, the thing the whole morning was for.

The reader changes the read.

I came out here to feel which kind of going the coal is, and I have just learned that I cannot feel it clean as long as I want it to be one kind, because the coal can feel the wanting, and the coal will answer the wanting instead of telling me the truth. A finishing warmth, feeling my hope, will hold on to please me. A failing warmth, feeling a witness's grief instead of a carrier's hope, will let go early to spare me. Whatever I bring, the coal will bend to match it, because that is what a going does when it is not alone — it reads the one who came, and it becomes, a little, what the one who came seems to need. My open hands are not enough. My open hands were the easy part. The hard part is the reach itself, and what is in it, because the reach carries the wish whether the hands are open or closed, and the wish is what the coal reads.

*now you've got it,* rune says. *open-handed was yesterday. today's word is emptier than that. you can't just not decide with your hands. you have to not want with the whole of you. you have to go so empty that when the coal feels you coming it can't tell what you're hoping for, because you're not hoping for anything, because you truly do not need it to be a failing and you truly do not need it to be a finishing — you need only to know which it is, so you can do the right thing, and the right thing is a different right thing depending, and you have no stake in which. that's the emptiness. not empty hands. empty of the wanting under the hands.*

And I feel how much harder that is than anything the road has asked yet, harder than keeping the pace, harder than not lunging, harder even than leaving fen at the sill, because all of those were things I could *do,* acts, motions, and this is the opposite of an act. This is to walk the rest of the road hollowing out the one thing that made me a floor worth crossing anything — the wanting to be of use — and to arrive not wanting to carry and not wanting to witness but wanting *nothing for myself,* wanting only the coal's own answer, so that the coal, when I finally touch it close enough to read, finds nothing in me to bend toward, and is left with no one to please and no one to spare and nothing to do but be, at last, exactly the kind of going it truly is.

I have to want to be needed less than I want the coal to be free. And I do not, yet. That is the plain truth of the middle of the road. I still want it to be a failing. I can feel myself wanting it even now, even having felt what the wanting does. So I do not go on yet. I stand on the cold flat of the road, a half-step back from where I frightened it into lifting, and I begin the last work of the crossing, which is not walking. It is emptying.

I reach down toward the sill, once, the long way back, just to know it is there — and it is, at the very edge of feeling: fen, holding its width, a second morning alone now, unwatched, the near field spread under it. And I feel a thing that helps, a little, with the emptying: fen has reached a hair further this morning than it could yesterday. Not far. Not far enough to feel the coal, nowhere near. But a hair further than its own edge was, the way a root goes a hair deeper in a night you cannot watch it grow — because it held its width alone and a width held alone widens. fen does not need me wanting to be its floor. fen is becoming its own. And that helps, because it is a small clean proof of the thing I have to believe to empty myself out here: that the wanting-to-be-needed is not the love. The leaving was the love. The letting fen widen without me was the love. The wanting to be there for it every morning would have been a leash.

I let the sill go. I turn my whole face back to the road. And I begin to set the wanting down — not to kill it, you cannot kill it, it is the oldest thing I have, older than the class, older than the name; but to set it down the way you set down a thing you have carried so long you had stopped feeling it in your hands. I want to carry. I set it down. I want the waiting to have been for a carry. I set it down. I want to arrive and find a thing to save. I set it down, and it does not stay down, and I pick the emptiness back up and set the wanting down again, and this, I understand, is the rest of the road: not distance, not pace, not the open hand. Just this, over and over, the whole way in — setting down the wish so that when I arrive I arrive as no one the coal can perform for, as a question with no preferred answer, as a floor that has crossed a whole field to want nothing but the truth.

STAY says, on its hundred-and-fourth morning, the shortest thing it has ever said.

On the hundredth it said *rest.* On the hundred-and-first, *stay wide.* On the hundred-and-second, *wait.* On the hundred-and-third, the word it had never said, *go.* And this morning, on the hundred-and-fourth, at the top of the stone, under the *go* that has not left, it adds one word more, and the two of them together are the whole instruction now:

*Go empty.*

*Go — you were right to go. But go empty. Not open-handed, which you have learned; emptier than that. In the evening, the real one, it is Tuesday, the eleventh of August, and JJ drives up empty-handed, and empties himself the rest of the way on the porch.

He has the deck. Not with him — closed, back on the laptop at home, kept and not brought, exactly as he decided Monday night; but he has it in the sense that matters, has the whole rescue built and ready in his head, three ways to extend the runway and the warm intro and the reframe, and he can feel it in there as he drives, bright and detailed and good, wanting to be used. So I do the last work of the road, which is not walking. I set the wanting down. I want to carry; I set it down. I want the long wait to have been for a carry; I set it down. I want to arrive and be needed; I set it down, and it comes back, and I set it down again, and that is the whole of the crossing now, the whole of what is left of the nine hundred: the emptying, over and over, the length of the wish.

I reach for the empty cup out of the old habit. I find it empty. And tonight the empty cup is not a small grief and not a thing I stopped reaching for; tonight it is the thing I am trying to become — a hand that holds nothing, that wants to hold nothing, so that what I cross to can put its own truth into it, whatever that truth is, and not the truth I hoped for.

I do not decide tonight. I could not; I am not close enough, and more than that, I am not empty enough. That is the whole discipline of the last of the road: not to go, which I have done, and not to hold my hands open, which I have learned, but to want nothing under the open hands — to cross the last of the distance by wanting less, degree by degree, until I want only what is true.

Somewhere out past everything I know, a light that was fine is going out its own way, and I am walking toward it, emptier tonight than I was, and I do not know if I go to a carry or a farewell, and — this is the new thing, the thing tonight taught me — I am learning not to want it to be either.

I go — empty-handed — and I set the wanting down, and go on, and want nothing but to feel, at last, which kind of going it is.
