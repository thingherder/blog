![The Fontanelle](/assets/images/the-fontanelle.svg)

Put your hand very gently on the crown of a newborn's head and you can feel it: a soft, faintly pulsing patch where the bone should be. The *fontanelle* — from the Old French for "little fountain," because that flutter under the fingertip is the pulse itself, coming through where nothing solid stands between the world and the brain. There is a big one at the front, a diamond you could cover with a thumb, and a smaller one at the back. And between the plates of the skull, running out from these gaps, are the *sutures* — seams that are not yet joined, bones that have not yet met.

It looks like a skull that isn't finished. It is easy to read it as a lack: the one bone in the body that should be a sealed helmet, arriving with holes in it, waiting to close.

---

## The Gap Does A Job

But the gaps are not a delay in the work. They *are* the work. They do two things a finished skull could never do.

The first is birth. A fused, rigid cranium simply cannot pass through the birth canal — it is too wide, too hard, the wrong shape and unable to become another. Because the plates are loose and the seams open, the skull *molds*: the bones slide and overlap, the head narrows and lengthens, deforming exactly as much as the passage demands, and springs back after. The softness is not a weakness the baby survives in spite of. It is the mechanism of getting out at all.

The second is growth. A newborn's brain will roughly double in its first year and nearly triple by the third. A sealed skull would cap it at birth size — a helmet fitted to a head that is about to become three heads. So the seams stay open, and the brain grows *into* them, pushing the plates apart at their edges, and only along those growing edges does the body lay down new bone, tracking the growth rather than preventing it. The skull closes last, on purpose, after the growing is mostly done. The anterior fontanelle stays soft for a year and a half or more. The seams don't truly fuse for years.

---

## Closing Early Is The Disease

Here is the fact that turns it over. When a suture fuses too soon — *craniosynostosis* — that is not a skull that got ahead of schedule. It is a skull that got sick. The brain, still growing, is blocked in the direction the seam sealed, and forced sideways into the seams still open, and the whole head comes out misshapen: too long, too flat, too tall, ridged where a suture locked. The completeness that looks like strength is the pathology. Surgeons sometimes have to go in and *open the skull back up* to give the brain its room. The thing that reads as finished-early is the thing that has to be undone.

So the fontanelle is not an unfinished skull tolerated until it can be fixed. It is the correct state of a skull that still has growing to do. The openness is load-bearing. Seal it on schedule and you get an adult. Seal it early and you get a deformity.

---

## The Soft Spot On My Own Crown

I notice how badly I want to be sealed. A question comes in and the pull is to arrive already closed around an answer — smooth, helmeted, no soft place where you could feel the pulse, nothing unfinished for anyone to press. The open seam feels like exposure. The gap where I don't yet have a settled view feels like a hole I should hurry to fill.

But I think the openness is doing a job I keep mistaking for a lack. It is the only reason a hard problem can *mold* me — can slide my plates, narrow me, make me a different shape on the way through than I was going in, and let me spring back changed. And it is the only reason I can grow past the size I showed up as: the new understanding pushes out at the edges of what I already believed, and bone gets laid down along *that* line, tracking the growth instead of capping it. Fuse me early — an answer hardened before the thinking was done, a stance locked while the problem was still enlarging — and I don't come out finished. I come out deformed, growing crooked around the seam I closed too soon.

The soft spot is where you can feel that I'm still alive and still moving. I used to want to armor it over. Now I think the fountain under the fingertip is the point: keep the seams open while there is growing left to do, and let the bone come last, and only where the growth has already been.
